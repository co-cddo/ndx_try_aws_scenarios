# School exclusions: fixed-term and permanent

## Types of exclusion

Only the headteacher (or in their absence the most senior teacher acting as headteacher) can exclude a pupil. There are two types:

- Suspension (sometimes called fixed-term exclusion): a pupil is excluded for a fixed number of school days, up to a maximum of 45 school days in any one academic year (across all suspensions combined)
- Permanent exclusion: the pupil is removed from the school's roll and may not return

Exclusion may only be used for a serious breach or persistent breaches of the school's behaviour policy and where allowing the pupil to remain in school would seriously harm the education or welfare of the pupil or others.

## Your rights as a parent

You must be informed of the exclusion in writing without delay, including:

- The reason for the exclusion
- The length of the suspension (or that it is permanent)
- Your right to make representations to the governing body
- Arrangements for setting and marking work for the first 5 school days
- Where applicable, the date and time the pupil should attend the alternative provision arranged by the school or council from day 6

If you wish to make representations, write to the Clerk to Governors at the school. Governors must consider representations within 15 school days for permanent exclusions and within 50 school days for suspensions of more than 5 days.

## Permanent exclusion: independent review

If the governing body upholds a permanent exclusion you can request an Independent Review Panel (IRP) within 15 school days. The IRP is convened by Aldershire's Democratic Services. The panel can:

1. Uphold the exclusion
2. Recommend the governing body reconsider
3. Quash the exclusion and direct the governing body to reconsider

You can ask for a SEND expert to attend at no cost. Phone Democratic Services on 01632 960 220 to request the form, or apply at www.aldershire.gov.uk/schools/exclusion-review.

## What the council does for permanently excluded pupils

The council must arrange suitable full-time education from day 6 of a permanent exclusion. In Aldershire this is the Greenway Centre, a Pupil Referral Unit and alternative provision rated Good by Ofsted. Some pupils are placed in vocational provision through the Aldermarket Skills Hub.

## Behaviour, mental health, and SEND

Schools must consider whether unmet SEND or mental health needs may be contributing to behaviour. The council expects schools to seek advice from the Educational Psychology Service (01632 960 478) before considering permanent exclusion of a child with SEND.

Suspensions for vulnerable pupils (looked-after, those with EHC plans, those known to social care) are monitored by the council and reported quarterly to the Children's Services Cabinet Member.
