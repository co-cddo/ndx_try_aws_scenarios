# Pavement obstructions

## What we mean by an obstruction

A pavement obstruction is anything that blocks, narrows, or makes hazardous the public footway. The Highways Act 1980 (sections 137 to 154) gives the council a duty to keep the highway clear and powers to remove obstructions. Common categories:

| Type | Examples |
| ---- | -------- |
| Commercial encroachment | A-boards, café tables, racks of stock |
| Vehicle encroachment | Cars parked on the pavement, trailers, skips |
| Vegetation | Overgrown hedges, low branches, weeds |
| Domestic | Bins left out, building materials |
| Street furniture clutter | Disused cabinets, sandwich-board signs |

The minimum effective pavement width Aldershire applies is 1.5 metres for routine pedestrian flow and 1.8 metres on busy footways. A wheelchair user with carer needs at least 1.5 metres to pass. Anything reducing the clear width below this triggers action.

## A-boards

Aldershire's A-board policy (revised 2024) requires:

- The board to be placed within 0.5 m of the building line
- A clear width of at least 1.5 metres between the board and any other obstruction (e.g. lamp column, tree pit)
- The board to be removed at end of business each day
- The board to bear the business name and contact (so the council can route any liability claim)

Boards that do not meet the policy are warned in writing; persistent breaches lead to seizure under section 149 of the Highways Act. Seized boards can be reclaimed for a fee within 28 days.

## Vehicles parked on the pavement

Aldershire is consulting on a borough-wide pavement parking ban under the Traffic Management Act 2004 (in line with national consultations from 2023). Until the ban is in force:

- Where pavement parking obstructs passage (for example forces a wheelchair user into the road), it is dealt with as obstruction by the police on 101 (a criminal-law matter under the Highway Code rule 244 in London, and by obstruction case-law elsewhere)
- Where the vehicle is also breaching parking restrictions (e.g. on yellow lines), Civil Enforcement Officers can issue a PCN
- Persistent offenders are reported to the police and the registered keeper

To report obstructive pavement parking that is not in a CPZ, phone the police 101 line. For pavement parking in a CPZ, report to the Council on 01632 960 570.

## Hedges and vegetation

A hedge growing out from private property over the public footway is the responsibility of the landowner. Section 154 of the Highways Act 1980 lets the council require cutting back. We send a notice giving 14 days; if not complied with, we cut and recharge.

Report at www.aldershire.gov.uk/highways/hedge or phone 01632 960 615.

## Bins on the pavement

Wheeled bins should be returned to the property by the end of the collection day, in line with bin policy. Bins left long-term on a footway are an obstruction. The Waste Team works with residents first; persistent cases are referred to the Highways enforcement officer.
