# Public footpaths and rights of way

## The Definitive Map

Aldershire holds the Definitive Map and Statement under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981. This is the legal record of public rights of way in the borough and includes:

- Footpaths (pedestrian only)
- Bridleways (pedestrians, horse riders, pedal cyclists)
- Restricted byways (non-mechanically-propelled use)
- Byways open to all traffic (BOATs) - rare in Aldershire (4 routes, total 2.1 km)

The current Definitive Map is published at www.aldershire.gov.uk/rightsofway/definitivemap. Updates (modification orders) are advertised in the local press and on noticeboards in affected parishes.

In total Aldershire has 612 km of public rights of way.

## Reporting an obstruction

Common obstructions:

- Overgrown vegetation reducing the path width
- Locked gates or new fencing across a path
- Crops planted across a path (must be reinstated within 14 days of cultivation)
- Aggressive livestock (separate, urgent process)
- Surfacing failures (potholes, missing footbridges)

Report at www.aldershire.gov.uk/rightsofway/report or phone the Rights of Way Team on 01632 960 605. We aim to inspect within:

- 2 working days for safety obstructions (collapsed bridge, dangerous animal, locked gate)
- 10 working days for vegetation, surface defects
- 28 working days for boundary issues, signage

## Maintenance responsibilities

Aldershire is responsible for:

- The surface of the path (where adopted)
- Footbridges across watercourses
- Stiles and gates that pre-existed the Definitive Map and are maintained at council cost
- Way-marking (the yellow, blue, purple, and red discs)

The landowner is responsible for:

- Boundary features (hedges, walls)
- Stiles and gates installed by the landowner (with a 25 percent grant available from the council towards replacement)
- Removing obstructions caused by their activity
- Reinstating cropped paths within 14 days of cultivation

## Definitive Map Modification Orders

Anyone can apply to add, delete, or change the recorded status of a path. Submit a Definitive Map Modification Order (DMMO) application at www.aldershire.gov.uk/rightsofway/dmmo. Applications must include user evidence (typically 20 years' use as of right) or documentary evidence (historical maps, deeds, conveyances).

Aldershire processes DMMOs in date order; current backlog is around 18 months from application to determination, due to the volume and complexity of historic evidence.

## Public Path Orders

For temporary closures (e.g. for works) we make Public Path Orders under section 14 of the RTRA 1984 (same power as for road closures); for permanent diversions we use orders under section 119 of the Highways Act 1980. Permanent diversions require formal consultation and are confirmed by the Secretary of State or by the council where unopposed.
