# Skip permits

## When you need a permit

A skip placed on the public highway needs a Skip Licence under section 139 of the Highways Act 1980. This includes:

- Skips on the carriageway (road)
- Skips on the footway (pavement, where permitted)
- Skips overhanging the highway from a private drive

A skip placed wholly within your private property does not need a council licence, but if the skip will be loaded by a vehicle parked on the highway you may need a parking suspension; check with the council before booking.

## How to apply

Most residents do not apply directly; the skip hire company applies on your behalf. Aldershire-licensed skip companies are listed at www.aldershire.gov.uk/highways/skip-companies. If you use an unlicensed company you may both be in breach.

Applications come in via www.aldershire.gov.uk/highways/skip-licence. The standard processing time is 3 working days. Same-day permits are available for an additional fee but are not guaranteed; weekends and bank holidays add 1 to 2 working days.

## Conditions of the permit

The skip:

- Must be marked with the operator's name and 24-hour contact number
- Must carry yellow / red reflective markings (under the Builders' Skips (Markings) Regulations 1984)
- Must be lit at night with two red lamps if the skip is on the carriageway
- Must not exceed 5.5 metres in length or be sited within 15 metres of a junction without express permission
- Must not block a fire hydrant, manhole, or stopcock
- Must not be placed on grass verges, footways less than 1.8 metres wide (without alternative pedestrian provision), or in residents-only parking bays without prior approval
- Must not contain hazardous waste

Permit-holders are responsible for the safety of the skip and any liabilities arising. The council may direct removal at the operator's cost if conditions are breached.

## Liaison with Planning

If you are using a skip as part of a development that has planning conditions about hours of working or construction access, the Skip Licence is granted independently of the planning condition. You are still bound by your planning condition. Major-scheme projects will usually have a Construction Management Plan covering skip placement; the skip permit must align with that plan.
