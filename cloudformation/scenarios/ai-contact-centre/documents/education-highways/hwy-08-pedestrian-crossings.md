# Pedestrian crossing requests

## How we decide where to put crossings

Aldershire uses the national PV2 framework (pedestrian flow squared, multiplied by vehicle flow) along with a wider set of factors to assess where a new pedestrian crossing is justified. PV2 indicates relative demand; modern guidance also looks at:

- Vulnerable groups using the route (children, older people, disabled people)
- Severance and detour to the nearest existing crossing
- Casualty history (in the last 5 years)
- Speed (85th percentile)
- Available width and visibility

We do not provide every crossing requested, because crossings can introduce risk where they are not warranted (drivers not expecting them, low usage leading to false expectation of priority).

## Request a crossing

You can ask for a crossing at www.aldershire.gov.uk/highways/crossing-request or by writing to the Traffic Engineering Team. We need:

- The exact location (a What3Words reference is ideal)
- Why you think a crossing is needed (e.g. school commute, hospital approach, retirement complex)
- Approximate counts of pedestrians and any difficulty crossing observed

A community petition (with at least 25 signatures from local residents) escalates a request to the next quarterly Local Area Traffic Committee.

## What we do with your request

The Traffic Engineering Team carries out a desktop assessment using existing pedestrian and traffic count data and the casualty database. If the case looks promising we commission a manual count (peak-hour) and a site visit. The full assessment takes 8 to 12 weeks.

If a crossing is justified, the scheme enters the Council's Local Transport Capital Programme. Funding is limited; the wait from approval to delivery can be 12 to 36 months depending on size and competing priorities. Schemes attached to a school or development typically attract section 106 or Community Infrastructure Levy funding and may move faster.

## Crossing types

| Type | Description |
| ---- | ----------- |
| Zebra | Black-and-white markings, belisha beacons, pedestrian priority. Best on lower-speed roads with high pedestrian flow |
| Pelican | Push-button, separate phases for pedestrians and vehicles. Mostly being phased out in favour of puffins |
| Puffin | Push-button, with on-crossing detectors that hold the pedestrian green only as long as needed |
| Toucan | Shared with cyclists; wider crossing, used on cycle routes |
| Pegasus | Higher-set button for horse riders (rare in Aldershire; one at Vale Riding Centre) |

## School crossings
