# Speed limit changes: community petition route

## How speed limits are set

Speed limits in Aldershire are set under the Road Traffic Regulation Act 1984 by Traffic Regulation Order (TRO). Limits are based on the Department for Transport's Setting Local Speed Limits guidance (Circular 01/2013), which considers:

- The function of the road (strategic, distributor, residential)
- Existing 85th percentile speeds (the speed below which 85 percent of drivers travel)
- The mean speed of free-flowing traffic
- Casualty history
- Pedestrian, cyclist, and vulnerable road-user activity
- Frontage development and access density

A speed limit that is significantly lower than what feels natural to drive often has limited effect without supporting measures (engineering, enforcement, community engagement).

## Ask for a change

Residents, community groups, and parish councils can ask for a speed limit review. Two routes:

1. Local councillor request: write to your ward councillor, who can raise it with the Traffic Engineer at the next Local Area Traffic Committee.
2. Community petition: a petition signed by at least 25 residents living on or near the road triggers a formal review, regardless of councillor support. Submit through www.aldershire.gov.uk/petitions.

The review takes 12 to 16 weeks. We may collect a temporary 7-day speed survey using a mobile speed indicator device.

## 20 mph zones

Aldershire's 20 mph strategy (2024) sets a presumption of 20 mph on residential streets where:

- The road is mainly fronted by housing
- Existing mean speeds are at or below 24 mph (so the new limit is broadly self-enforcing)
- There is community support

Where existing mean speeds are higher we add traffic calming (speed cushions, pinch points, raised tables) before changing the limit. Bare-sign 20 mph (signs only, no engineering) on a road with mean speeds above 24 mph generally produces only a 1 to 2 mph reduction, in line with national evidence.

A 20 mph rollout has been completed across the Aldermarket town centre and is being phased into Vale villages (Greenwood, Highmere, and Lowfold went live in spring 2026; consultation on Brook Green and Crowfield closes in May).

## 60 mph national limits on rural lanes

Many rural lanes in the Aldershire Vale carry the national speed limit (60 mph for cars) by default, even where 60 mph would be unsafe to drive in practice. The Vale Lanes Initiative (2025) introduced 50 mph or 40 mph limits on 38 km of lanes where the geometry, sightlines, and casualty record warranted change. Suggestions for further lanes are welcome via the petition route.

## Enforcement

Speed limits are enforced by Aldershire Police (101) and not by the council. The council partners with Police on the Community SpeedWatch scheme; phone 01632 960 547 to volunteer.
