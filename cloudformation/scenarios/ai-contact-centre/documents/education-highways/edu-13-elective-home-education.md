# Elective home education

## Your right to home educate

Parents have a legal right under section 7 of the Education Act 1996 to educate their child at home rather than in a school. The education provided must be efficient, full-time, and suitable to the child's age, ability, aptitude, and any special educational needs.

You do not need permission to home educate from the start. If your child has never been on a school roll you can begin home educating without informing the council, although we encourage you to do so. If your child is currently registered at a school you must write to the headteacher to deregister; the school will then notify the council.

For children at a special school named in an EHC plan, you also need consent from the council to deregister.

## Telling the council

To tell us your child is home educated, complete the form at www.aldershire.gov.uk/schools/ehe or phone the EHE team on 01632 960 426. We will:

- Add your child to the EHE register (separate from the school admissions register)
- Send you a welcome pack with information on examinations, special educational needs, exam access arrangements, and local home education networks
- Offer (but not require) a visit or other communication from an EHE adviser

## EHE advisers and informal enquiries

Aldershire employs two part-time EHE advisers, both former teachers, who make annual informal enquiries about the education being provided. We follow the DfE's Elective Home Education guidance for local authorities (2019, as updated). The annual enquiry can be:

- A visit at home, at a community venue, or at the Town Hall
- A written report with samples of work
- An online video meeting
- A telephone call followed up in writing

You decide which format you prefer. Where the council has reason to believe a suitable education is not being provided, we will use the statutory school attendance order process under section 437 of the Education Act 1996. This is rare; we issued one school attendance order in 2024.

## Examinations

Home-educated young people take GCSEs and A-levels as private candidates. Aldershire Sixth Form College and Aldermarket Adult Learning Centre both accept private candidates for a fee; book by 31 January for summer entry. The council can advise on exam centres but does not pay fees.

## Returning to school

You can apply for a school place at any time using the in-year admissions process. The EHE adviser will help you transition.

## Support and networks

The Aldershire Home Education Network (community-run, not council-run) holds weekly meet-ups at the Vale Community Hall on Thursdays. Phone 01632 960 426 for contact details.
