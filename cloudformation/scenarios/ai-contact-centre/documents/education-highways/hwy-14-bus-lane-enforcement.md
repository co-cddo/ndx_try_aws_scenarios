# Bus lane enforcement

## Where the bus lanes are

Aldershire operates 18 km of bus lanes across the network, predominantly in Aldermarket and on the A52 Vale Bypass approach. Each lane is signed at the entry, at intermediate points, and (where ending into general traffic) at the exit.

A bus lane carries one of three time-of-operation conventions, which appear on the signs:

- 24 hours: in force at all times. Common on the Aldermarket Travel Quality Bus Corridor.
- Peak only: in force at specified weekday peak times (e.g. Mon to Fri 07:00 to 10:00 and 15:00 to 19:00).
- Termly: in force during school terms only (used near a small number of schools).

The signs also list who is permitted to use the lane: typically buses, taxis, motorcycles, and cycles. Some lanes admit only buses and cycles; check the signs.

## CCTV enforcement

Aldershire enforces bus lanes under the Transport Act 2000 and the Bus Lane Contraventions (Penalty Charges, Adjudication and Enforcement) Regulations 2005. Enforcement is by approved CCTV cameras at fixed locations and on selected mobile units mounted on buses (the latter introduced in 2023 across half the urban fleet).

Penalty Charge Notices for bus lane offences are issued by post to the registered keeper. The discount and challenge process mirrors parking PCNs (see PCN appeal guidance):

- 50 percent discount if paid within 14 days
- Standard rate to day 28
- Surcharge if a Notice to Owner is issued and unpaid

## What counts as a contravention

A contravention is driving any part of a vehicle in a bus lane during its hours of operation, when not in a permitted category.

Common edge cases:

- Crossing a bus lane to enter or leave a side road or driveway: permitted, provided you do not travel along the lane
- Stopping in a bus lane to drop off a passenger: not permitted
- Entering a bus lane to overtake a stationary vehicle blocking your lane: still a contravention; the lane is for the listed permitted users only
- Cycling in a "buses only, no cycles" lane: not permitted; check the sign

## Challenging a bus lane PCN

The grounds and process are the same as for parking PCNs. Common acceptances include:

- Emergency manoeuvre to avoid a collision (with supporting evidence)
- Clear signage failure (we will check the photographs)
- Vehicle was a permitted user (e.g. bona fide taxi)

We do not accept satnav-led routing as a defence; signs on the road take priority.
