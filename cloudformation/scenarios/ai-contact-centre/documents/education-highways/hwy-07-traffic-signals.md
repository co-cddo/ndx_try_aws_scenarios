# Traffic signal faults

## What we look after

Aldershire is responsible for 142 sets of traffic signals, 38 puffin / pelican / toucan crossings, and 7 zebra crossings (zebras have no signals but the beacon is part of our maintenance). The signals are linked to the Aldermarket Urban Traffic Management Centre (UTMC) on Holborough Road.

We are not responsible for railway level-crossing barriers (Network Rail), private estate signals (the management company), or signals on the M-numbered motorway network (National Highways).

## Reporting a fault

Report a signal fault at www.aldershire.gov.uk/highways/signals or phone the Traffic Signals Help Desk on 01632 960 535 (24-hour line, answered by UTMC operators). Provide:

- The junction (e.g. "London Road / Aldermarket High Street") or nearest landmark
- The fault you have observed (lights all out, lights stuck on red, pedestrian phase missing, signal head loose, button not working, graffiti)
- Whether the junction is causing congestion or risk

## Response targets

| Fault | Target |
| ----- | ------ |
| All lights out at any signal | Make-safe within 30 minutes (typically by switching to part-time operation or signed give-way) |
| Stuck phase on a strategic route | Make-safe within 1 hour |
| Single faulty lamp (other phases working) | Within 8 working hours |
| Pedestrian button broken | Within 5 working days |
| Cosmetic damage (graffiti, missing pole reflector) | Within 10 working days |

## Linked signals and SCOOT

Around 60 percent of Aldermarket town-centre signals are SCOOT-controlled (Split Cycle Offset Optimisation Technique). SCOOT optimises green time across a corridor based on real-time vehicle counts at upstream and downstream stop-lines. If a SCOOT detector is faulty the affected signals fall back to fixed-time plans. We aim to repair SCOOT detectors within 5 working days; in the meantime corridor performance may degrade in the peak.

## Bus priority

Around 20 sets of signals on Aldermarket Travel routes use Bus Selective Vehicle Detection (SVD). A bus running late triggers an early green. SVD is provided by RFID transponders on buses; if a bus appears to have lost priority please tell us the route number and approximate time as it helps us pinpoint the affected vehicle.

## Pedestrian crossings

If a pedestrian crossing is out of service, the rule is to give way to anyone visibly waiting to cross. Drivers should slow and treat the crossing as an unmarked junction. Faults are made-safe with portable temporary lights or stop-and-go boards if traffic flows are heavy.

## Pedestrian "wait" indicator not lighting

The wait indicator confirms your button-press has been registered. If it does not light, the button or wait lamp may be faulty. Most crossings will still bring the pedestrian green even with a faulty wait lamp; report the fault but the crossing should still cycle.
