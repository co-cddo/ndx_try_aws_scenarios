# Speed cameras: operation and signage

## Who operates speed cameras in Aldershire

Speed cameras in Aldershire are operated by the Aldershire and Mereborough Safer Roads Partnership, a joint body of:

- Aldershire District Council (highways data and engineering)
- Aldershire County Police
- His Majesty's Courts Service
- The Office of the Police and Crime Commissioner

The partnership funds, sites, and maintains the camera network. Aldershire District Council does not issue Notices of Intended Prosecution and does not collect fines; that is a Police function.

## Camera types

| Type | Description |
| ---- | ----------- |
| Fixed Gatso | Yellow rear-facing cameras on a roadside pole. Cover one direction. |
| Truvelo | Forward-facing, capture driver image. Less obvious. |
| Average speed (SPECS / VECTOR) | Pairs of cameras over a measured distance. Calculate average speed across the section. |
| Mobile / van-based | Police-operated vans, marked, deployed at variable sites |
| Red light | Cameras at signalised junctions to enforce the red signal |

Average-speed enforcement is in use on the A52 Vale Bypass between junctions 4 and 7 (a 6.4 km stretch).

## Signage and visibility

All fixed and average-speed camera installations in Aldershire are:

- Painted yellow (national standard since 2016)
- Signed at the start of the enforcement zone with the diamond camera sign and where applicable a "Cameras change to average speed" sign
- Listed on the partnership's public site at www.am-saferroads.org.uk/cameras with map and location

Mobile enforcement vans are also signed locally; a list of the ten most-used sites in Aldershire is published quarterly.

## Tolerance

The partnership does not publish a tolerance threshold. National guidance from the National Police Chiefs' Council suggests typical thresholds of 10 percent plus 2 mph (e.g. 35 mph in a 30 mph limit), but each force decides. Higher speeds and certain offences (e.g. inside a 20 mph zone) may attract a Notice of Intended Prosecution at lower margins.

## Speed Awareness Course offer

Drivers caught at low margins above the threshold may be offered a National Speed Awareness Course as an alternative to a fixed penalty and points. The offer is at the discretion of the Police; not everyone is eligible (eligibility includes no recent course attendance, valid licence, etc.). Course delivery in Aldershire is through TTC at the Aldermarket Conference Centre.

## Reporting a faulty camera

If you believe a camera is malfunctioning, phone the Safer Roads Partnership on 01632 960 555 or use the contact form at www.am-saferroads.org.uk. Fixed cameras have status indicators that the partnership monitors centrally.

For challenges to a Notice of Intended Prosecution, follow the instructions on the notice itself; this is not an Aldershire District Council matter.
