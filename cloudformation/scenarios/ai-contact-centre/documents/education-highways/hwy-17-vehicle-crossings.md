# Vehicle crossings (drop kerb)

## What a vehicle crossing is

A vehicle crossing (commonly called a "drop kerb") is the strengthened section of footway and lowered kerb that allows a vehicle to drive across the pavement to a private driveway. It must be installed under licence by an Aldershire-approved contractor.

If you park your car on your front garden without a vehicle crossing, you may be:

- Damaging the kerb and footway (which the council can recover the cost of repairing from you)
- Causing inconvenience or danger by mounting the kerb in a non-strengthened section
- Liable to enforcement under the Highways Act 1980

## Eligibility

Before applying you should make sure your driveway:

- Is at least 4.8 metres from front kerb to property frontage (so a typical car can park clear of the highway)
- Is wide enough (typically 2.4 metres minimum, 3.0 metres for double driveways)
- Does not create access onto a road with a 50 mph or higher limit (those need a Section 184 application reviewed by the network manager)
- Does not require felling a council-protected highway tree (separate consent under the Tree Preservation rules)
- Does not block an existing pavement bus stop, controlled crossing, or critical sightline

Front gardens are usually owned freehold by you, but the area between the back of your boundary and the kerb (the verge and footway) is council highway. You cannot legally drive over this without a crossing.

## How to apply

Apply at www.aldershire.gov.uk/highways/dropkerb. The two-stage process:

1. Pre-application assessment (free): a Highways officer site-visits within 4 weeks. Outcomes are "approved", "approved with conditions", "refused", with reasons.
2. Application proper: pay the fee and choose an approved contractor from the list. Aldershire's approved contractors carry the council's specifications and insurance; using an unapproved contractor invalidates the licence.

## Fees (2025 to 2026)

| Item | Fee |
| ---- | --- |
| Pre-application assessment | Free |
| Standard licence (single vehicle, residential) | 285 GBP |
| Larger / double crossing | 380 GBP |
| Contractor work (paid to the contractor, not the council) | Approximately 1,600 to 3,200 GBP depending on width, surface, and trees |
| Re-inspection following snagging | 95 GBP |

The contractor invoices you separately for the build cost.

## What the contractor does

The contractor saws and removes the existing footway, installs a strengthened build-up to council specification (Type 1 sub-base, asphalt or block paving to match, dropped kerbs and tactile paving where the crossing affects a controlled-crossing alignment), and reinstates to a sign-off inspection. Sign-off is by the council inspector, not the contractor; you should not pay the final invoice until sign-off is issued.

## Maintenance

Once installed, the council adopts the highway portion of the crossing and is responsible for surface defects (e.g. potholes in the crossing). You remain responsible for keeping the crossing clear of obstructions on the private side.
