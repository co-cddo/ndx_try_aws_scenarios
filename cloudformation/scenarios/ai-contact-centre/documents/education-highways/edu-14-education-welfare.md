# Education welfare officers: attendance and truancy

## What we do

Aldershire's Education Welfare Service is a small team of seven officers, plus a senior practitioner, working with schools, families, and partner agencies to keep children in school and learning. We support attendance, tackle persistent absence, and act on the council's statutory duties around school attendance.

We are not the police. Our work is rooted in support and partnership, but we do enforce attendance law where voluntary engagement fails.

## When schools refer to us

Schools refer pupils to the Education Welfare Service typically when overall attendance falls below 90 percent, or when a pattern of unauthorised absence emerges. Each Aldershire school has a named link education welfare officer who attends termly attendance review meetings.

The officer's first step is usually a phone call or home visit to understand what is going on. Attendance can be affected by anxiety, bereavement, parental ill health, bullying, transport, or simply chaotic mornings. The officer brokers support from the school, the council, and partners (housing, mental health, family support).

## Penalty notices

Where unauthorised absence persists despite support, the council can issue a Fixed Penalty Notice (FPN) under the Education Act 1996. From August 2024 the national framework is:

| Stage | Penalty |
| ----- | ------- |
| First offence in a 3-year rolling window | 80 GBP if paid within 21 days, rising to 160 GBP if paid within 28 days |
| Second offence in the same window | 160 GBP, no early-payment reduction |
| Third offence | Direct prosecution, no further FPN |

FPNs are issued per parent per child. Unauthorised term-time holidays and unauthorised lateness are both grounds for an FPN, alongside general persistent absence.

## Prosecution

Where parents do not pay an FPN within 28 days, or where a third offence occurs, the council prosecutes under section 444 of the Education Act 1996. There are two offences:

- Section 444(1): not securing regular attendance (a strict liability offence; maximum fine 2,500 GBP, no custody)
- Section 444(1A): the aggravated offence where parents knew of the absence and did not act (maximum 2,500 GBP and up to 3 months custody)

In Aldershire we prosecute around 30 cases a year at Aldermarket Magistrates' Court. We always offer a final opportunity for engagement before laying information.

## Attendance support that works

Aldershire's attendance pilot (with the school nurse service) reduced persistent absence by 14 percent in pilot schools in 2024 to 2025. The model is being rolled out borough-wide. Schools can self-refer to the pilot via 01632 960 432.

For attendance worries, parents should always speak to the school first. The Education Welfare Service main line is 01632 960 430.
