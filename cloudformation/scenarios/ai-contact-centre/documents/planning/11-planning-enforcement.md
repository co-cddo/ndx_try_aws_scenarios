# Planning enforcement

If development takes place without planning permission, or in breach of conditions, Aldershire can take enforcement action under Part VII of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990.

## What is unauthorised development

- Building work without permission (e.g. a rear extension exceeding PD limits)
- Change of use without permission (e.g. a house used as an HMO in an Article 4 area without consent)
- Breach of a planning condition (e.g. opening times, materials, landscaping requirements)
- Failure to comply with a section 106 obligation
- Display of unlawful advertisements

## How the council finds out

- Public complaints (about 65% of cases)
- Officer site visits
- Aerial photography comparison
- Cross-checks against application records and council tax / business rates data

To report an enforcement concern, call 01394 555 0231 or use planning.aldershire.gov.uk/enforcement-report.

## Time limits ("immunity")

Unauthorised development becomes immune from enforcement after time has passed:

- Operational development (building work): 4 years from substantial completion
- Change of use to a single dwelling: 4 years
- Other changes of use, breach of condition: 10 years

Note: the Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023 changes some of these to a 10-year period for new breaches occurring on or after 25 April 2024. For breaches before that date, the old rules apply.

If the breach is concealed (e.g. a building disguised as a haystack), section 171BA allows enforcement at any time.

## Enforcement notices

A planning enforcement notice (section 172) requires the breach to be remedied. It must specify:

- The breach
- Steps to remedy it
- Time for compliance (typically 3 to 12 months)

You can appeal an enforcement notice to the Planning Inspectorate before it takes effect.

## Other enforcement tools

- Breach of Condition Notice (section 187A): no appeal, criminal prosecution if not complied with
- Stop Notice / Temporary Stop Notice: stops works pending decision (see separate document)
- Injunction (section 187B): High Court order for severe breaches
- Section 215 Notice: requires land to be tidied up (untidy land affecting amenity)

## Penalties

- Failure to comply with an enforcement notice: unlimited fine on indictment
- Operating without consent for advertisements: fine up to GBP 2,500
- Listed building offences (separate regime): up to 2 years' imprisonment
