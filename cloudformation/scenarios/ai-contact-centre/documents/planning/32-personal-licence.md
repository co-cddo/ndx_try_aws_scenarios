# Personal licence (alcohol service)

A Personal Licence allows the holder to authorise the sale of alcohol from any premises with a Premises Licence. It is governed by sections 111 to 135 of the Licensing Act 2003.

## When a Personal Licence is needed

- To be the Designated Premises Supervisor (DPS) of any licensed premises selling alcohol
- To authorise individual sales of alcohol when the DPS is not present
- It is generally good practice for any responsible person managing alcohol sales

## Eligibility

You must:

- Be 18 or over
- Hold an "accredited licensing qualification" (e.g. APLH at Level 2)
- Not have forfeited a personal licence in the last 5 years
- Disclose any "relevant" or "foreign" convictions
- Provide a Disclosure and Barring Service Basic check (issued in the last month)
- Be entitled to work in the UK (the council verifies under the Immigration Act 2014)

## Disqualifying convictions

Schedule 4 of the 2003 Act lists "relevant offences", including:

- Trading Standards offences (false labelling, age-restricted sales)
- Sexual Offences Act offences
- Theft, fraud, dishonesty
- Most violence offences
- Drug offences (Misuse of Drugs Act 1971)
- Driving while disqualified or under the influence

Spent convictions (under the Rehabilitation of Offenders Act 1974) generally do not need to be disclosed for personal licences, except in specified circumstances.

## How to apply

Submit Form PL1 to Aldershire Licensing. Documents required:

- Completed application form
- Two passport-size photographs (one endorsed by a person of standing as a true likeness)
- Original APLH certificate (or accredited equivalent)
- Original DBS Basic Disclosure (under one month old)
- Proof of right to work in the UK
- Fee of GBP 37

Aldershire processes applications within 28 days. The police have 14 days to object on crime and disorder grounds. If they object, a hearing follows.

## Validity

Personal Licences do not expire. They remain valid as long as the holder:

- Notifies any change of name or address within 14 days
- Notifies any conviction for a relevant offence
- Surrenders the licence if convicted of a Schedule 4 offence and disqualified

A current photograph and current address are required at all times.

## Forfeiture

A court can forfeit a Personal Licence on conviction of a relevant offence (section 129). The licensing authority is notified by the court.

## Replacement

Lost or damaged licence: apply for a replacement on Form PL3, fee GBP 10.50.

## Notification of changes

You must notify Aldershire of:

- Change of name or address (Form PL2, fee GBP 10.50)
- Conviction for a relevant offence (no fee, but mandatory)

## Holders' duties

Personal Licence holders must:

- Be present (or available) when authorising alcohol sales (varies by Premises Licence conditions)
- Sign the relevant pages of the licence
- Refuse sales to under-18s (Challenge 25 is best practice)
