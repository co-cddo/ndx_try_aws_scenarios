# Delegated decisions

Aldershire's Constitution delegates the great majority of planning decisions to officers. This speeds up determination, reduces costs, and reserves Committee time for the most significant cases.

## What is delegated

The Director of Place (or designated officers) can decide:

- All householder applications
- All Lawful Development Certificate applications
- All Listed Building Consent applications for Grade II buildings (Grade I and Grade II* go to Committee unless minor)
- All Discharge of Conditions
- All Non-material amendments
- All Section 73 variations on existing permissions
- Tree works (TPO and Conservation Area)
- Hedgerow Removal Notices
- Advertisement consent (most categories)
- Minor commercial applications under 100 sqm
- Minor housing applications of 1 to 9 dwellings (with no objection from key consultees and no objection from 3+ residents or the parish council)

## What goes to Committee

The Constitution requires Committee determination where:

- 10+ dwellings
- 1,000+ sqm of new commercial floorspace
- Any application called in by a Ward Councillor (with reasons)
- Any application where the parish council objects and the officer recommendation is approval
- Any application where 6+ residents have objected and the officer recommendation is approval
- Any application departing from the Local Plan
- Any application where Aldershire is the applicant or has an interest in the land

## How call-in works

A ward councillor can call an application to Committee within 14 days of validation. The reasons must be material planning considerations (not, for example, "the applicant is unpopular"). The Director of Place reviews the reasons; clearly non-material reasons are rejected.

## Officer reports

Every delegated decision is supported by an officer report which:

- Describes the proposal
- Lists consultations and representations
- Identifies the development plan policies
- Assesses material considerations
- Concludes with a reasoned recommendation

Reports are published on the planning register at planning.aldershire.gov.uk. Anyone can read them.

## Decision notices

Issued within 5 working days of the decision. Sent to the applicant and posted on the register. Decisions list any conditions (with reasons) and informatives.

## Performance

Aldershire's delegation rate is currently 92% (2024). This is in line with the national average. The remaining 8% go to Committee. Of Committee decisions, 78% follow the officer recommendation.

## Scrutiny

The Audit Committee reviews delegated decisions quarterly to ensure compliance with the Constitution. Annual training is mandatory for all members of the Planning Committee and for any substitute.
