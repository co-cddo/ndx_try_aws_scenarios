# Outline versus full planning permission

For larger or speculative schemes you can apply for either outline or full planning permission. Choosing the right route saves money and time.

## Full permission

A full application includes all details of the proposed development: siting, layout, scale, appearance, access, landscaping. The decision authorises the development as drawn. You must commence development within 3 years of the decision (section 91 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990).

When to use:

- Householder works (the only available route)
- Single building schemes where you have detailed plans
- Where you want certainty about the appearance before investing in detailed design

## Outline permission

An outline application establishes the principle of development. Some matters are "reserved" for later approval. You can reserve:

- Access (the points of access to the site)
- Appearance
- Landscaping
- Layout
- Scale

You must apply for "Reserved Matters" approval within 3 years of the outline decision. The outline itself runs out 2 years after the last reserved matter is approved (or 5 years total, whichever is earlier).

When to use:

- Large housing or commercial sites where detailed design is premature
- To establish development principle before spending on detailed design
- To attract bids from developers who will design within the outline parameters

## Hybrid applications

Aldershire accepts hybrid applications: full permission for one part of a site (e.g. the first phase of houses) and outline for the rest. These are useful for phased schemes such as Wendlebury Garden Village.

## Fees comparison

Outline: GBP 578 per 0.1 hectare (capped at GBP 17,335)
Full housing: GBP 578 per dwelling for first 50 dwellings, then GBP 173 per dwelling thereafter (capped)

Reserved Matters: based on the same per-hectare or per-dwelling rate as the original outline. The "free go" provision does not apply once outline has been granted.

## Reasonable conditions

Outline permissions usually attach more conditions than full permissions because details are not yet known. These may include:

- Submission of a phasing plan
- Pre-commencement conditions on contamination, archaeology, drainage
- Section 106 obligations (see separate document)
- Community Infrastructure Levy liability (see separate document)
