# Reserved matters

Once outline planning permission is granted, the "reserved matters" still need approval before development can start. Reserved matters are the details that were deliberately set aside at the outline stage.

## What can be reserved

Article 2 of the Town and Country Planning (Development Management Procedure) (England) Order 2015 defines five reserved matters:

- Access: the points where the development connects to the highway
- Appearance: aspects of design, materials, finishes, fenestration
- Landscaping: planting, boundary treatments, ecological features
- Layout: the way buildings are arranged on the site, internal roads, public spaces
- Scale: the height, width, length of buildings

You only need to apply for those matters that were reserved at the outline. If the outline included full details on (say) access, that point is fixed.

## Time limits

Section 92 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 sets the timetable:

- Reserved matters application(s): within 3 years of the outline decision
- Commencement of development: within 2 years of the final reserved matters approval (or 5 years from the outline, whichever is earlier)

The council can vary these limits by condition (often does for large phased developments).

## How to apply

Apply through the Planning Portal. You will need:

- Site plan showing red line boundary
- Detailed drawings: floor plans, elevations, sections
- Materials schedule
- Landscaping plan (often a Level 2 specification)
- Tree retention and protection plan
- External lighting (if any)
- Confirmation of compliance with the outline's pre-commencement conditions

## Fees

The reserved matters fee is based on the same scale as the original outline (per dwelling or per hectare). If the original outline had fewer than the eventual dwelling count, the fee is calculated on the actual number proposed in the reserved matters.

There is no free go provision once outline has been granted.

## Determination

Statutory period is 8 weeks (or 13 weeks for major reserved matters). Aldershire usually consults the same parties as for the outline but the consultation can be lighter where principle is already established.

The Planning team cannot revisit matters that were resolved at outline. New objections about (say) the principle of development cannot be considered. Objections must focus on the reserved matters themselves.

## Variations

If the reserved matters proposed depart from the outline (e.g. more units than indicated), you may need a section 73 application to vary the outline first.
