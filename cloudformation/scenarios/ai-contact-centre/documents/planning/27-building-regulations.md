# Building regulations - scope and triggers

The Building Regulations 2010 (made under the Building Act 1984) apply to most building work in England. Their purpose is to ensure buildings are safe, healthy, and accessible. The regulations are separate from planning law.

## What counts as "building work"

Regulation 3 defines building work to include:

- Erection or extension of a building
- Material alteration of a building (e.g. structural changes, work affecting fire resistance)
- Provision or extension of a "controlled service or fitting" (heating, hot water, drainage, electrics, replacement windows)
- Work to or in connection with a "thermal element" (insulation, replacement of more than 25% of a thermal element)
- Material change of use (e.g. dwelling to flats)

## Not building work

- Internal redecoration
- Replacing like-for-like a window where no thermal element is changed (rare; most window changes are caught)
- Repairing existing service pipes
- Most repairs to a building of the same nature and quality as the original

## Material change of use

Triggers full Building Regulations compliance. Examples:

- A building first used as a dwelling
- A building containing a flat where it did not before
- A hotel or boarding house first formed from another use
- An institution first formed
- A public building first formed
- A shop or café first formed

A "material change of use" can occur even without external building work; the use change alone triggers the regulations.

## Exempt buildings

Schedule 2 of the Regulations exempts:

- Class 1: buildings controlled under other legislation (e.g. some scheduled monuments)
- Class 2: most agricultural buildings
- Class 3: temporary buildings for less than 28 days
- Class 4: small detached buildings (no sleeping accommodation, max 30 sqm, fire-safe)
- Class 5: extensions to dwellings (carports, conservatories under 30 sqm with safety glazing and direct fresh air ventilation - rare in practice)
- Class 6: small porches under 30 sqm
- Class 7: small garages under 30 sqm

The exemptions are narrow and frequently misread. If in doubt, ask Building Control.

## Self-certification

Some work can be self-certified by an installer registered with a Competent Person Scheme:

- Replacement windows (FENSA, CERTASS, Certas)
- Electrical work in dwellings (NICEIC, NAPIT, ELECSA, Stroma)
- Gas appliances (Gas Safe Register)
- Oil-fired appliances (OFTEC)
- Solid fuel appliances (HETAS)
- Solar panels (MCS)

A certificate is issued direct to the householder and lodged with the local authority.

## Penalties

Carrying out unauthorised building work is a criminal offence. Penalties under section 35 of the Building Act 1984:

- Magistrates' Court: fine up to GBP 5,000
- Daily fine: GBP 50 per day continuing
- Section 36: enforcement notice requiring removal or alteration of non-compliant work (up to 12 months after completion)

Civil enforcement can also include refusal of completion certificate, with conveyancing implications on sale.
