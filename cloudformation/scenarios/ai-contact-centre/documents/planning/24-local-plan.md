# Aldershire Local Plan

The Local Plan sets out Aldershire's planning policies and the strategy for managing development across the district. It is the starting point for determining all planning applications, in line with section 38(6) of the Planning and Compulsory Purchase Act 2004 and section 70 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990.

## Plan documents

The current adopted Local Plan was adopted on 18 March 2022 and covers the period to 2040. It comprises:

- Strategic Policies (10 policies covering housing, employment, climate, design)
- Development Management Policies (32 policies on specific issues)
- Site Allocations (134 sites for housing, employment, mixed-use)
- The Policies Map (digital and printed format)

All documents are at planning.aldershire.gov.uk/local-plan.

## Key policies

- Policy SS1: Spatial strategy - 60% of growth at Aldermarket, 25% at Bramley Heath, 15% in the Vale
- Policy H1: Housing requirement - 1,260 new homes per year (2022 to 2040)
- Policy H5: Affordable housing (30% on schemes of 11+ dwellings)
- Policy E1: Economic development - protect employment land, encourage diversification
- Policy CC1: Climate change - net zero new homes from 2027, embodied carbon reporting
- Policy DM1: Design - mandatory adherence to Aldermarket Design Code
- Policy NE3: Biodiversity Net Gain - 10% minimum (not just statutory minimum)
- Policy H10: Settlement boundaries - development outside boundaries strictly controlled

## Site allocations

The Plan allocates:

- 23 housing sites (total capacity: 8,400 dwellings)
- 9 employment sites (62 hectares of new B1/B2/B8)
- 4 mixed-use regeneration sites (Aldermarket Quayside, Wendlebury Garden Village, Bramley Heath North, Mill Lane Riverside)
- 2 strategic transport sites (Aldermarket Transport Hub, Vale Park & Ride)

Each allocation is a "Strategic Site" and supported by a site-specific policy that sets density, infrastructure, and design requirements.

## Policies Map

The interactive Policies Map shows: Settlement Boundaries, Conservation Areas, Listed Buildings, TPOs, Flood Zones, Open Spaces, Green Belt (only the Mill Lane corridor), Heritage Coast, AONB (Aldershire Vale Western Slopes).

Search by address at planning.aldershire.gov.uk/policies-map.

## Five-Year Housing Land Supply

Aldershire has a current 5.6-year supply (April 2024). This is published quarterly. If supply drops below 5 years, the National Planning Policy Framework's "tilted balance" applies, weakening the Council's ability to refuse housing applications on policy grounds.

## Plan review

Local plans must be reviewed at least every 5 years (Town and Country Planning (Local Planning) (England) Regulations 2012). Aldershire's Plan Review started in 2025 with a Call for Sites; the new Plan is expected to be adopted by 2028.

## Examination

The 2022 Plan was examined by Inspector Caroline Heath BSc (Hons) MA MRTPI. Examination hearings ran for 4 weeks. Modifications were imposed (notably to the housing trajectory) before the Plan was found "sound" and "legally compliant".
