# Building Control

Building Control is a separate function from Planning. Planning regulates the use and external appearance of buildings. Building Control regulates the structural and technical safety of building work. You may need both, just one, or neither.

## What Building Control covers

The Building Regulations 2010 set out 18 technical "Approved Documents" covering:

- Part A: Structure (foundations, loadbearing walls)
- Part B: Fire safety (escape routes, fire detection)
- Part C: Site preparation, contaminants, dampness
- Part D: Toxic substances
- Part E: Sound insulation
- Part F: Ventilation
- Part G: Sanitation, hot water, water efficiency
- Part H: Drainage and waste disposal
- Part J: Combustion appliances and fuel storage
- Part K: Protection from falling, collision, impact
- Part L: Conservation of fuel and power (replaced by the Future Homes Standard from 2025)
- Part M: Access to and use of buildings
- Part N: (now repealed)
- Part P: Electrical safety in dwellings
- Part Q: Security in dwellings
- Part R: Physical infrastructure for high-speed electronic communications
- Part S: Infrastructure for the charging of electric vehicles (added 2022)
- Part T: Toilet accommodation (added 2024)

## When Building Control approval is needed

Most building work, including:

- Extensions and loft conversions
- Internal structural alterations (removing chimney breasts, lintels)
- New windows replacement (FENSA / CERTASS schemes are an alternative)
- Electrical work in kitchens, bathrooms, outdoors (Part P)
- New boilers, heating systems (Gas Safe / OFTEC alternatives)
- Drainage alterations
- Replacement roofs (where over 50% of an element is replaced)
- New foundations

## Two routes for approval

### Building Notice

Cheaper, faster, but less protective. Used by householders for low-risk work. Inspectors visit at key stages and decide if work complies.

### Full Plans

Detailed plans submitted in advance. Plans are checked, approved, and form a contractual standard. Used for commercial work and complex domestic projects.

## Aldershire Building Control fees

Indicative scale (call for exact quote):

- Single-storey rear extension up to 40 sqm: GBP 600 plan check + GBP 720 inspection (incl VAT)
- Loft conversion: GBP 540 plan + GBP 660 inspection
- New 2-bed dwelling: GBP 950 plan + GBP 1,140 inspection
- Internal alterations: GBP 360 minimum
- Electrical work only (Part P): GBP 250

Aldershire competes with Approved Inspectors (private sector). Quotations are also available from Approved Inspectors registered with the Construction Industry Council.

## Inspection regime

For each approved set of works, you call the inspector before key stages:

- Excavation of foundations
- Concrete pour
- DPC level
- Drainage
- Roof (before plasterboard)
- Pre-occupation completion

Failure to call for inspection can mean later difficulties when selling the house (requires a Regularisation Certificate).

## Completion certificate

Issued at the end of the project. Your conveyancer will require this on sale.
