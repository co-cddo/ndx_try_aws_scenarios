# Party Wall etc. Act 1996

If your building work affects a party wall, party fence wall, or excavation near a neighbour's building, you have legal duties under the Party Wall etc. Act 1996. The Act applies in England and Wales.

## Scope

The Act covers:

- Section 1: building a new wall on or astride the boundary
- Section 2: works to an existing party wall (cutting in, raising, demolishing, repairing, weatherproofing)
- Section 6: excavation within 3 metres of a neighbour's building, going below the foundations; or within 6 metres if a 45 degree line from the bottom of the foundations would be cut

## Aldershire's role

Aldershire is NOT a party-wall enforcement authority. The Act is a private dispute resolution code. The council does not adjudicate party wall disputes, does not enforce notices, and cannot stop work.

That said, when issuing planning permission or building control approval, Aldershire may attach an "informative" reminding the applicant of party wall obligations.

## What you need to do

1. Identify if the Act applies (use the diagrams in the official Communities and Local Government Explanatory Booklet, available free at gov.uk).
2. Serve a written party wall notice on each adjoining owner. Notice periods:
   - 1 month for section 6 excavations or new boundary walls
   - 2 months for section 2 works to existing party walls
3. The neighbour can:
   - Consent in writing (cheapest, simplest)
   - Dissent: a "dispute" under the Act arises automatically
4. If dissented, both parties appoint surveyors. Either:
   - Each appoints their own surveyor (two-surveyor procedure)
   - They jointly agree one "agreed surveyor"
5. The surveyor(s) prepare a Party Wall Award - a binding document setting out works, conditions, access, and condition of the property before works.
6. The building owner pays the surveyor fees (typically GBP 1,000 to GBP 3,000 for a two-surveyor procedure on a residential extension).

## Awards

A Party Wall Award is binding on both parties. It typically covers:

- The works to be carried out
- The way the works will be done
- Access arrangements
- The condition of the adjoining property before works
- A schedule of pre-existing defects

If either party is unhappy with the Award, they have 14 days to appeal to the County Court.

## Common mistakes

- Starting work without serving notice (the neighbour can apply for an injunction)
- Treating an oral agreement as sufficient (the Act requires notice in writing)
- Forgetting that a permitted development extension still needs party wall notice if it triggers the Act
- Mistakenly thinking planning permission overrides party wall duties (it does not)
