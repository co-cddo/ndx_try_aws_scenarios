# Listed building consent

Aldershire has 412 listed buildings, including the Grade I Aldermarket Guildhall, several Grade II* churches, and over 380 Grade II properties. Listing covers the entire structure, inside and out, and any object or structure fixed to it.

## What needs consent

Listed Building Consent (LBC) is needed for any works that affect the character of the listed building. This includes:

- Internal alterations: removing partitions, replacing fireplaces, replastering historic walls
- External alterations: window replacement, repointing, repainting (where specifically restricted), satellite dishes
- Demolition of all or part of the building
- New extensions, however small
- Changes to fixtures fitted to the building (e.g. panelled doors, staircases)

Consent is needed even when no planning permission is required. The two are separate regimes.

## Grade differences

- Grade I: buildings of exceptional interest (about 2.5% of listings nationally). Aldermarket Guildhall is the only Grade I in the district. Heritage England must be consulted on most applications.
- Grade II*: particularly important buildings of more than special interest (about 5.8% of listings). Includes St Cuthbert's Church, Bramley Heath, and the Aldershire Vale Manor House. Historic England must be consulted on works affecting the principal interest.
- Grade II: buildings of special interest, warranting effort to preserve them (about 91.7% of listings). Most decisions are taken by Aldershire's Conservation Officer under delegated powers.

## How to apply

Apply through the Planning Portal. There is no fee for Listed Building Consent. You must include:

- Heritage Statement describing the building's significance and the impact of the proposed works
- Photographs of all areas affected, internally and externally
- Detailed drawings (typically 1:20 or 1:10 for joinery, mouldings, fireplaces)
- Method statements for sensitive repairs (e.g. lime mortar specification)

Statutory determination is 8 weeks. Complex applications often take 13 weeks by agreement.

## Penalties

It is a criminal offence under section 9 of the Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990 to carry out works without consent. Maximum penalty on conviction is an unlimited fine and up to 2 years' imprisonment.
