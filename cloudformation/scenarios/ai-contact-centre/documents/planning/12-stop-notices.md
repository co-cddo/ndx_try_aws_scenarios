# Stop notices

A stop notice is an emergency planning enforcement tool. It can prohibit specified works from continuing while the council investigates or pursues a longer-term remedy.

## Two types

### Temporary Stop Notice (TSN)

- Issued under section 171E of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990
- Effect: immediate, no appeal, no compensation
- Maximum duration: 28 days
- Used to halt works while the council assesses whether to issue an enforcement notice
- Cannot stop residential occupation of a dwellinghouse already in use as a residence

### Stop Notice (full)

- Issued alongside or after an Enforcement Notice
- Section 183 of the Act
- Lasts until the Enforcement Notice takes effect (typically 28+ days)
- Can be appealed indirectly through the Enforcement Notice appeal
- Compensation may be payable if the Enforcement Notice is later quashed

## When Aldershire uses them

Stop notices are reserved for cases where continuing works would cause "serious harm to amenity, public safety, or environmental damage". Recent examples in Aldershire:

- 2024: TSN served on a property in Mill Lane Conservation Area where original windows were being removed without consent
- 2023: TSN served on unauthorised tree felling at Bramley Heath Common
- 2023: full Stop Notice issued alongside an Enforcement Notice for an unauthorised car repair business in residential land

## Penalties

It is a criminal offence to contravene a Stop Notice. Maximum penalties:

- Magistrates' Court: GBP 20,000 fine
- Crown Court: unlimited fine

Each day the breach continues counts as a separate offence.

## Compensation

Compensation is payable on a full Stop Notice (not a TSN) if:

- The associated Enforcement Notice is quashed on appeal, OR
- The notice is withdrawn, OR
- The works in question turn out to be lawful

Compensation covers loss directly attributable to the notice. Claims must be made within 12 months of the notice ceasing to have effect.

## Authorisation

A Temporary Stop Notice can be issued by a Senior Enforcement Officer. A full Stop Notice requires the Head of Planning's authorisation. Aldershire's Constitution requires consultation with the Legal team and the Director of Place before issue.
