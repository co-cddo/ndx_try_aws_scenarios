# Public Spaces Protection Orders (PSPOs) - drinking control

Public Spaces Protection Orders replaced Designated Public Place Orders (DPPOs) under the Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014. The transition was completed in 2017. Existing DPPOs were converted into PSPOs.

## What a PSPO does

A PSPO can prohibit or require behaviours in a defined public area to address persistent anti-social behaviour. Common uses:

- Restricting drinking of alcohol in public
- Restricting dog control (e.g. dogs on leads, dogs banned from children's play areas)
- Restricting persistent begging or street drinking
- Prohibiting unauthorised use of e-scooters in pedestrian zones (where appropriate)
- Restricting amplified noise (busking)

A PSPO cannot create new criminal offences; it triggers fixed-penalty notices and on-conviction fines for breach.

## Aldershire's PSPOs

Aldershire has 4 active PSPOs (as of 2024):

1. Aldermarket Town Centre PSPO (2022) - alcohol consumption restriction in Aldermarket Square, Mill Street, Bridge Lane and Riverside Walk
2. Bramley Heath Common PSPO (2021) - dog control (on lead in specified zones), alcohol restriction in the bandstand area
3. Wendlebury Pleasure Park PSPO (2020) - dog control near play equipment
4. Stoneycombe Beach PSPO (2023) - dogs banned from main beach 1 May to 30 September; alcohol restriction in beach huts and adjoining promenade

Maps and full conditions: aldershire.gov.uk/pspo

## Effect on drinking

Within an alcohol-restriction PSPO, an authorised officer (police, council enforcement officer, designated PCSO) can:

- Require you to surrender an open or unsealed alcohol container
- Require you to stop drinking
- Issue a Fixed Penalty Notice (FPN) of GBP 100 if you refuse

Failure to comply with the FPN within 14 days can lead to prosecution; on conviction, fine up to GBP 1,000.

## Limits and protections

A PSPO cannot:

- Prevent activities that are not anti-social
- Apply to private premises (only public places)
- Prohibit lawful protest or peaceful assembly (Article 11 ECHR)
- Last more than 3 years without renewal
- Be made without consultation

## Making and reviewing a PSPO

Aldershire must:

- Be satisfied of the statutory test (persistent activity having a detrimental effect on the quality of life)
- Consult the police, residents, businesses, and owners or occupiers of land affected
- Publish the proposed Order before making it
- Review and consider whether to extend or vary the Order before its 3-year expiry

The Order is published on the council's website and signed at every entry to the controlled area.

## Challenge

Anyone aggrieved by a PSPO can appeal to the High Court within 6 weeks of the Order being made or extended. Grounds: the council had no power, or it failed to comply with consultation requirements.
