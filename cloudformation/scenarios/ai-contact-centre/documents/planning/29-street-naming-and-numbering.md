# Street naming and numbering

When new buildings, conversions, or roads are created, addresses must be allocated by Aldershire. Street naming and numbering (SNN) is a statutory function under the Public Health Act 1925 (sections 17 to 19) and the Towns Improvement Clauses Act 1847.

## Why it matters

Without an official address, a property cannot:

- Receive Royal Mail deliveries reliably
- Be assessed for council tax or business rates
- Be located by emergency services (999 / 112 dispatch)
- Connect to utilities under their formal address records
- Appear on Ordnance Survey AddressBase or the National Address Gazetteer

## When SNN applies

- New build dwellings (single units and developments)
- Conversion of existing buildings into new units (e.g. office to flats)
- Subdivision of an existing dwelling
- Renaming an existing property (subject to objection rights)
- New street names on developments
- Reconfiguring an existing development

## Process for new developments

1. Developer or applicant submits SNN application with site plans and unit numbering proposals
2. Council reviews against principles:
   - Numbers run consecutively along the road
   - Even on one side, odd on the other (or sequential where unavoidable)
   - No "13" or "13A" if cultural sensitivity (developer choice; not enforced)
3. Council consults on proposed street name with:
   - Relevant ward councillors
   - Parish council (if applicable)
   - Royal Mail (for postcode allocation)
   - Aldershire Constabulary (avoid duplication, navigation clarity)
4. Aldershire confirms address and notifies LLPG / OS / Royal Mail
5. Postcodes assigned by Royal Mail (allow 4 weeks)

## Aldershire fees (2024)

- Single new dwelling or unit: GBP 95
- Up to 5 plots: GBP 190
- 6 to 30 plots: GBP 285 + GBP 25 per plot
- 31 to 100 plots: GBP 760 + GBP 22 per plot
- Over 100 plots: GBP 2,520 + GBP 19 per plot
- Renaming existing property: GBP 65
- Confirmation of existing address: GBP 35

Fees include LLPG, Royal Mail, and OS notification.

## New street names

Naming proposals should:

- Reflect local heritage or geography (e.g. Stoneycombe Brook Way)
- Avoid duplication within the post town
- Avoid difficult spelling or pronunciation
- Avoid offence
- Avoid commercial branding

The council reserves the right to reject names. There is no appeal route.

## Existing properties

You can apply to give your house a name, with or without a number. Royal Mail rules require the postal number to remain in use; the name is added.

## Aldershire LLPG

Aldershire maintains the Local Land and Property Gazetteer (LLPG) and provides updates daily to the National GeoPlace hub. The LLPG is the authoritative address dataset for council services.
