# Hedgerow regulations

Important countryside hedgerows are protected by the Hedgerows Regulations 1997 (made under section 97 of the Environment Act 1995). The regulations apply to most rural hedgerows over 20 metres long, or shorter ones meeting another field boundary.

## When the regulations apply

A hedgerow is regulated if it:

- Is at least 20 metres long, OR is shorter but joins another hedgerow at each end
- Is on or borders agricultural land, common land, a Local Nature Reserve, an SSSI, or land used for forestry, equestrian or breeding/keeping animals
- Is in countryside (not within the curtilage of a domestic garden)

If you have a garden hedge, the regulations do not apply. High Hedges complaints (Anti-social Behaviour Act 2003 Part 8) are a separate process for evergreen domestic hedges over 2 metres.

## Hedgerow Removal Notice

If you want to remove a regulated hedgerow, you must submit a Hedgerow Removal Notice (Form HR1) to Aldershire at least 42 days before works. There is no fee.

## Council response

Within 42 days the council can:

- Issue a Hedgerow Retention Notice (the hedge is "important" and must be kept)
- Allow removal (silence after 42 days = consent)
- Confirm in writing that the hedge is not regulated

## "Important" hedgerow tests

Schedule 1 of the Regulations sets out 8 criteria. A hedge is important if it meets any of:

- Marks a parish or township boundary pre-1850
- Contains an archaeological feature
- Is part of a designated landscape (battlefield, SSSI, etc.)
- Contains a Schedule 5 protected species or Schedule 8 plant
- Has 7+ woody species in a 30m length (or 6+ in northern England)
- Includes 4+ associated features (ditch, bank, mature standards, etc.)

## Penalties

Removal without notice is a criminal offence under regulation 7. Penalties:

- Magistrates' Court: fine up to GBP 5,000
- Crown Court: unlimited fine
- Replacement planting can be required

The court typically considers the value of the timber and the conservation value of the hedge.
