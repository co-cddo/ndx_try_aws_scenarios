# Section 106 obligations

A Section 106 (S106) obligation is a legal agreement between Aldershire and a developer (or landowner) that secures planning contributions or restrictions which cannot fairly be imposed by a planning condition. It is made under section 106 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990.

## When S106 is used

The Community Infrastructure Levy (CIL) covers many infrastructure contributions, but S106 is still used for:

- On-site or off-site affordable housing
- Mitigation of specific local impacts (e.g. travel plan, contribution to a junction improvement)
- Site-specific contributions (e.g. towards Special Protection Area mitigation)
- Restrictions on use (e.g. occupancy of holiday lets, agricultural ties)

## CIL vs S106

CIL collects pooled contributions for general infrastructure (schools, transport, open space). S106 funds the specific impacts of a specific development. The two are complementary; CIL Regulation 122 prevents double-counting.

## Affordable housing in Aldershire

Aldershire's Local Plan Policy H5 requires:

- 30% affordable housing on schemes of 11+ dwellings
- 40% in the Aldershire Vale rural area where viability allows
- Tenure split: 70% social/affordable rent, 30% intermediate (shared ownership / First Homes)

The S106 will set out the affordable housing schedule, mortgageability, nominations, and review mechanisms.

## Tests for S106 (regulation 122)

A planning obligation is only lawful if it is:

1. Necessary to make the development acceptable in planning terms
2. Directly related to the development
3. Fairly and reasonably related in scale and kind to the development

If a proposed obligation fails any test, it cannot be a reason for granting permission.

## Negotiation

Heads of Terms are agreed during the application. A draft S106 deed is drafted by Aldershire Legal team, reviewed by the developer's solicitor, and engrossed for signature. The deed must be signed and dated before planning permission is issued.

## Costs

The developer pays Aldershire's legal costs (typically GBP 1,800 to GBP 4,500 for a standard S106). Major schemes with bespoke clauses can run to GBP 12,000+.

## Modification or discharge

After 5 years, you can apply to modify or discharge an S106 obligation under section 106A. The council assesses whether the obligation still serves a useful planning purpose. Refusal can be appealed to the Planning Inspectorate.

Affordable housing obligations can also be reviewed if the scheme becomes unviable; viability evidence must be provided in line with PPG 10-021.

## Monitoring

Aldershire monitors S106 compliance via a digital register at planning.aldershire.gov.uk/s106-register. Triggers are set against milestones (commencement, occupation thresholds, completion).
