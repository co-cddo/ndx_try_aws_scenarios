# Lawful Development Certificate

A Lawful Development Certificate (LDC) is a legal document confirming that a use, operation, or activity is lawful for planning purposes. There are two types: existing and proposed.

## Existing (LDC-E)

For development that has already taken place. You apply to confirm that:

- The development is immune from enforcement (see Planning enforcement document for time limits), OR
- The use was begun under a previous permission that has not lapsed, OR
- The development was carried out under permitted development rights

Common uses:

- Demonstrating an unauthorised extension is now too old for enforcement
- Confirming an HMO has been in use as such for over 4 years (and so does not now need permission)
- Confirming a building has been in residential use long enough to count as a dwelling

You will need evidence: utility bills, council tax records, electoral roll entries, dated photographs, sworn statements (statutory declarations).

## Proposed (LDC-P)

For development not yet started. You apply to confirm that:

- The proposed development falls within Permitted Development rights
- The proposed change of use does not need permission

Common uses:

- A homeowner planning a rear extension within Class A limits
- A landlord intending to convert a B8 storage building to E-class commercial under Class M
- A business intending to install solar panels under Class A of Part 14

## Fees (from 1 December 2023)

LDC-E (existing) - same fee as if a planning application had been required, e.g.:

- Single dwelling: GBP 258
- Other use: based on the relevant fee scale

LDC-P (proposed):

- Householder: GBP 129
- Other: half of the equivalent planning application fee

## Burden of proof

The burden is on the applicant. You must show, on the balance of probabilities, that the development is lawful. Aldershire does not investigate beyond the evidence supplied. Insufficient evidence will result in refusal.

## Determination

Statutory period: 8 weeks. There is no consultation period. The decision is on facts and law, not on planning merits.

## Effect

A granted LDC is conclusive proof of lawfulness for planning purposes (section 192(4) of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990). It cannot be revoked unless obtained by deception.

## Refused LDCs

You can appeal to the Planning Inspectorate within 6 months. There is no fee for the appeal.

## Risks

- An LDC application can alert the council to unauthorised development. If refused, enforcement may follow.
- If the LDC describes the use inaccurately, the certificate is worthless.
