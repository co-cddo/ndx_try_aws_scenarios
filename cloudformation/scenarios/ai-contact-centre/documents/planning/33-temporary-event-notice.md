# Temporary Event Notice (TEN)

A Temporary Event Notice authorises licensable activities at a temporary location for a short period. It is the simplest licensing route under the Licensing Act 2003 (sections 100 to 110).

## What a TEN can cover

- Sale of alcohol
- Late-night refreshment (hot food or drink between 23:00 and 05:00)
- Regulated entertainment (live music, recorded music, performance of a play, etc.)

## Limits

- Maximum 499 attendees at any one time
- Maximum 168 hours per event (7 days)
- Maximum 21 days separation between events at the same premises

For each premises (single address):

- 15 TENs per calendar year (note: Levelling-up Act may temporarily allow 20 in some years)
- Maximum 26 days of TENs in total
- TENs by the same individual: 50 per year (5 if no Personal Licence)

## How to apply

File Form TEN1 with Aldershire Licensing AND Aldershire Constabulary AND Aldershire Environmental Health. All three must receive the notice.

Notice periods:

- Standard TEN: at least 10 working days before the event
- Late TEN: 5 to 9 working days before the event (more limited)

Delivery: online (lic.aldershire.gov.uk/ten), in person, or first-class post. Online is fastest and the police get a copy automatically.

## Fee

GBP 21 per TEN, payable to Aldershire on submission.

## Police and Environmental Health objection

The police and Environmental Health each have 3 working days to object. Grounds for objection:

- Prevention of crime and disorder
- Public safety
- Prevention of public nuisance
- Protection of children from harm

If either objects, the council holds a Sub-Committee hearing within 7 working days. The Sub-Committee can:

- Allow the event to proceed
- Allow the event with conditions
- Refuse the TEN

For a Late TEN: police or EH objection means the TEN cannot be used at all (no hearing).

## Conditions

A TEN does not normally carry conditions, BUT if the premises holds an existing Premises Licence, conditions of that licence apply automatically to the TEN activities. Sub-Committee can also impose conditions following an objection hearing.

## Common pitfalls

- Forgetting to notify Aldershire AND the police AND EH (all three required)
- Filing too late (the 10-working-day rule, not 10 days)
- Exceeding the 499-attendee limit (a Premises Licence is needed instead)
- Exceeding 168 hours (planning a longer event needs multiple TENs or a Premises Licence)
- Mistaking a beer festival for a "small event" - usually needs a Premises Licence
- Using a TEN for "premises" that are not properly defined (must be specific address or area)

## Examples

- Village summer fete with bar and live music: TEN covers the day
- Charity quiz night at a function room: TEN if no Premises Licence at venue
- Wedding reception in a marquee in a private garden: TEN if alcohol is sold (not if free-bar)
