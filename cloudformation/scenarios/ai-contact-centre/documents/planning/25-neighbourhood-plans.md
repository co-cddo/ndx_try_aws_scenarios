# Neighbourhood plans

A Neighbourhood Plan is a community-led planning document, prepared by a parish council or designated Neighbourhood Forum. Once "made" (adopted), it forms part of the statutory development plan and carries weight in planning decisions.

## Legislative basis

Localism Act 2011 (section 116, schedule 9), inserting new sections into the Town and Country Planning Act 1990. The detailed procedure is in the Neighbourhood Planning (General) Regulations 2012.

## What a neighbourhood plan can do

- Set local design policies (e.g. roof pitches, materials, boundary treatments)
- Allocate housing sites for development
- Identify Local Green Spaces
- Set higher housing numbers than the Local Plan (cannot set lower)
- Set policies on shop frontages, local services, character

## What it cannot do

- Reduce the housing requirement set by the Local Plan
- Conflict with strategic policies in the Local Plan
- Impose CIL or other infrastructure charges (a separate process)
- Override national planning policy

## Process (simplified)

1. Designation of the area (parish or forum)
2. Consultation: pre-submission (Regulation 14)
3. Submission to Aldershire (Regulation 15) with consultation statement and basic conditions statement
4. Aldershire publishes for further consultation (Regulation 16, 6 weeks)
5. Independent examination (paper or hearing)
6. Examiner's report and required modifications
7. Local referendum (over 50% in favour, with majority in favour)
8. "Making" by Aldershire Council
9. The plan forms part of the development plan

Typical timescale: 3 to 5 years. Aldershire provides up to GBP 10,000 in technical support per plan, including officer time, evidence-base contributions, and venue costs.

## Effect on planning decisions

Once made, a Neighbourhood Plan has the same weight as the Local Plan in deciding applications. Where the two conflict, the most recently made document prevails (subject to certain exceptions for strategic policies).

## CIL share

A "made" Neighbourhood Plan increases the CIL share returned to the parish from 15% to 25%. This funds local infrastructure decided by the parish.

## Reviewing a plan

Plans should be reviewed every 5 to 10 years to keep them up to date. Bramley Heath's 2024 review modernised design policies and added a new Local Green Space designation.
