# Bonfires and smoke from gardens

There is no law that bans garden bonfires outright in England. However, smoke that drifts persistently into a neighbour's home, or smoke from burning unsuitable materials, can be a statutory nuisance.

## Aldershire's policy

The Council asks residents not to have garden bonfires for routine garden waste, because:

- The brown-bin garden waste collection is available across the district (see www.aldershire.gov.uk/garden-waste)
- Free composting bins are available to all residents (collect from Mereford depot)
- The district's three Household Waste Recycling Centres accept garden waste at no charge
- Smoke is a major source of PM2.5 pollution

If you do have a bonfire, please:

- Burn only dry vegetation (not green or wet material that smoulders)
- Light only when wind is gentle and away from neighbours' homes
- Keep it small and burn for no more than 2 hours
- Stay with it until it is completely out
- Avoid weekends, sunny evenings, and any time washing is on neighbouring lines

## What you must NEVER burn

It is an offence under the Environmental Protection Act 1990 to dispose of household waste by burning where this could cause nuisance or harm to health. Specifically, do not burn:

- Plastic (any kind)
- Foam, rubber, tyres
- Paint cans, aerosols
- Treated wood, plywood, MDF, painted wood
- Furniture
- Animal carcasses
- Asbestos cement, vehicle parts, or any item containing oils

Burning these materials releases dioxins, furans, and other persistent organic pollutants. Penalties include a Fixed Penalty Notice of up to £400, or prosecution with an unlimited fine.

## Reporting a bonfire problem

For an active bonfire causing nuisance right now:

- In office hours: 01632 960140
- Out of hours: 01632 960199

For repeated bonfires from the same address:

- Online: www.aldershire.gov.uk/bonfire-complaint

We will:

1. For a first complaint, write to the resident with advice
2. For repeated complaints, visit the property and assess
3. If a statutory nuisance is found, serve an Abatement Notice under section 80 of the Environmental Protection Act 1990

Breach of an Abatement Notice is a criminal offence (£5,000 maximum on summary conviction).

## Commercial waste burning

Trade waste burning (a builder burning offcuts on a domestic site, a garden contractor burning customer hedges, a farmer burning crop residue outside the very narrow exemptions) is a much more serious matter. It can constitute:

- A statutory nuisance (EPA 1990)
- Unlawful waste disposal under the Environmental Permitting Regulations 2016 (offence with unlimited fine and up to 5 years imprisonment)
- A breach of the Waste Incineration Directive

Trade waste burning is investigated jointly with the Environment Agency. Report on 0800 80 70 60 (EA hotline) or to us on 01632 960140.

## Crop and stubble burning

Stubble burning has been banned in England since 1993 except under exemption (small areas of cereal/linseed for plant health, narrow strips for railway/road safety, and specific archaeological purposes). Permitted burns must comply with the Crop Residues (Burning) Regulations 1993 including:

- Notify the local authority and Fire Brigade in advance
- Maintain firebreaks of specified widths
- Stay present throughout

## Bonfire Night

5 November and weekend events around it: organised displays at Mereford Park, Aldershire RFC, and Eastfield Cricket Ground. Garden fireworks and bonfires are not banned but please:

- Tell neighbours in advance
- Check for hedgehogs in piles before lighting
- Move bonfires to a fresh site immediately before lighting
- Keep them small if in a residential garden

## Statutory background

Environmental Protection Act 1990 sections 79 and 80. Clean Air Act 1993 (in Smoke Control Areas, see separate article). Crop Residues (Burning) Regulations 1993. Environmental Permitting Regulations 2016.
