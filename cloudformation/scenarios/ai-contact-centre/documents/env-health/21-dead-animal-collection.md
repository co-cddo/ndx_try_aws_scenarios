# Dead animal collection: wild and domestic

The Council's Streetscene team collects dead animals from public roads, footpaths, and Council-owned land. The service is free for animals on the highway and Council land; charges may apply for collection from private property.

## What we collect

| Animal | Where | Service |
|--------|-------|---------|
| Wildlife (foxes, badgers, deer, birds) | Public road or footpath | Free, within 24 working hours |
| Cats and dogs | Public road | Free, within 4 working hours; microchip scanned |
| Cats and dogs | Private property | £35 collection fee (waived for pet owners on Council Tax Reduction) |
| Livestock (sheep, cattle, horses) | Highway | Forwarded to landowner; safe removal coordinated by Highways |
| Wildlife in your garden | Private | Self-disposal advice (small animals can be double-bagged in general waste; large animals via specialist contractor) |

## How to report

- Online: www.aldershire.gov.uk/dead-animals
- Phone: 01632 960100 (Streetscene)
- Out of hours (animal in road causing hazard): 01632 960199

Tell us:

- Address or what3words location
- Type of animal (and an estimate of size)
- Whether it is on the carriageway, pavement, or verge
- Whether it might be a pet (collar, tag, body condition)
- A photo (via WhatsApp on 07700 900100) is helpful

## What we do with deceased pets

For dogs and cats found on the highway, our priority is to identify and contact the owner. The collection officer will:

- Scan for a microchip (universal scanner)
- Photograph the body and any identifying features (collar, tag)
- Search the council's lost-and-found register for matching reports
- If a microchip match is found, contact the keeper within 24 hours
- Hold the body for 7 days at the Mereford depot to allow identification

If the keeper is identified, the body is held free of charge; we ask the keeper to collect within 7 days. If no identification is possible, the body is sent to a Category 1 animal by-product disposal route.

This service is provided as a kindness; section 27 of the Animal Health Act 1981 imposes a duty only for livestock and notifiable disease cases, not pets.

## Wildlife: when to leave it alone

Some wildlife you should NOT collect or move:

- A live, injured wild animal: call the RSPCA on 0300 1234 999
- A protected species (badger, otter, owl, bat): leave in place and call Aldershire Wildlife Rescue on 01632 960890
- Multiple dead wild birds in the same place (5+): may indicate avian flu; do not touch and call DEFRA on 03459 33 55 77

Most domestic ducks, geese, and chickens are kept (not wild) and the keeper should be identified.

## Roadkill deer

Deer are common on the A329 and B3081. A deer collision involves both an animal welfare and a public safety concern.

If you hit a deer:

1. Make yourself safe; turn on hazards
2. Call 999 if the deer is alive and obstructing the road, or your vehicle is damaged and obstructing
3. Call 101 to report the collision (police log entry)
4. Call us on 01632 960100 for collection

Picking up a roadkill deer is legal (and the meat is edible if treated as venison) provided you did not cause the death. You do not need to report taking the carcass, but be aware that you are liable for any disease risk from improper handling.

## Livestock

Dead farm livestock on a public highway must be moved as soon as possible. The Council does not handle livestock disposal directly:

- Identify the owner through ear tags or in consultation with neighbouring farmers
- Owners are responsible for engaging an approved knacker or category 1 contractor (currently Coopers of Mereford on 01632 960950)

Highways will manage temporary closure or signage where needed.

## Pets at home

If your pet has died at home and you do not want a private cremation arrangement, your vet can dispose of the body. The Council does not provide a domestic pet collection service.

If you cannot afford vet disposal and you do not have garden space for burial, contact the Animal Welfare team on 01632 960140; we can sometimes help on hardship grounds.

## Statutory background

Highways Act 1980 section 130 (cleaning the highway). Animal By-Products (Enforcement) (England) Regulations 2013 (categories). Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 (protected species).
