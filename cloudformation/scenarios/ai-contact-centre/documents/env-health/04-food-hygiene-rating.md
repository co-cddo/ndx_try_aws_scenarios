# Food Hygiene Rating Scheme: stars and re-inspections

The Food Hygiene Rating Scheme (FHRS) is run jointly by the Food Standards Agency and local authorities. Aldershire's Environmental Health team carries out the inspections that produce the ratings displayed on stickers in restaurants, takeaways, cafes, schools, and food shops across the district.

## What the ratings mean

The scheme uses a 6-point scale. The rating reflects the standards found at the most recent inspection.

| Rating | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| 5 | Hygiene standards are very good |
| 4 | Hygiene standards are good |
| 3 | Hygiene standards are generally satisfactory |
| 2 | Some improvement is necessary |
| 1 | Major improvement is necessary |
| 0 | Urgent improvement is necessary |

Ratings are based on three areas: how hygienically the food is handled and prepared; the cleanliness, layout, and condition of the building; and the management of food safety procedures.

You can search ratings at ratings.food.gov.uk or via the Food Standards Agency app.

## How often we inspect

Inspection frequency is based on a risk score, not a fixed timetable. The risk score considers the type of food handled, the customer base (vulnerable groups such as care homes attract more frequent visits), and the standard of compliance found at the last visit.

Typical frequencies:

- Category A (highest risk): every 6 months
- Category B: every 12 months
- Category C: every 18 months
- Category D: every 2 years
- Category E (lowest risk): every 3 years

## Re-inspection after a poor rating

If your business received a rating of 0, 1, 2, or 3, you can request a re-inspection (called a "rescore visit") once you have addressed the issues identified. The fee is £180 and you must:

- Submit a written request describing what has changed
- Wait at least 14 days from the original inspection
- Be ready: the re-inspection visit is unannounced

The new rating, whether higher or lower, replaces the old one. You cannot ask to revert to the old rating.

## Right of appeal

If you disagree with the rating, you have 21 days from receipt of the notification letter to appeal. Appeal in writing to the Food Lead Officer, Environmental Health, Aldershire District Council, Civic Centre, Mereford AL1 4QQ, or by email to food.appeals@aldershire.gov.uk.

## Right to reply

You have a right to publish a "right to reply" of up to 250 words alongside the rating on the FSA website. Submit at www.aldershire.gov.uk/fhrs-reply.

## Display of ratings

Display in England is voluntary (mandatory in Wales and Northern Ireland). The Council strongly encourages display, particularly for ratings of 3 and above. The all-Aldershire average rating in 2025 was 4.6 stars. 87% of premises rated 4 or 5.

## Statutory background

The scheme operates under the Food Safety Act 1990 and Regulation (EC) 852/2004 on the hygiene of foodstuffs (retained EU law). The brand is licensed to local authorities by the FSA.
