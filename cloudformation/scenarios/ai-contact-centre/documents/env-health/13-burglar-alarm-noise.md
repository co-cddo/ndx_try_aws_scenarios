# Burglar alarm noise: keyholders and call-out procedure

A burglar alarm sounding for hours, especially overnight, is a common source of complaint. Aldershire's procedure is set out below.

## Why alarms become a nuisance

Modern audible alarms are usually programmed to ring for 20 minutes and then reset, but older systems can ring continuously until silenced manually. If the keyholder is on holiday, at work, or unaware, the alarm can sound for hours or days.

Police forces no longer routinely respond to unverified alarm activations. Keyholder management is the responsibility of the property owner.

## Reporting an alarm sounding

- Online: www.aldershire.gov.uk/burglar-alarm
- Phone: 01632 960140 (9am-5pm)
- Out of hours: 01632 960199 (after 6pm and weekends)

Give us:

- The full address of the property (vital, alarms in the wrong town are not our problem)
- Roughly when the alarm started
- Whether it is a domestic or commercial property
- Whether you can see signs of break-in (call 999 if you suspect a burglary in progress)
- Any keyholder contact you have

## What we do

For a domestic alarm sounding for more than 60 minutes, or any commercial alarm sounding overnight (10pm-7am), an Environmental Protection Officer will normally attend within 2 hours.

The officer will:

1. Confirm the alarm is actually causing a nuisance to a person of ordinary sensitivity
2. Try to locate a keyholder (looking for a sign on the building, asking neighbours, checking our keyholder database for commercial premises)
3. If keyholder contact is unsuccessful within a reasonable time, take action under powers in the Clean Neighbourhoods and Environment Act 2005

## Powers of entry

Under section 77 of the Clean Neighbourhoods and Environment Act 2005, an authorised officer can:

- Enter the premises (using force if necessary, but reasonable steps must be taken first)
- Silence the alarm
- Charge the property owner for the cost of doing so

A warrant from a magistrate is required if the property is residential and entry must be by force; we can usually obtain this within 2 hours from the duty magistrate.

A locksmith is engaged on contract; typical call-out and reasonable damage is recharged at cost (currently around £180-£450 depending on time of day and lock type).

## Keyholder registration (commercial premises)

Aldershire maintains a voluntary commercial keyholder register. Businesses register the contact details of two keyholders who can attend within 30 minutes. Registration is free and helps avoid forced entry costs. Register at www.aldershire.gov.uk/keyholder-register.

For domestic premises we encourage you to:

- Display a small, discreet notice with a mobile number for a trusted keyholder
- Tell at least one neighbour the alarm code
- Service the alarm annually so it does not falsely trigger

## Police-monitored alarms

If your alarm is monitored by an Alarm Receiving Centre (ARC) and connected to police response (Type A), keyholder details are held by the ARC. False activations can lead to police withdrawing response under the National Police Chiefs' Council policy. Reinstatement requires the alarm system to be re-certified.

## Common false-activation causes

- Wandering pets activating motion sensors
- Spider webs over PIRs
- Loose wiring after building work
- Low backup battery causing intermittent siren
- Open window letting curtain billow

If your alarm has activated twice or more without any cause within a month, have it serviced.

## Statutory background

Clean Neighbourhoods and Environment Act 2005 sections 69-82. Environmental Protection Act 1990 (alarm noise can be a statutory nuisance). Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984 (powers of entry with warrant).
