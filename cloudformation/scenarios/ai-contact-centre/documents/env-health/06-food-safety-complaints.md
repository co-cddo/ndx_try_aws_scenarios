# Food safety complaints: feeling unwell after eating out

If you became ill after eating food from a restaurant, takeaway, cafe, or shop in Aldershire, please report it to Environmental Health. We treat all reports confidentially and investigate where there is a credible link to a specific food business.

## What to tell us

The more detail you can give, the more useful the report. We need:

- Your name, address, and contact number (kept confidential, not shared with the business)
- The name and address of the food business
- The date and time you ate
- What you ate (be as specific as you can; ask others in your party what they ate)
- When symptoms started (incubation periods help us identify the likely organism)
- What symptoms you had (vomiting, diarrhoea, fever, blood in stool, duration)
- Whether others who ate the same food were ill
- Whether you have seen a GP or attended A&E
- Whether you have provided a stool sample to your GP

If you still have any of the food, do not eat it, but keep it refrigerated. We may want to test it.

## How to report

- Online: www.aldershire.gov.uk/food-complaint
- Phone: 01632 960140 (Environmental Health, 9am-5pm Monday to Friday)
- Out of hours: 01632 960199

## What we do

Within 2 working days, a food safety officer will contact you to take a statement. Depending on what you tell us, we may:

- Visit the food business unannounced
- Inspect the food handling, temperature controls, and HACCP records
- Take samples of food, water, or environmental swabs
- Interview the food business operator and staff
- Contact other people who ate at the same business
- Liaise with UKHSA if a notifiable disease is suspected

If we find food safety failings, we can serve a Hygiene Improvement Notice (Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regulations 2013, regulation 8) requiring specific actions within a stated time, or a Hygiene Emergency Prohibition Notice closing the business immediately if there is an imminent risk.

## Common organisms and incubation periods

This helps explain why we ask when symptoms started:

- Staphylococcus aureus toxin: 1-6 hours
- Bacillus cereus (rice): 1-5 hours (vomiting type) or 8-16 hours (diarrhoeal)
- Salmonella: 12-72 hours
- Campylobacter (UK's most common food poisoning, often chicken): 2-5 days
- Listeria: 3-70 days (most cases 3 weeks)
- Norovirus: 12-48 hours
- E. coli O157: 3-4 days (urgent if blood in stool: contact GP same day)

Symptoms starting within 24 hours of eating are more likely to be linked to that meal. Symptoms starting after 5 days could be from a meal earlier in the week.

## What you should do for your own health

- Drink fluids and rest. Most adults recover without treatment in 2-7 days.
- Contact 111 if you have severe pain, blood in stool, signs of dehydration, or symptoms lasting more than 7 days.
- Children under 5, pregnant women, people over 65, and people with weakened immune systems should contact 111 sooner.
- Stay off work or school until 48 hours after the last episode of vomiting or diarrhoea (longer for some food handler roles; your employer or our team can advise).

## Civil claims

The Council does not pursue civil claims on your behalf. If you wish to claim compensation for lost earnings or distress, you may need to instruct a solicitor. We can usually share our investigation findings with your solicitor on request, subject to data protection.

## Statutory background

The Food Safety Act 1990 and the Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regulations 2013 are the main enforcement powers. The Public Health (Control of Disease) Act 1984 covers notifiable diseases.
