# Dangerous dogs: the law and what we do

The Council works alongside Aldershire Constabulary on dangerous dogs. Police generally lead on incidents involving injury; the Council leads on Community Protection Notices, dog control issues, and welfare concerns.

## Banned breeds (section 1, Dangerous Dogs Act 1991)

Five types of dog are banned from being owned, bred, sold, exchanged, abandoned, or allowed in a public place without a lead and muzzle:

- Pit Bull Terrier
- Japanese Tosa
- Dogo Argentino
- Fila Brasileiro
- XL Bully (added February 2024)

Whether a dog is "of type" depends on conformity to a published standard, not pedigree paperwork. An apparent crossbreed can be "of type" if it meets the criteria.

## XL Bully transition (since February 2024)

Existing owners of XL Bully dogs as of January 2024 had to apply for a Certificate of Exemption. The conditions of an exempt dog include:

- Microchipped (free check at Mereford Dogs' Home if needed)
- Neutered (with veterinary certificate)
- Insured (third-party insurance specifically for the dog)
- Kept on lead and muzzled in any public place
- Kept in secure conditions
- Owner over 16 years old
- No transfer of ownership

If you keep an XL Bully without exemption, the dog will be seized and may be destroyed. Owners face up to 6 months imprisonment.

## Dogs dangerously out of control (section 3)

Section 3 of the Dangerous Dogs Act 1991 makes it an offence for any dog (regardless of breed) to be dangerously out of control in any place. This applies in a private garden or in someone's home, not just public places.

Penalties:

- Without injury: up to 6 months imprisonment, unlimited fine, control order, destruction order
- With injury: up to 5 years
- With death: up to 14 years

## Council role

We do not enforce section 1 or section 3 directly (those are police-led). However, we use:

### Community Protection Notices (CPN)

Under the Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014, we can serve a CPN on a dog owner whose dog is causing a persistent nuisance (barking, fouling, intimidating behaviour without an actual attack). A CPN may require:

- Keep the dog on lead in specified areas
- Muzzle the dog in public
- Not let the dog into the front garden unsupervised
- Attend a dog training course

Breach is a Fixed Penalty of £100 or prosecution.

### Public Spaces Protection Orders (PSPO)

Aldershire's PSPO 2023 (renewed to 2029) requires:

- Dogs kept on lead on Mereford High Street and the Riverside Walk at all times
- Dogs kept on lead in all parks between 10am and 6pm
- Dogs banned from the children's play areas (3 in Mereford, 4 in villages)
- Dog fouling cleaned up immediately

Breach is a £100 Fixed Penalty.

### Welfare investigations

Where we suspect the dog is being kept in conditions that cause unnecessary suffering, we work with the RSPCA under the Animal Welfare Act 2006. Reports: 0300 1234 999.

## Reporting a dangerous dog

- Immediate threat or attack: 999
- Dog has bitten someone, no longer on scene: 101 (police non-emergency)
- Persistent intimidating behaviour, no bite yet: 01632 960140 (Council) or 101
- Concerns about welfare: RSPCA 0300 1234 999

## Statutory background

Dangerous Dogs Act 1991 (sections 1, 3, 4, 4A). Dangerous Dogs Exemption Schemes (England and Wales) Order 2015. Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014 sections 43-58. Animal Welfare Act 2006.
