# Caravan sites and holiday parks: licensing

Aldershire licences caravan sites under the Caravan Sites and Control of Development Act 1960. The Council holds the site licence; the site operator delivers the operation.

## Sites in Aldershire

The district has 14 licensed caravan sites:

- 6 holiday touring parks (mainly Cliveden Lakes, Mereford Riverside, Stoneford Park)
- 4 residential mobile home parks (where people live year-round)
- 3 transit-only sites
- 1 council-owned permanent gypsy and traveller site (Bramley Lane)

Together, these have around 850 pitches.

## Holiday park licensing

Holiday parks (touring caravans and static holiday homes used for leisure, not as a main residence) are licensed by the District Council. Licence conditions cover:

- Maximum number of caravans
- Density and spacing (minimum 6 metres between caravans)
- Roads, paths, drainage
- Water supply, sewage disposal, electricity
- Refuse storage and collection
- Fire precautions and emergency vehicle access
- Recreation space
- Site layout plan registered with the Council
- Maximum lengths of stay (typically 11-month season; not year-round occupation)

Annual fee: £550 base + £15 per pitch. Sites with over 100 pitches pay a tiered rate.

## Residential park homes

Residential mobile home sites (where the home is the resident's main address) are licensed under the Mobile Homes Act 1983 (as amended by the Mobile Homes Act 2013). Site operators must:

- Hold a "fit and proper person" registration
- Provide a written agreement to each resident in standard form
- Comply with pitch fee review rules (RPI link, maximum 5%)
- Comply with site rules registered with the Council

Fees: £680 first registration + £210 per year + £18 per pitch. Concession for sites under 5 pitches.

## Park homes "fit and proper person"

Since 1 October 2021, park home site operators must be registered as "fit and proper persons" under the Mobile Homes (Requirement for Manager of Site to be Fit and Proper Person) (England) Regulations 2020. The application includes:

- Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) check
- Confirmation of management training
- Declaration of any criminal convictions or relevant judgements
- Public liability insurance details

We assess every 5 years, or sooner if a complaint is upheld. Refusal of registration can be appealed to the Residential Property Tribunal.

## Site licence inspection

We inspect each licensed site at least:

- Annually for residential parks
- Annually for holiday parks during the season
- After any complaint that suggests a breach of conditions

Inspections cover safety, occupancy, waste, fire precautions, and resident welfare.

## Touring caravan stays in your garden

You can stay in a touring caravan parked on land you own or that you rent for that purpose, for up to 28 days in any 12-month period, without needing a site licence (the "28-day rule" under the Caravan Sites Act 1968). Beyond that, the site needs a licence.

The 28-day rule does NOT cover:

- Static caravans (always need a licence regardless of duration)
- Multiple caravans on the same site
- Long-term occupation in a touring caravan as a residence

If your neighbour has parked a touring caravan on their drive permanently, this is an enforcement matter for the planning team (not necessarily a caravan site licensing issue).

## Gypsy and traveller sites

Bramley Lane is the Council's permanent site, with 14 pitches. Pitches are allocated through Aldershire Housing Choice scheme on the basis of waiting list and local connection. Additional transit pitches are available at three temporary stopover sites managed by Aldershire County Council.

For applications to live at Bramley Lane, contact the Travelling Communities Officer on 01632 960380 or travellers@aldershire.gov.uk.

## Encampments on unauthorised land

Unauthorised encampments on Council-owned land are dealt with via section 77 of the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994. We follow Welfare Assessment guidance: assess needs of children, pregnant people, those with health conditions before any direction to leave. Police may use section 60C/60D powers in serious cases.

For unauthorised encampments on private land, the landowner is responsible. The Council will offer welfare advice if requested.

## Statutory background
