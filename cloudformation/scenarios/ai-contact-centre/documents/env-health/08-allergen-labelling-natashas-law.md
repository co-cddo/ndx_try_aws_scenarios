# Allergen labelling and Natasha's Law

Allergen rules in the UK changed substantially in October 2021 with the introduction of "Natasha's Law", named in memory of Natasha Ednan-Laperouse who died in 2016 after eating a baguette containing sesame that was not labelled. Aldershire's food team enforces these rules and can advise food businesses on compliance.

## The 14 regulated allergens

UK food law requires information on the presence of 14 specified allergens:

1. Celery
2. Cereals containing gluten (wheat, rye, barley, oats and their hybrids)
3. Crustaceans (prawns, crab, lobster)
4. Eggs
5. Fish
6. Lupin
7. Milk
8. Molluscs (mussels, squid, oysters)
9. Mustard
10. Nuts (almond, hazelnut, walnut, cashew, pecan, Brazil, pistachio, macadamia)
11. Peanuts
12. Sesame seeds
13. Soybeans
14. Sulphur dioxide and sulphites at levels above 10mg/kg or 10mg/litre

## Natasha's Law (PPDS food)

Food that is "prepacked for direct sale" (PPDS) must carry on the package itself:

- The full name of the food
- A full ingredient list
- Allergens within that ingredient list emphasised (bold, italics, or different colour)

PPDS is food packaged at the same place it is offered to consumers, before the consumer orders it. Examples:

- A sandwich made in the morning, wrapped in cling film, and sold from a chiller
- A salad bowl pre-made and labelled, sold at a counter
- A boxed slice of cake displayed in a cabinet

Food made to order at the customer's request (a sandwich made fresh on request) is NOT PPDS and follows the non-prepacked rules: allergen information must be available, but not necessarily on a label.

## Non-prepacked food (loose food, restaurant meals)

For food sold loose (restaurant meals, takeaway food made to order, market stalls), allergen information must be available. It can be:

- Written on the menu next to each item
- Provided in an allergen matrix or folder available on request
- Communicated verbally, provided a clear sign tells the customer where to ask

The information must cover all 14 allergens, by dish, and be accurate at the time of service.

## Common compliance failings

Officers most often see:

- Allergen information not updated when a recipe changes (a substitute ingredient introducing a new allergen)
- Cross-contamination risks not declared (use of the same fryer for fish and chips, or shared utensils)
- Staff unable to point a customer to the allergen matrix
- Generic statements like "may contain nuts" used as a blanket disclaimer (this is not enough on its own)
- Online menus showing different information from the in-store menu

## Penalties

Failing to provide accurate allergen information is an offence under regulation 5 of the Food Information Regulations 2014. Maximum penalty on summary conviction is an unlimited fine. Where a customer has an allergic reaction (especially anaphylaxis), prosecution is likely.

## Statutory background

Food Information Regulations 2014 (as amended by the Food Information (Amendment) (England) Regulations 2019). Regulation (EU) 1169/2011 retained in UK law.
