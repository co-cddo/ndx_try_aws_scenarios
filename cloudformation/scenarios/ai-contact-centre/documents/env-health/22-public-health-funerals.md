# Public Health Funerals: when there is no family

When a person dies in Aldershire and there is no relative, friend, or executor able or willing to make funeral arrangements, the Council has a statutory duty to do so. These are sometimes called "Public Health Funerals" or "Section 46 funerals".

## Statutory basis

Section 46 of the Public Health (Control of Disease) Act 1984 places a duty on local authorities to arrange the burial or cremation of any person whose body is lying in their area where it appears no other suitable arrangements have been or will be made.

Aldershire deals with around 25-35 Public Health Funerals each year.

## When do we get involved

Typically:

- A person dies alone at home with no known next of kin
- A person dies in hospital or a care home and family cannot be traced (or are unwilling to act)
- The named executor is unable to act (terminally ill, themselves deceased, lost capacity)
- Family decline involvement (this happens more often than people might think; family estrangement is real)

Hospitals, care homes, the Coroner's Office, or police inform the Council. We also pick up cases through ward councillors, GPs, and housing officers.

## What we provide

The standard Public Health Funeral package includes:

- A simple cremation at Aldershire Crematorium (we do not provide burial unless the deceased had a religious preference recorded that requires it, or family request and contribute)
- Collection of the body from place of death
- A simple coffin (no hardwood or metalwork)
- A short, non-religious or generic religious committal service of 15 minutes
- A funeral director (we use a rota of three local firms)
- A small floral tribute on the coffin

We do not:

- Place obituary notices
- Provide a printed order of service
- Arrange a wake or refreshments
- Pay for headstones or memorial plaques
- Allow more than 6 mourners by arrangement

Mourners are welcome to attend. Friends, neighbours, or distant family who hear about the funeral after we are involved can ask to attend; we facilitate where possible.

## Notification of date

Once the cremation is scheduled, the date and time are published at www.aldershire.gov.uk/public-health-funerals at least 7 days in advance. Anyone with a connection to the deceased can attend.

We send notification to:

- The named GP (where known)
- Any care home where the deceased lived in the past 5 years
- Any family member we are aware of, even if they have declined involvement

## Estate recovery

Section 46 also gives the Council the power to recover its costs from the deceased's estate, if there is one. We always try to recover.

We work with the Government Legal Department (Treasury Solicitor) to identify estate assets:

- Property
- Bank accounts
- Pensions
- Personal effects

If the estate exceeds the funeral cost (currently around £1,800 for a basic cremation), the surplus passes to the Crown as bona vacantia, unless a relative comes forward to claim within 30 years.

## Lost contact with the deceased: family enquiries

If you believe a relative or friend may have died and you want to know if a Public Health Funeral has been arranged, contact:

- Bereavement Services: 01632 960340 (9am-5pm Monday to Friday)
- Email: bereavement@aldershire.gov.uk

We will not share information by phone without verification, but we can confirm whether a funeral has been arranged in a specific name.

## Family wishes

If a family member comes forward before the funeral, we transfer responsibility to them, with reasonable notice. If after the funeral, we provide:

- A copy of the funeral arrangements
- Where the cremated remains were scattered (Aldershire Crematorium Garden of Remembrance, unless otherwise directed)
- An invoice for any costs paid by us (recoverable from the estate if not yet collected)

## Personal effects

We do NOT enter the deceased's home to clear it. That is the duty of the executor, the housing landlord, or the Crown via bona vacantia. The Council holds no personal items.

If you are an executor or family member entering the home for the first time, take photographs first, secure valuable items, and contact the Treasury Solicitor on 020 7210 4700 if there is no will.

## Statutory background

Public Health (Control of Disease) Act 1984 section 46. National Assistance Act 1948 section 46 (now largely superseded but still cited). Cremation (England and Wales) Regulations 2008. Local Authorities' Cemeteries Order 1977.

## What this AI cannot do

The bot can confirm whether a Public Health Funeral has been arranged in a specific name and give the date. It cannot release the body, change the funeral arrangements, or grant access to personal effects.
