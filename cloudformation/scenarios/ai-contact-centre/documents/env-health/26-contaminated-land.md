# Contaminated land: pre-purchase enquiries

If you are buying a property, your conveyancer will normally order an "environmental search" to check for contaminated land risk. The Council provides direct enquiry information for properties in Aldershire under the formal Environmental Information Regulations process.

## What is "contaminated land"

Under section 78A of the Environmental Protection Act 1990, contaminated land is land where substances are present at concentrations that pose a significant possibility of significant harm to human health, ecological systems, or controlled waters. It is a high legal bar; the everyday term "contaminated" is broader than the statutory test.

Aldershire's Contaminated Land Inspection Strategy (latest revision 2023) describes our approach: we focus on high-risk legacy uses (gasworks, landfills, foundries, fuel storage, wartime pollution, dry cleaners, metal plating, sheep dips) and intervene only where the legal test is met.

## What we record

The Council holds:

- A register of officially-declared contaminated land sites (very small in Aldershire: 4 sites currently)
- An informal database of suspected legacy uses (around 1,400 sites in the district, mostly low risk)
- Historical maps from 1872 onwards showing former industrial activity
- Records of past Environmental Permits, landfills (active and closed), fuel storage installations
- Aldershire Coal Board legacy maps (mining occurred in the south-east of the district up to 1962)

This information is mainly used to inform planning decisions and respond to enquiries.

## Pre-purchase enquiries

Conveyancers typically order one of the commercial environmental search products (Landmark, Groundsure, Argyll, Future Climate Info). These cover Aldershire properties and combine our data with national databases. Most domestic transactions stop at the commercial product.

If the commercial search flags a concern, your conveyancer (or you directly) can request more detailed information from us under the Environmental Information Regulations 2004. We respond within 20 working days. Fee: £180 standard, £270 expedited (10 working days).

Request at www.aldershire.gov.uk/contaminated-land-enquiry. Provide:

- Full address with postcode
- Plan or aerial extract showing boundaries
- Reason for enquiry (purchase, planning, due diligence)
- Whether you have already obtained a commercial search

## What we tell you

A standard response includes:

- Whether the property is on the formal contaminated land register (very rare)
- Whether the property is within the buffer zone of any closed landfill (we use a 250m precautionary buffer)
- Whether there is a known historical industrial use on or immediately adjacent to the site
- Whether the property has been redeveloped under a planning condition requiring contaminated land remediation, and if so a copy of the verification report
- Whether the Council has any current investigation or proposed enforcement action

We do NOT tell you:

- Whether your property is "safe" or "free from contamination" (we do not warrant condition)
- Whether to proceed with the purchase
- The condition of land outside the boundary of the property in detail

## Planning and remediation

Most contaminated land in Aldershire is dealt with through the planning system. New developments on potentially contaminated sites get planning conditions requiring:

- A Phase 1 desk study (history)
- A Phase 2 site investigation (boreholes, sampling, analysis)
- A remediation method statement
- A verification report after works

We have around 40-50 such conditions discharged each year. The reports are public documents and available through the planning portal at www.aldershire.gov.uk/planning.

## What if you find contamination after purchase

If you discover contamination after buying:

- Tell your conveyancer immediately; you may have a claim under the seller's enquiries (CPSE forms)
- Consider an environmental insurance claim if you bought a policy at completion
- Contact the Council on 01632 960140 to discuss whether the legal contaminated land test is met
- Consider whether the contamination originates from a neighbour's land (a private nuisance claim may apply)

The Council's first action on declared contaminated land is to identify the original polluter (the "appropriate person Class A"); only if not found do we look at the current owner (Class B). Most domestic purchasers of "Class B" land are not asked to pay for remediation.

## Statutory background

Environmental Protection Act 1990 Part IIA (sections 78A-78YC). Environmental Information Regulations 2004. Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (planning conditions for contaminated land).
