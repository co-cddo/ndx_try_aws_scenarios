# Acupuncture and electrolysis: premises registration

Acupuncture, electrolysis, and other "skin treatment" activities require registration with the Council in the same way as tattooing and body piercing, under the Local Government (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1982. This page covers acupuncture and electrolysis specifically; see the body-piercing article for the general scheme.

## Acupuncture

Acupuncture is the insertion of fine needles into the skin for therapeutic purposes. It includes:

- Traditional Chinese Medicine acupuncture (TCM)
- Western medical acupuncture (often used by physiotherapists and osteopaths)
- Auricular (ear) acupuncture (often offered as part of stop-smoking support)
- Cosmetic facial acupuncture

It does NOT include:

- Acupressure (no needle insertion; not regulated)
- Reiki, reflexology, and other non-needle therapies
- Dry needling carried out by a registered physiotherapist or chiropractor in a clinical setting (these professions are regulated by the HCPC and GCC respectively, who set their own standards)

## Exemption for certain professionals

Acupuncture practitioners who are members of these recognised bodies have a streamlined registration:

- British Acupuncture Council (BAcC)
- Acupuncture Association of Chartered Physiotherapists (AACP)
- British Medical Acupuncture Society (BMAS)

These practitioners must still register their premises but the practitioner registration fee is reduced to £55 with the professional body certificate.

## Electrolysis

Electrolysis is the use of electrical current to remove hair or to treat broken capillaries, skin tags, and similar conditions. Methods include:

- Galvanic electrolysis (DC current)
- Thermolysis (high-frequency AC)
- Blend (combined)

These are regulated. IPL (Intense Pulsed Light) and laser hair removal are NOT regulated under this scheme but are subject to the Cosmetic Practitioners Standards Authority voluntary scheme and local Trading Standards.

## How to register

Same process as for tattooing/piercing:

1. Apply at www.aldershire.gov.uk/skin-piercing-registration
2. Provide ID, training certificates, premises plan, equipment list, and £2m public liability insurance
3. An officer visits before registration

Fees (2026/27):

- Practitioner registration: £165 (£55 if member of recognised acupuncture body)
- Premises registration: £230 first time, £75 annual renewal

## Inspection priorities

For acupuncture and electrolysis, the officer will check:

- Single-use disposable needles (acupuncture needles must be sterile, individually packaged, and never reused)
- Sharps disposal (yellow sharps bins, licensed clinical waste contractor)
- Hand wash facilities separate from treatment area
- Cleanable, impervious treatment table coverings (or single-use paper)
- Disinfection of skin before needle insertion
- Treatment record-keeping
- Aftercare information in writing
- For electrolysis: equipment maintenance schedule, use of fresh needles

## Bylaws

Aldershire's Bylaws (2018) require:

- Minimum age 18 for acupuncture without GP letter (under-18s permitted with letter)
- No acupuncture or electrolysis of pregnant clients in first trimester without GP letter
- No acupuncture of clients on anticoagulants without GP letter
- Single-use needles only
- Treatment records kept for 3 years

## Mobile and home practice

Acupuncturists who treat clients in the client's home (a common practice for older or housebound clients) need a slightly different registration. The "premises" is the practitioner's main place of business; we add a note for mobile work. Standards are the same: needles must be sterile and disposed of safely, sharps bins kept in a locked car not in the boot under direct sun.

Practitioners working from their own home: the home is the premises. We will inspect a defined treatment room.

## Wider regulation

Acupuncture is also regulated as a complementary therapy in healthcare contexts:

- The Care Quality Commission registers some acupuncture clinics (those with prescribers, those treating regulated activities)
- The British Acupuncture Council holds a voluntary professional register
- A complaint about treatment quality (rather than premises hygiene) goes to the relevant professional body

## Health concerns

Pneumothorax (collapsed lung), nerve injury, and bloodstream infection are rare but documented complications. Always check the practitioner uses single-use sterile needles.

If you have a complication, see your GP or 111 immediately. Reports to the Council are useful for future inspections but do not affect your immediate clinical care.

## Cancellation

Tell us within 28 days if you stop trading or move premises. Practitioners returning to practice after more than 3 years' absence must re-register.

## Statutory background
