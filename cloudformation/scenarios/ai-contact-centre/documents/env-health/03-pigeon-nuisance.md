# Pigeon nuisance: humane management

Feral pigeons (Columba livia domestica) are a common feature of Mereford town centre, Aldershire railway station, and the riverside walk. They become a nuisance when they roost in numbers on residential buildings, fouling walkways, balconies, and shop fronts.

## Aldershire's approach

Aldershire follows a humane, proofing-first approach. We do not cull, poison, or sticky-trap pigeons. The council's policy was reviewed in 2024 following representations from local residents and the RSPB. The current approach is:

1. Identify and remove the food source (the single most effective control)
2. Proof the building so pigeons cannot land or nest
3. In limited circumstances, install passive deterrents (bird spikes, wire, netting)
4. In exceptional circumstances on council buildings only, use trained falconers as a humane displacement method

We do not lay anti-coagulant or narcotic baits in any public place.

## Reporting a pigeon problem

For pigeons on a council-owned building, public footpath, or within a public car park, report to:

- Online: www.aldershire.gov.uk/pigeons
- Phone: 01632 960140

For pigeons on a private building (your home, your neighbour's home, a shop), the building owner is responsible for proofing. The council will only intervene if the fouling amounts to a statutory nuisance under section 79 of the Environmental Protection Act 1990, which is a high bar.

## Feeding pigeons

Aldershire's Public Spaces Protection Order 2023 (renewed 2026) prohibits the deliberate feeding of feral pigeons in the town centre conservation area, the riverside walk, and the railway station forecourt. A fixed penalty notice of £100 applies. Persistent breach can be prosecuted in the magistrates' court with a maximum fine of £500.

The PSPO does not prohibit feeding garden birds in your own back garden, even if pigeons sometimes share the food. Use enclosed feeders to discourage pigeons.

## Proofing your home

If pigeons are roosting on your roof, balcony, or window sills:

- Remove any nesting material (during the breeding season this requires a licence from Natural England under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 if eggs or chicks are present)
- Install bird spikes on flat ledges (DIY kits from £20 per metre)
- Install bird netting under eaves and balconies (professional installation typically £150-£600)
- Remove standing water and food scraps from balconies

The Council's environmental health team will provide free advice on proofing methods. Call 01632 960140 to request a callback.

## Health concerns

Pigeon droppings can contain organisms causing psittacosis, cryptococcosis, and histoplasmosis, but the risk to the general public from outdoor exposure is very low. People with weakened immune systems should avoid handling dry droppings without PPE. Always wash hands after contact and do not allow children to play with pigeon droppings.

## Statutory background

Feral pigeons are listed under Schedule 2 of the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981. They may be controlled under a general licence issued by Natural England (currently GL42), but only by, or on behalf of, the landowner, and only where there is a serious public health risk and proofing alternatives have been considered.
