# Loud music and noise abatement notices

Loud music is the most common reason for an Abatement Notice in Aldershire. Around 280 notices were served in the year ending March 2026, of which 76% related to amplified music in domestic premises.

## What "loud music" means in this context

Officers assess music against several factors:

- Whether bass or vocals are clearly audible inside neighbours' closed-window homes
- The time of day (a rule of thumb: music audible inside a closed bedroom after 11pm is likely a nuisance)
- Frequency: a one-off party rarely meets the legal test; weekend-after-weekend events do
- Whether warnings have been issued before
- Whether the responsible person has refused reasonable requests to reduce volume

We do not measure absolute decibel levels in most domestic cases; the test is "nuisance to a person of ordinary sensitivity". We do use sound level meters to support evidence in commercial cases.

## Stage 1: warning letter

The first action is usually a warning letter to the responsible person, summarising the complaint and reminding them of their duty to avoid causing a statutory nuisance. About 60% of cases stop at this stage.

## Stage 2: noise diary and witnessing

If the noise continues, we ask the complainant to keep a diary for 2-4 weeks. An officer may attempt to witness the noise, either during normal hours or via the out-of-hours service (01632 960199, weekend evenings).

We may install a noise monitor in the complainant's property. The kit records audio when triggered above a set threshold. We do NOT record continuously. Recordings are evidential and disclosed to the alleged perpetrator if proceedings are taken.

## Stage 3: Abatement Notice

If we are satisfied a statutory nuisance exists or is likely to recur, we serve an Abatement Notice under section 80 of the Environmental Protection Act 1990. The notice typically requires:

- Cessation of the noise (usually amplified music after a stated time, often 11pm)
- Compliance with specific limits on volume, frequency, or duration
- Within a stated timescale (usually immediate or 7 days)

The recipient has 21 days to appeal to the Magistrates' Court. Grounds include the notice being unreasonable, the noise not being a nuisance, or the time given being insufficient.

## Stage 4: prosecution and seizure

Breach of an Abatement Notice is a criminal offence. We will normally:

- Issue a Fixed Penalty Notice of £110 (paid within 14 days, reduces to £75)
- Or, for repeat or serious breaches, prosecute in the Magistrates' Court
- Maximum fine on conviction: £5,000 for domestic premises, unlimited for commercial
- We can apply for a warrant to enter the property and seize sound equipment

In 2025/26 we seized equipment from 12 properties. Seized equipment can be reclaimed only if the owner pays storage costs and gives an undertaking; otherwise it is destroyed after 28 days.

## Powers under the Noise Act 1996

For night-time noise (11pm-7am) from a dwelling, officers have an additional power under the Noise Act 1996 to issue a Fixed Penalty Notice of £110 on the spot if reasonable level of noise is exceeded (the "permitted level" measured at the complainant's facade).

This is faster than the Abatement Notice route but is limited to nights, dwellings, and music-type noise.

## Tips for parties and events

If you are planning a party, you can:

- Tell your neighbours in advance (most disputes start with no warning)
- Move bass-producing speakers off external walls
- Close windows and doors after 10pm
- Move music indoors before 11pm
- Provide a number neighbours can call before they call us

For events with public ticket sales or live music for crowds over 50, a Temporary Event Notice may be required from the licensing team (separate process; www.aldershire.gov.uk/ten).

## Statutory background

Environmental Protection Act 1990 sections 79-82. Noise Act 1996. Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014.
