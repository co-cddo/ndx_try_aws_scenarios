# Drainage problems: surface water and blocked drains

Drainage in Aldershire involves several different organisations, depending on the type of drain and where it sits. This page explains who does what.

## Public sewers (foul and combined)

Severn Valley Water owns and maintains the public sewer network: the larger pipes that take household waste from your property to the treatment works.

- Reporting blockages or sewage flooding: Severn Valley Water 0800 070 7777 (24/7)
- Suspected pollution from sewage: Environment Agency 0800 80 70 60

Severn Valley Water has free 24/7 emergency response. Do not pay a private contractor for blockages on the public sewer.

## Highways drainage (gullies, surface water on the road)

Aldershire Council's Highways team owns and maintains:

- Road gullies (the metal grilles at the edge of the carriageway)
- Carrier drains under the road
- Soakaways for rural roads
- Highway drainage swales and ditches

To report:

- A blocked gully causing flooding: 01632 960100 (during office hours; after hours 01632 960199 if causing road danger)
- Standing water on the road: same numbers
- A collapsed gully: same numbers

We have a routine cyclical cleaning programme: most gullies are cleaned every 18 months; busy or known-problematic gullies more often. Investigation of a single blocked gully typically attended within 7 working days; if causing flooding to homes, prioritised within 24 hours.

## Private drains (within your boundary)

Anything within your property boundary, before it joins the shared drain or public sewer, is your responsibility:

- The waste pipes from your sinks, baths, and toilets to a drain outside
- The drain in your garden up to the property boundary

For blockages:

- Try a hand auger or sodium hydroxide drain cleaner (follow safety instructions)
- For complex or recurring problems, call a private drainage contractor (CCTV survey from £180; jetting from £150)
- Your home insurance may cover blockages and damage; check your policy before paying

Aldershire does not maintain private drains and does not pay for their unblocking.

## Shared drains (between properties)

Where a drain serves two or more properties before joining the public sewer, it is now (since 2011) the responsibility of Severn Valley Water as part of the public sewer network. Most drains in semi-detached and terraced housing fall into this category.

If you are not sure whether a drain is shared, look for the position of the inspection chamber; if it is on the boundary or carries flow from two or more houses, it is likely a shared drain.

## Surface water flooding

Surface water flooding (water from heavy rain that does not soak away or reach a drain) is a growing concern with more intense rainfall events.

Lead Local Flood Authority for Aldershire is Aldershire County Council. Major surface water flooding is reported to:

- ACC Flood Risk Management: 01632 950800
- Online: www.aldershirecountycouncil.gov.uk/flooding

In an emergency (water entering homes, road impassable): 999 (Fire and Rescue) or 0800 80 70 60 (Environment Agency Floodline).

## Watercourses and ditches

Watercourse maintenance: the riparian owner of land bordering a watercourse is responsible for its flow. This includes streams, brooks, drains in fields, and even some piped sections (called "culverts") under driveways.

Most riparian responsibilities people are unaware of are:

- Keeping a free flow (clearing vegetation, removing debris)
- Not damming or blocking the watercourse
- Not introducing pollution
- Maintaining banks

If you think a riparian owner is failing in their responsibility, contact the Lead Local Flood Authority. The Environment Agency consents work in or near "main rivers" (the larger watercourses); minor work needs ACC consent.

## Septic tanks and small sewage treatment plants

If your property is not on the public sewer (around 8% of Aldershire homes), you have a septic tank or small treatment plant. You must:

- Comply with General Binding Rules 2020 (no discharge to surface water unless specific consent; max 2 cubic metres per day for treatment plants)
- Have it emptied periodically (typically annually) by a licensed contractor
- Repair leaks promptly

The Environment Agency regulates discharges. The Council can become involved if your septic tank causes a statutory nuisance (smell, surface flooding) under the Environmental Protection Act 1990.

## Statutory background

Water Industry Act 1991. Flood and Water Management Act 2010 sections 14-19 (Lead Local Flood Authority). Public Health Act 1936 (drainage). Land Drainage Act 1991 (riparian responsibilities and ordinary watercourses).
