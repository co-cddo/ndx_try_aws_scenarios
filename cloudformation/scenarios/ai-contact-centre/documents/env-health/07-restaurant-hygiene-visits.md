# Restaurant and takeaway hygiene visits: how often and what we look for

Aldershire's food team carries out around 1,200 routine food hygiene inspections each year across approximately 1,100 registered food premises. The frequency for any individual business depends on its risk score.

## How frequency is decided

Each business is scored after every inspection using the FSA's Food Law Code of Practice. Five factors are scored:

1. Type of food and method of handling
2. Method of processing
3. Customer base (vulnerable groups increase risk)
4. Compliance with food safety law
5. Confidence in management and HACCP

Total score determines category A (highest risk) to E (lowest):

| Category | Inspection frequency |
|----------|----------------------|
| A | At least every 6 months |
| B | At least every 12 months |
| C | At least every 18 months |
| D | At least every 2 years |
| E | At least every 3 years (often by alternative means: questionnaire) |

Examples: a school kitchen serving 500 children daily would typically be A or B. A village pub serving cold sandwiches only at lunchtimes might be D.

## What an inspection covers

A typical visit takes 60-180 minutes and covers:

- Premises: walls, floors, drainage, ventilation, pest evidence, lighting
- Equipment: condition, cleaning, maintenance, calibration of probes
- Food handling: cross-contamination, temperature control, cooking, cooling
- HACCP: documented system, training records, monitoring, corrective action
- Allergen management (Natasha's Law compliance)
- Personal hygiene: clothing, hand washing, fitness to work, training
- Traceability: invoices, supplier records, batch codes

The officer will speak to the food business operator (or most senior person on site) and may speak to other staff. We try to inspect at a time when food preparation is happening.

## Outcome

After the visit you receive:

- An inspection letter within 10 working days
- A Food Hygiene Rating Scheme score (0-5)
- A list of any issues, classified as legal contraventions, recommendations for good practice, or matters of concern
- Where appropriate, a Hygiene Improvement Notice with statutory deadlines

## Unannounced inspections

Routine inspections are unannounced. We arrive in officer uniform with a council ID. Refusing access is an offence under section 32 of the Food Safety Act 1990; the maximum penalty is unlimited fine.

We do allow for the principal food handler to finish a critical task (for example, removing food from a hot oven) before starting the inspection. Inspections of premises that are part of a clearly separate residential dwelling will be scheduled at a reasonable time.

## Reactive visits

Outside the routine cycle, we will visit if:

- A food safety complaint is received
- An allergic reaction is reported
- An infectious disease outbreak is suspected
- A change of ownership or substantial change to the menu has occurred
- A previous Hygiene Improvement Notice deadline is approaching

## Officer authorisation

All inspections are carried out by Authorised Officers under section 32 of the Food Safety Act 1990. Officers must be qualified to the level required by the Food Standards Agency Code of Practice. Aldershire has 8 fully qualified food officers and 2 trainees.

## Statutory background

The Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regulations 2013, the Official Controls Regulations (Regulation 2017/625 retained), and the FSA Food Law Code of Practice for England (current version 2024) govern how we carry out inspections.

## What this AI cannot do

The bot can explain inspection frequency and what to expect. It cannot schedule, advance, or delay a specific business inspection; those are determined by the risk-based programme.
