# Dust and odour complaints: assessment process

Dust and odour from industrial, agricultural, or commercial premises can amount to a statutory nuisance. Aldershire investigates around 180 such complaints a year, mostly relating to:

- The Eastside Industrial Estate (food processing, woodworking, vehicle bodyshops)
- Mereford Sewage Treatment Works (managed by Severn Valley Water)
- Brookside Farm (intensive pig unit; ongoing nuisance case)
- Construction dust on the A329 corridor
- The materials recycling facility at Clayton

## Assessment principles

Both dust and odour are assessed using the FIDOL test:

- Frequency: how often does it occur?
- Intensity: how strong is it?
- Duration: how long does each episode last?
- Offensiveness: how unpleasant is the character?
- Location: is the receiving area sensitive (homes, schools, hospitals)?

A single overnight episode rarely meets the legal threshold; recurring exposure does.

## How we investigate

1. You report online (www.aldershire.gov.uk/dust-odour) or by phone (01632 960140)
2. We send a diary pack: 21 days of timed entries with intensity ratings
3. An officer makes site visits during typical episodes
4. For odour, the officer carries out an "odour walk" along the boundary at different times
5. For dust, we may install passive monitors (sticky pads or directional gauges) over 4-12 weeks
6. We obtain process information from the operator

## Types of odour

Different industries produce characteristic odours:

| Source | Typical odour |
|--------|--------------|
| Sewage treatment | Hydrogen sulphide (rotten eggs); ammonia (sharp) |
| Food processing | Cooking, fish, vegetable, sweet/sickly |
| Composting | Earthy, ammoniacal, sometimes sour |
| Rendering | Strong "burnt" or "boiled meat" |
| Pig and poultry farming | Faecal, ammonia |
| Foundry / paint shop | Solvent, burnt rubber |

Identifying the character helps us narrow down sources, especially in industrial estates.

## Outcome

Where we find a statutory nuisance, options include:

- Voluntary improvement plan (preferred; faster than enforcement)
- Abatement Notice under section 80 EPA 1990 (specifying technical requirements such as a biofilter, dust suppression, or process change)
- Permit variation if the site holds an Environmental Permit
- Prosecution for breach (unlimited fine for commercial premises)

For sites holding an Environmental Permit issued by the Environment Agency (large incinerators, large livestock units, large landfills), enforcement is shared with the EA. The Council can serve an Abatement Notice but the EA will normally lead on permit modifications.

## Construction dust

For construction sites, we expect:

- Wheel washes for HGVs leaving the site
- Damping down of haul roads in dry weather
- Sheeted skips
- Dust netting for crushing operations
- A nominated dust monitor on site

Best practice is set out in the IAQM (Institute of Air Quality Management) guidance "Assessment of dust from demolition and construction" (2024 edition). For new development, we may require an IAQM-compliant dust assessment as a planning condition.

## Agricultural odour

Pig and poultry farms over a certain threshold need an Environmental Permit. Smaller units rely on:

- Good Agricultural Practice (Defra Code, latest revision 2025)
- Minimum separation distances from sensitive receptors (typically 400m for pig units)
- Slurry management and incorporation timings

Aldershire has had a long-running case at Brookside Farm where the Council and the operator are working through a court-approved improvement plan. Updates at www.aldershire.gov.uk/brookside.

## Statutory background

Environmental Protection Act 1990 sections 79-82. Environmental Permitting Regulations 2016. Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014 (Community Protection Notices for less industrial scale problems).
