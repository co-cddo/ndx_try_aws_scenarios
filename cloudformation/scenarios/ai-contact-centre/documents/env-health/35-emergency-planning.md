# Emergency planning: flood, fire, and public health

Aldershire's Emergency Planning team works with partner organisations through the South Severn Resilience Forum to plan for, respond to, and recover from major emergencies in the district.

## Hazards Aldershire plans for

The Community Risk Register (latest 2025 revision) identifies the following hazards, in approximate order of likelihood and impact:

1. Severe weather (storm, snow, heatwave, cold snap)
2. Surface water and fluvial flooding
3. Pandemic (influenza, novel respiratory pathogen)
4. Major industrial incident (chemical release at Eastside Industrial Estate)
5. Loss of utilities (electricity, gas, water for >24 hours)
6. Mass casualty incident (rail, motor, public event)
7. Animal disease (foot-and-mouth, avian flu)
8. Wildfire (rare but increasing risk on Aldershire Down and the heathland)
9. Cyber-attack on critical infrastructure
10. Major fire in town centre or care home

## Flood preparation

Aldershire has flood risk along the River Mereford and the Brookside Beck. About 1,400 properties are in Flood Zone 3 (high risk).

Sign up for free flood warnings at:

- Floodline: 0345 988 1188 (24/7)
- Online: www.gov.uk/sign-up-flood-warnings
- Severn Valley Water alerts (sewer flooding): www.svw.co.uk/flood

If your area is at risk:

- Make a personal flood plan (template at www.aldershire.gov.uk/flood-plan)
- Know how to turn off gas, electricity, and water
- Know your nearest rest centre (for major events; we open Mereford Leisure Centre or Eastfield Sports Hall)
- Sign up for AldershireAlert SMS service: text ALERT to 61010
- Consider property-level resilience measures (flood doors, air brick covers; grants of up to £5,000 sometimes available)

In a flood:

- Do not drive through flood water (just 30cm of moving water can sweep a car away)
- Do not walk through flood water (manholes may be open; sewage contamination)
- Move possessions upstairs
- Call 999 if life is in danger
- Call us on 01632 960199 for community support

## Fire

Severn Valley Fire and Rescue Service is the responsible authority for fire response. They lead at any incident with a fire risk; the Council's role is to:

- Provide community support after a fire
- Open rest centres if needed
- Assist with temporary accommodation
- Check for asbestos or contamination concerns after a major fire
- Coordinate the recovery phase

Major fires in Aldershire in the past 5 years include:

- Eastside Mills fire 2022 (warehouse, hours of smoke; air quality monitoring)
- Sycamore Care Home 2024 (8 elderly residents safely evacuated)
- Mereford High Street parade fire 2025 (3 shops, 2 flats above; rest centre opened)

Smoke alarm advice: ensure working alarms on every storey, test monthly, replace every 10 years. Fire and Rescue offer free home fire safety visits to vulnerable residents on 01632 920900.

## Heatwave and cold weather

Temperatures over 30°C cause increased mortality, especially among older adults, people with cardiovascular conditions, and outdoor workers. The Council's Heatwave Plan is activated through the UK Health Security Agency Heat Health Alert system.

When a Heat Health Alert is in force:

- Cooling spaces are opened at libraries, leisure centres, and community centres (locations at www.aldershire.gov.uk/heat)
- Adult Social Care telephones vulnerable residents
- Schools follow their heatwave plan (PE moved indoors, frequent water breaks)
- Council buildings increase ventilation; outdoor working hours adjusted

In cold snaps, the Severe Weather Emergency Protocol (SWEP) opens additional rough sleeper accommodation at the night shelter on Bridge Street.

## Pandemic and public health

The Council's pandemic plan was substantially revised after COVID-19 lessons. Current arrangements include:

- Annual pandemic exercise with NHS Aldershire and the South Severn Resilience Forum
- Stockpile of PPE (refreshed every 12 months)
- Plans to deliver food and medicines to shielded residents (volunteer scheme registered through Aldershire Cares)
- Plans to maintain critical Council services with reduced workforce
- Communication plans across multiple channels (text, web, radio, leaflet)
- Surge mortuary plans (see separate article)

## Industrial incidents

The Eastside Industrial Estate has two COMAH (Control of Major Accident Hazards) sites: a paint manufacturer (Lower Tier) and a chemical wholesaler (Upper Tier). Both have:

- On-site emergency plans
- Off-site emergency plans coordinated with Council, Fire, and Police
- Public information leaflets (delivered every 3 years to residents within the consequence zone)

If you live within the consequence zone (mapped at www.aldershire.gov.uk/comah-sites), you receive instructions on what to do in case of an off-site incident.

## How to help in an emergency

The Aldershire Volunteer Reserve is a registered group of trained volunteers who help with rest centre operation, deliveries to vulnerable residents, and information sharing during emergencies. Sign up at www.aldershire.gov.uk/volunteer-reserve.

In a current emergency, listen to local radio (Severn Valley Radio 96.3 FM), follow @AldershireDC on social media, and check www.aldershire.gov.uk for live updates.

## Statutory background

Civil Contingencies Act 2004 (Category 1 responder duties). Flood and Water Management Act 2010. Public Health (Control of Disease) Act 1984. Control of Major Accident Hazards Regulations 2015. Fire and Rescue Services Act 2004.
