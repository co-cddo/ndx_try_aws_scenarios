# Drinking water: private supplies and water quality complaints

Around 200 properties in rural Aldershire are not connected to the public water supply and rely on a private supply (a borehole, a spring, or a private well). The Council regulates private supplies under the Private Water Supplies (England) Regulations 2016.

## Mains water complaints

For properties on Severn Valley Water mains supply, complaints about water quality, pressure, or supply interruption go directly to the water company:

- Severn Valley Water customer services: 0800 070 7777 (24/7)
- Online: www.svw.co.uk/water-quality

The Drinking Water Inspectorate (DWI) regulates Severn Valley Water and other public supplies. Escalate persistent unresolved complaints to DWI on 0300 068 6400 or dwi.water@defra.gov.uk.

The Council does NOT investigate mains water complaints. We will redirect such calls.

## Private water supplies

If your home, farm, business, or holiday let is supplied by a private source (borehole, spring, private well), the Council inspects and may sample under the 2016 Regulations.

Three categories of private supply:

| Category | Examples | Sampling frequency |
|----------|----------|--------------------|
| Regulation 9 (large or commercial) | Holiday parks, pubs, B&Bs, schools, food businesses | At least annual; full check every 5 years |
| Regulation 10 (multiple homes) | A spring serving 2+ houses on one estate | Every 5 years (more often if risk) |
| Single domestic | One house only on a private supply | On request; Council may decline if not registered |

Charges for sampling are set by the Regulations:

- Risk assessment: from £150
- Sampling and analysis (Reg 9 full): from £680
- Sampling and analysis (Reg 9 check): from £160
- Investigation visit: £180 per officer day

Fees are recharged at cost of analysis. Domestic-only single-property supplies are not charged for the first risk assessment.

## What we test for

Standard parameters under the Regulations include:

- E. coli and enterococci (faecal contamination)
- Total coliforms
- Nitrate, nitrite (agricultural runoff, septic tank failure)
- Lead, copper (pipework corrosion)
- pH (low pH causes plumbing corrosion)
- Conductivity, turbidity, colour
- Specific contaminants where risk indicates (pesticides, hydrocarbons, fluoride)

Results are usually received from the lab within 14-21 days. We send a written report and discuss any failures with the supply operator.

## What if the water is contaminated

If our sampling shows the water fails the regulatory standards, we may:

1. Issue an immediate "boil notice" if there is a microbiological risk
2. Issue a Notice under Regulation 18 requiring investigation and improvement
3. Restrict use (no drinking, may continue for washing) until the supply is fixed

Common fixes include:

- UV treatment (around £1,200-£2,500 installed; runs at low ongoing cost)
- Reverse osmosis (for nitrate or chemical removal; £900-£3,500)
- Replacing the spring intake or borehole headworks
- Repairing or replacing the chlorinator on a multi-property supply

We can grant-fund up to £400 toward improvement works for owner-occupied single domestic supplies, on application.

## New private supply

If you are installing a new private supply (a borehole for a newly-built rural home, a spring for a barn conversion), tell us before commissioning. We can:

- Advise on borehole construction standards
- Issue a Risk Assessment after first sampling
- Set the sampling frequency for ongoing operation

## Drinking water complaints from a registered supply

If your private supply is unsafe to drink:

- Stop drinking it immediately; switch to bottled water for drinking and food prep
- Contact us on 01632 960140
- An officer will sample within 5 working days (urgent within 48 hours)
- If the supply is shared, we will contact the operator and other users
- We may issue a precautionary boil notice while investigating

## Lead in old plumbing

Lead pipes have not been used in plumbing since the 1970s, but lead service pipes (the pipe from the street to the property) are still common in pre-1970 housing. Severn Valley Water replaces the public part free; the private side is the homeowner's responsibility (typical £1,200-£2,500). For pregnant women, infants under 6 months, and children under 6, the Council can advise on a temporary filter or grant.

## Statutory background

Private Water Supplies (England) Regulations 2016. Water Industry Act 1991 (mains supplies). Drinking Water Quality Regulations 2016 (England). Water Supply (Water Quality) Regulations 2018 (the regulator's own remit).
