# Pet cremation: end of life arrangements

The death of a pet is upsetting. Aldershire does not run its own pet crematorium, but the Council can advise on options and works with two licensed local providers.

## Your options

When a pet dies you can:

1. Bury the pet in your own garden
2. Use a pet crematorium (individual or communal cremation)
3. Use the council's clinical waste route via your vet (no return of remains)
4. Bury at a licensed pet cemetery

You can NOT:

- Bury a pet on land you do not own (including allotments, parks, woodland)
- Bury a pet in a public cemetery alongside human remains (one specific exception, see "Companion Burials" below)
- Cremate a pet at home (not safe and not legal in most circumstances)

## Garden burial rules

You can bury a pet in your own garden if:

- You own the property (tenants need landlord written consent)
- The pet died of natural causes or was put to sleep by a vet
- The pet was not treated with cytotoxic drugs in the 24 hours before death (your vet will tell you)
- The grave is at least 1.25m deep (4 feet) and not within 10m of a watercourse or land drain
- The grave is not on land used for food production
- The pet is wrapped in a biodegradable container (cardboard, untreated wood, natural fabric)

Garden burial is a private matter; you do not need a permit.

## Local pet crematoria

The two licensed pet crematoria within 30 miles of Aldershire are:

**Mereford Pet Crematorium**, Stoneford Lane, Mereford. 01632 960620. www.merefordpetcrem.co.uk
- Communal cremation (no return of remains): from £85
- Individual cremation with return of ashes: from £160
- Memorial caskets and scatter urns from £35

**Riverside Pet Memorials**, Bridge Road, Eastfield. 01632 960720. www.riversidepets.co.uk
- Communal cremation: from £80
- Individual cremation: from £155
- Memorial garden plot lease (10 years): from £180

Both providers will collect from your home (£35-£60 fee) and from local vet practices (often included in vet fees).

The Council does not endorse a specific provider and is not responsible for their service. We will check that any provider operates under the Animal By-Products (Enforcement) (England) Regulations 2013.

## Via your vet

Most vets offer a cremation service through one of the providers above. The vet adds a small handling charge (typically £15-£25) and arranges everything. This is the easiest option for many families.

Communal cremation through your vet typically costs £100-£140 inclusive. Individual cremation with return of ashes is £170-£280.

## Pet cemeteries

Riverside Pet Memorials operates a small pet cemetery. Aldershire has no other licensed pet cemetery within the district. The Council has not designated land for pet burial in public cemeteries.

## Companion Burials (very limited)

Aldershire Crematorium offers a "Companion Burial" service: cremated remains of a pet may be buried with a deceased family member's cremated remains in a cremated remains burial plot at Aldershire Crematorium. This is only available where:

- The plot is being newly opened or re-opened for the human cremated remains
- The pet's ashes are in a sealed container
- Family agreement is recorded

Fee: £85 in addition to the human plot fees. Request through Bereavement Services on 01632 960340.

## Disposal as clinical waste

If you do not want the body returned and have no preference about cremation, your vet can arrange disposal as Category 1 animal by-product. This is the cheapest option (typically included in euthanasia fees with no extra charge).

## Bereavement support

Losing a pet can affect mental health, especially for older or isolated owners. Free phone support is available from:

- The Blue Cross Pet Bereavement Service: 0800 096 6606 (8:30am-8:30pm)
- Cruse Bereavement Care (general): 0808 808 1677
- Aldershire Counselling Service self-referral: 01632 960260

## Statutory background

Animal By-Products (Enforcement) (England) Regulations 2013. Animal Welfare Act 2006. Disposal of Pet Animals legislation administered by APHA.
