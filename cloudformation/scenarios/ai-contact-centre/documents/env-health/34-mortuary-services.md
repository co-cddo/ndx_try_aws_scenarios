# Mortuary services: public health interest

Aldershire does not directly run a mortuary, but Environmental Health and Bereavement Services have public health interests in mortuary capacity, infection control, and dignified handling of the deceased.

## Local mortuary provision

The main mortuary serving Aldershire is at Mereford Hospital (Severn Valley NHS Trust), with capacity for around 90 deceased. There are smaller mortuaries at:

- Aldershire Cottage Hospital (12 capacity)
- Eastfield Community Hospital (8 capacity, often closed)
- Two private funeral director-operated facilities (Dignified Departure and Atkin & Sons)
- The Aldershire Crematorium small holding facility (used as overnight transit only, not for long storage)

Total district capacity is around 130 deceased at any time.

## Surge capacity

The Council's Emergency Planning team holds standing arrangements with the NHS Trust and the County Resilience Forum for surge capacity in case of:

- Pandemic flu or other infectious disease
- Mass casualty incident (rail, motor, terrorist)
- Heatwave-related excess deaths
- Major fire

The current surge plan provides for an additional 80 places at a temporary facility in Eastside Industrial Estate (a refrigerated warehouse made available by Skyline Logistics under MoU). This was used during the 2020-22 pandemic.

## Public Health Funeral mortuary access

When the Council arranges a Public Health Funeral (see the separate article), the deceased is held at Mereford Hospital mortuary or one of the funeral director facilities until the funeral. Access for next-of-kin viewing (if family come forward late) is arranged through Bereavement Services on 01632 960340.

## Infectious disease

When a death occurs from a notifiable disease (Public Health (Control of Disease) Act 1984 and the Health Protection (Notification) Regulations 2010), additional handling protocols apply:

- The mortuary is informed in advance
- Specific PPE is used
- Embalming may not be possible
- Open-casket viewings may be discouraged
- Body bags are double-sealed in some cases (for example, suspected viral haemorrhagic fever)

The UKHSA leads on the public health response. The Council Environmental Health team is informed and will advise families and funeral directors.

## Body retrieval and removal

When a person dies at home and the death is expected (under GP care, with palliative arrangements), the funeral director is normally called by the family or the GP to remove the body. This is not a council function.

When death is unexpected, the police attend, then call the Coroner. The Coroner's contracted funeral director removes the body to the appropriate mortuary. The Council has no role.

The Aldershire Coroner is HM Coroner for Aldershire and South Severn, based at the Coroner's Court, Mereford. Coroner's Office: 01632 950900.

## Visiting the deceased

Each mortuary operates its own viewing arrangements:

- Mereford Hospital Bereavement Office: 01632 970090
- Aldershire Cottage Hospital: arrange via Mereford
- Funeral directors: arrange direct
- Aldershire Crematorium: small chapel of rest available, fee £35

The Council does not run a chapel of rest of its own.

## Dignified handling and complaints

The Council has an oversight interest in standards of care of the deceased. If you have concerns about:

- The condition in which a body was returned to a family
- Mix-ups or errors of identification
- Inappropriate behaviour by staff
- Damage to a body during transport

Report to the appropriate body:

- NHS mortuary: PALS at Severn Valley NHS Trust, 01632 970900
- Funeral director: complaint via the National Association of Funeral Directors (NAFD) 0121 711 1343 or the Funeral Standards Council
- Coroner-related: HM Coroner's Office 01632 950900
- Crematorium: Aldershire Bereavement Services 01632 960340

The Council can mediate or investigate where there is a public health concern. Call 01632 960140.

## Mortuary regulation

In England, NHS mortuaries are regulated by the Care Quality Commission and the Human Tissue Authority. Funeral director-run facilities are regulated by the HTA where they hold tissue or perform post-mortem activities; the Council has no formal regulatory role over routine funeral director facilities.

## Statutory background

Public Health (Control of Disease) Act 1984. Health Protection (Notification) Regulations 2010. Coroners and Justice Act 2009 (Coroner's powers). Human Tissue Act 2004. Civil Contingencies Act 2004 (emergency planning).
