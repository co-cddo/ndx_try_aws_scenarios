# Noise nuisance: domestic and commercial

Noise complaints are the largest single category of work for the Environmental Health team, accounting for around 2,800 cases a year in Aldershire. Most relate to neighbour disputes, but we also handle commercial premises (pubs, gyms, clubs) and industrial sites.

## What counts as a statutory nuisance

Section 79 of the Environmental Protection Act 1990 defines a statutory nuisance as noise emitted from premises that is "prejudicial to health or a nuisance". This is a high legal bar; the test depends on:

- Volume and frequency of the noise
- Duration and time of day
- Whether the noise is in a normally quiet area
- The character of the locality
- Whether the noise is part of normal living (children playing, occasional parties)

A single loud party rarely meets the test; recurring late-night music does.

## Time-based assessment

The team uses these working principles, although every case is judged on its facts:

| Time | Threshold |
|------|-----------|
| 7am-7pm | Normal daytime activity is generally tolerated |
| 7pm-11pm | Reasonable enjoyment, but no excessive noise audible inside neighbouring homes |
| 11pm-7am | Sleep period; noise audible inside a closed bedroom is likely to be a nuisance |

Sundays and bank holidays attract slightly tighter expectations, especially for DIY and gardening machinery.

## How we investigate

1. Call us or report online (www.aldershire.gov.uk/noise; 01632 960140; out of hours 01632 960199)
2. We send you a noise diary to record incidents over 14 days
3. We may install noise monitoring equipment in your home (a small recorder that captures evidence when triggered)
4. We send a warning letter to the responsible party
5. If the noise continues, we serve an Abatement Notice under section 80 EPA 1990

## Abatement Notices

An Abatement Notice requires the recipient to stop or restrict the noise. Breach is a criminal offence:

- Domestic premises: maximum fine £5,000
- Commercial premises: unlimited fine
- Right of appeal to the Magistrates' Court within 21 days

We can also seize equipment (PA systems, speakers) under section 81 EPA 1990 and apply to the magistrates' court for an order to permanently confiscate them.

## Out-of-hours noise service

For loud music, parties, and similar disturbances between 7pm and 4am Friday-Saturday, and 9pm-2am Sunday-Thursday, our out-of-hours team can attend the same evening. Call 01632 960199. The officer will witness the noise, speak to the responsible person, and gather evidence for any future Abatement Notice.

## Commercial premises

Pubs, clubs, gyms, takeaways, and industrial premises are held to higher standards because the disturbance is to fund-private profit, not normal living. We expect them to:

- Comply with their premises licence conditions
- Use sound limiters where music is amplified
- Provide adequate ventilation so windows can be kept closed
- Manage smoking areas and customer dispersal

Persistent commercial noise nuisance can lead to a premises licence review at the Licensing Sub-Committee.

## What is not a statutory nuisance

- Noise from highways (use the Highway Authority for road resurfacing complaints)
- Aircraft noise (Department for Transport / CAA)
- Children playing in the street
- Babies crying
- Religious worship and bells from a place of worship (special considerations apply)
- Noise from emergency services responding to incidents

## Mediation

For neighbour disputes that have not yet reached the threshold of statutory nuisance, we offer free mediation through Aldershire Mediation Service. Often a single conversation resolves the issue. Self-refer at www.aldershire.gov.uk/mediation or call 01632 960260.

## Statutory background

Environmental Protection Act 1990 sections 79-82. Noise Act 1996 (night noise specifically). Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014 (Community Protection Notices for less acute cases).
