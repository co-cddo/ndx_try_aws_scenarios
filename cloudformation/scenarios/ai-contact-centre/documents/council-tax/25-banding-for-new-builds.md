# Banding for new build properties: completion notices

When a new home is built or a property is converted into self-contained dwellings, ADC issues a "completion notice" specifying the date from which the property is treated as a dwelling for council tax purposes. The Valuation Office Agency then bands it.

## What a completion notice does

A completion notice is a formal legal document under section 17 of the Local Government Finance Act 1992 and Schedule 4A. It states the date on which the property is "complete" for council tax purposes — usually the date the building inspector signs off, or up to 3 months later for outstanding works (kitchen units, decoration, hard landscaping).

The completion date triggers:
- The property being added to the council tax list
- A council tax band assigned by the VOA (may take 4-12 weeks)
- Council tax becoming payable from that date

## When you receive a notice

Developers, self-builders and converters typically receive a completion notice when the property is substantially complete or is expected to be substantially complete within 3 months. Aldershire issues around 320 completion notices per year, primarily for the new estates at Ashvale (AL3 4QR), Brookmead (AL5 7TG) and Hopkins Park (AL7 2NF).

## Disputing a completion notice

If you think the completion date is wrong (for example, the works are genuinely unfinished), you have 28 days to appeal to the Valuation Tribunal at https://www.valuationtribunal.gov.uk. Reasons might include:
- Major works are still required (not just decoration)
- Services (water, electricity, gas) are not connected
- Building control sign-off is awaited

You should also tell ADC. We can withdraw a notice voluntarily if it has been issued in error.

## Empty period before occupation

There is no general exemption for new builds awaiting their first occupier. Council tax is due from the completion date. If the property is unsold and held by the developer, the developer is liable; from April 2026, second-home and empty premiums apply on the same basis as existing stock (see empty property and second home guides).
