# Severely disabled person: band reduction (disabled band relief)

Households where a member is severely disabled and the property has been adapted to meet their needs may qualify for a band reduction. This is known as "disabled band relief" or "disabled person reduction" and applies under regulation 4 of the Council Tax (Reductions for Disabilities) Regulations 1992.

## What the relief does

The reduction effectively bills you at the band below your actual band. So a Band D property is billed at Band C rates, a Band E at Band D rates, and so on. Properties already in Band A receive a 1/9 reduction (about 11%) on the Band A charge.

For a Band D household in 2026-27, the saving is around £218 per year.

## Qualifying conditions

To qualify, all of the following must be true:
- A member of the household is "substantially and permanently disabled" (long-term, severe condition affecting daily living)
- The property has features used by, and essential or of major importance to, that person
- These features are over and above what would be expected in any home

Qualifying features typically include:
- A second bathroom or kitchen needed because of the disability
- A room used predominantly by the disabled person (therapy room, hospital bed room)
- Sufficient space inside the home for the use of a wheelchair

## Evidence required

Apply at https://www.aldershire.example.gov.uk/counciltax/dpr. We will need:
- Medical evidence (GP letter, consultant report, or copy of PIP/DLA award)
- Description of the qualifying features
- Photographs of the adapted features (or willingness to allow an inspection)

A council inspector may visit to confirm the adaptations.

## Continuity

The relief continues year to year as long as conditions remain the same. Tell us if the disabled person moves out, the home is altered, or the disability significantly improves. We carry out periodic checks every 3 years.

## Combining with other discounts

Disabled band relief can be combined with single person discount, severe mental impairment disregard, and council tax reduction. Some households end up paying nothing.
