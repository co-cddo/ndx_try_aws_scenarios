# Adding or removing names on a council tax account: joint liability

Council tax law sets out who is liable for the bill in section 6 of the Local Government Finance Act 1992. The hierarchy is fixed and cannot be changed by agreement, but the names on the bill can be updated to reflect who actually lives at the property.

## The liability hierarchy

The first person on this list who applies is the liable person:
1. A resident with a freehold interest
2. A resident with a leasehold interest
3. A resident with a statutory tenancy (a tenant)
4. A resident with a licence to occupy
5. A resident
6. The owner (where no one is resident)

Where two or more people share the highest applicable interest (for example, a married couple as joint tenants), they are jointly and severally liable. The bill is in joint names and either person can pay it; we can pursue either for the full amount.

## Adding a name (a partner moves in)

Tell us within 21 days at https://www.aldershire.example.gov.uk/counciltax/move. The new resident becomes jointly liable from their move-in date if they share the same interest in the property (for example, both on the tenancy or both on the title deed).

If they are merely a lodger, they are not jointly liable; you remain solely liable but the single person discount ends.

## Removing a name (a partner moves out)

A separating couple often want one name removed. We do this if the person:
- Has moved out of the property as their main home, and
- No longer has a legal interest (transferred their share, signed off the tenancy)

If both names remain on the title deed or tenancy, both remain jointly liable even if one no longer lives there. To remove a name from the bill in this case, you need a court order or formal deed of transfer. Speak to a solicitor.

## Joint and several liability

Joint liability means we can chase either party for the full debt. We do not split the bill 50:50 between two people. If one person pays, the other is also discharged.

In separation/divorce, this can cause friction. Where one party is genuinely refusing to pay, the other can pay then pursue the difference through the family court. ADC cannot be the arbiter of who should "really" pay.

## Documents we may ask for

- Tenancy agreement showing both/all names
- Title deed or Land Registry entry
- Copy of court order (separation/divorce)
- Proof of new address for the leaving party
