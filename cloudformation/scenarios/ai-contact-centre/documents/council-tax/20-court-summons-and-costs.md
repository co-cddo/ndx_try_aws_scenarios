# Court summons and costs

If council tax remains unpaid after a final notice, ADC applies to the magistrates' court for a liability order. The application is preceded by a summons, which adds costs to your bill.

## What the summons looks like

You receive a printed summons by first-class post. It shows:
- Your full name and address
- The amount of council tax owed
- The court hearing date and time
- The address of Aldershire Magistrates' Court (Court House, AL1 5DD)
- The total costs being claimed (£75)

## £75 court costs

ADC's summons cost is £75. This is added to your account as soon as the summons is issued, regardless of whether you attend the hearing. The costs are calculated to reflect ADC's actual expenses in preparing and serving the summons (Reg 34(7), Council Tax (Administration and Enforcement) Regulations 1992).

If you pay the full amount including the £75 costs before the hearing date, the case is withdrawn and no liability order is granted.

## Hearing dates

Council tax liability hearings at Aldershire Magistrates' Court are held weekly on Thursdays at 10am. Most cases are listed in batches; the actual time you are heard depends on the queue.

You do not have to attend. If you do not attend, the magistrates will hear the council's evidence and decide whether to grant the order. If you have a valid defence (the bill is wrong, you are not the liable person, etc.) you should attend or write to the court in advance.

## Valid defences

The defences available at this stage are limited. They are:
- The bill has not been served
- The amount has been paid (with evidence of payment)
- More than 6 years have passed since the bill was issued
- The named person is not liable (for example, you do not occupy and have no other liability)
- An error in calculation

Disputing your band, claiming you cannot afford to pay, or challenging the council's spending are not valid defences and should be raised separately (see appeals guide).
