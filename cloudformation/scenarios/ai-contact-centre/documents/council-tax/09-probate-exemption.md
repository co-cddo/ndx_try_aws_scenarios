# Probate exemption (Class F)

When a property becomes empty because the sole occupant has died, council tax is exempt for as long as it remains in the deceased's estate, plus six months after grant of probate or letters of administration. This is known as the Class F exemption.

## What is covered

The exemption applies if:
- The deceased was the sole resident of the property
- The property has remained unoccupied since their death
- The estate has not yet been distributed

The exemption continues until probate (or letters of administration) is granted, and for a further six months afterwards. Most estates in Aldershire are settled within this combined period.

## After the six-month grace

Once six months have passed since grant of probate, full council tax becomes payable. The liable person depends on what has happened to the property:
- If the property has been transferred to a beneficiary, that person is liable from the date of transfer
- If the executors are still holding the property pending sale, they are liable in their capacity as executors (paid from the estate)
- If a beneficiary has moved in, normal liability rules apply

## Reporting a death

Tell us as soon as practicable using the bereavement notification form at https://www.aldershire.example.gov.uk/counciltax/bereavement, or call our bereavement officer on 01624 800230. We will pause any active recovery action and recalculate the bill.

You may wish to use the government's Tell Us Once service (https://www.gov.uk/tell-us-once) which notifies us automatically along with HMRC, DWP, DVLA and the passport office.

## Documents we may ask for

- Death certificate (or interim certificate)
- Grant of probate or letters of administration once obtained
- Solicitor's contact details where the estate is in administration

We do not require originals; clear photocopies or photographs sent by email to counciltax@aldershire.example.gov.uk are sufficient.
