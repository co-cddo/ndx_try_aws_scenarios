# Bankruptcy and council tax debt

Council tax is a debt that can be included in a bankruptcy or other formal insolvency procedure. ADC may also petition for bankruptcy where the debt is over £5,000 and other recovery options have failed. This guide explains the rules and the council's approach.

## Council tax in bankruptcy

If you are made bankrupt, council tax owed up to the date of the bankruptcy order is generally included in the bankruptcy and written off when you are discharged (usually 12 months later). This includes:
- Unpaid instalments before the bankruptcy date
- Court costs (£75 summons fees)
- Enforcement agent fees that had been added

Council tax that becomes due after the bankruptcy date is a new debt and must be paid as it falls due.

## Debt Relief Orders (DRO)

A DRO is a less formal alternative to bankruptcy for debts up to £30,000. Council tax can be included. ADC accepts DROs as soon as we receive notification from the Insolvency Service. We pause recovery for the moratorium period (12 months) and write off the included debt at the end if all conditions are met.

## Individual Voluntary Arrangements (IVA)

ADC will engage with IVA proposals where they are reasonable and offer a better return than bankruptcy. We typically vote at IVA creditor meetings via our insolvency service Wilkin Chapman LLP. If you are considering an IVA, ask your insolvency practitioner to contact us early on 01624 800210.

## ADC's bankruptcy petitions

For debts over £5,000 where attachment of earnings, benefit deductions, and enforcement agents have all failed, ADC may petition for your bankruptcy. This is rare in Aldershire (around 8 cases per year). It is a serious step that can include the loss of your home if there is sufficient equity, and we always send a final warning letter giving 21 days to pay or agree an arrangement.
