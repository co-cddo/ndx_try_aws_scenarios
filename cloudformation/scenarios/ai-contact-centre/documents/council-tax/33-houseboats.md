# Houseboats: moorings and council tax

A houseboat used as someone's sole or main residence is a dwelling for council tax. The Valuation Office Agency bands it, usually based on the value of the boat plus the mooring rights.

## When a houseboat is a dwelling

A boat is treated as a dwelling for council tax if:
- It is moored at a fixed location (not actively cruising)
- It has water, electricity and waste connections
- It is used as the sole or main residence of one or more people

In Aldershire, residential houseboats are mostly found at Aldershire Marina (AL3 6QQ), Hopkins Wharf (AL7 4SR) and the Aldershire Canal arm (AL2 8DT).

## Banding

The VOA bands a residential houseboat in the same way as a house. Bands typically come in at A or B because of modest 1991 hypothetical values. The mooring contract and any rights of permanent occupation are factored in.

## Liable person

The owner of the houseboat is normally the liable person, not the marina or canal authority. The mooring fee paid to the marina is separate and excludes council tax.

If you rent a houseboat as your main home, you become liable as a tenant in the same way as any other rental.

## Cruising boats

A boat actively cruising the inland waterways under a Canal & River Trust continuous cruiser licence is generally not a dwelling for council tax. It does not have a fixed mooring address. The boater receives services through their cruising licence rather than council tax.

If you change from continuous cruising to fixed mooring, register with ADC within 21 days at https://www.aldershire.example.gov.uk/counciltax/houseboat.

## Exemptions and discounts

Single person discount, council tax reduction and disabled band relief all apply to houseboats in the normal way. The 50% disabled band relief at Band A is calculated as a 1/9 reduction on the Band A charge.

## Postal address

Houseboats use the marina's postal address followed by the berth number. For example: "Berth 14, Aldershire Marina, Wharf Lane, AL3 6QQ". Council tax bills are addressed accordingly.
