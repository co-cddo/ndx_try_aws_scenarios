# Disregards: people not counted for council tax

Certain people living in a property are "disregarded" when working out how many adults occupy it. If everyone in the property is disregarded, the bill is reduced by 50%. If everyone except one person is disregarded, the bill is reduced by 25% (single person equivalent).

## Common disregard categories

- **Full-time students** (see student exemption guide)
- **Apprentices** earning less than £195 per week and working towards a recognised qualification
- **Youth trainees** under 25 on government-funded training schemes
- **Live-in carers** providing at least 35 hours of care per week to someone who is not a partner or child under 18, where the cared-for person receives a qualifying disability benefit
- **People who are severely mentally impaired** with a doctor's certificate and entitlement to a qualifying benefit (see severely disabled person guide)
- **Members of religious communities** with no income or capital of their own
- **Diplomats** and members of visiting armed forces
- **People in detention** (excluding those detained for non-payment of council tax)
- **Hospital patients and care home residents** whose main home is the institution
- **School and college leavers** aged under 20 between 1 May and 31 October in the year they leave

## How to apply

Most disregards are claimed using a single form at https://www.aldershire.example.gov.uk/counciltax/disregard. You will need supporting evidence appropriate to the category, for example a doctor's certificate for severe mental impairment, an apprenticeship contract, or a letter from a hospital social worker.

Allow 21 working days for processing. We may contact you for further information. If urgent (for example, you are facing court action), mark the application "PRIORITY" and we will aim to decide within 5 working days.

For complex cases, ring the discounts team on 01624 800220, Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm (Wednesday 10am to 5pm), or email counciltax@aldershire.example.gov.uk.
