# Liability orders: what they mean

A liability order is a magistrates' court order that confirms you owe the council tax debt and gives the council legal powers to recover it. It is not a criminal conviction and does not appear on a criminal record, but it does give the council a wide range of recovery options.

## What a liability order allows

Once granted, ADC can use any of these recovery actions:
- Request information from you about your finances (you must reply within 14 days, or risk a £500 fine)
- Make an attachment of earnings order against your wages
- Make a deduction from benefits (Universal Credit, JSA, ESA, IS, Pension Credit)
- Refer the debt to certified enforcement agents (bailiffs)
- Apply for a charging order against your property (debts over £1,000)
- Apply to make you bankrupt (debts over £5,000)
- Apply for committal to prison in extreme cases

## Information request (Form C)

Within days of the liability order, you may receive a "Form C" requesting information about your employer, bank account, benefits, savings and other property. You must reply truthfully within 14 days. Failing to respond, or giving false information, is a criminal offence punishable by a fine of up to £1,000 (£500 standard).

## Enforcement agents (bailiffs)

If we refer your case to enforcement agents, you will receive a "notice of enforcement" giving you 7 clear days to pay or set up a controlled goods agreement. Statutory fees apply at each stage:
- Compliance fee: £75 (added when notice issued)
- Enforcement fee: £235 + 7.5% of debt over £1,500 (added when bailiff visits)
- Sale fee: £110 + 7.5% of debt over £1,500 (added when goods removed)

ADC uses two firms: Bristow & Sutor and Marston. Both are members of CIVEA. Vulnerable households can request reassignment back to the council; ring 01624 800210 to discuss.

## Stopping enforcement

A reasonable payment offer agreed with the council, or with the enforcement agent, will usually pause further action. Ring 01624 800210, or the enforcement agent direct.
