# Houses in multiple occupation (HMO): single household council tax

A house in multiple occupation (HMO) is generally banded as a single dwelling for council tax purposes, with the landlord (not the tenants) liable for the bill. This applies even where the tenants have separate tenancy agreements and lockable rooms.

## Definition for council tax

For council tax, an HMO is defined in section 6(2) of the Local Government Finance Act 1992 (as amended) and the related regulations. A property is an HMO if:
- It was originally built or adapted for multiple households, or
- It is inhabited by people who do not form a single household and pay rent for separate parts

This is a wider definition than the licensing definition under the Housing Act 2004. A property can be an "HMO for council tax" but not an "HMO for licensing", and vice versa.

## Who is liable

The owner of the HMO is the liable person, not the tenants. The owner must include council tax in the rent or recharge it through service charges. The owner's name appears on the bill, and any reminders or recovery action are addressed to the owner.

Most landlords in Aldershire (estimated at 800 HMOs) recover the council tax cost through gross rents, often disclosed transparently to tenants.

## Banding

The whole HMO is banded as a single dwelling, normally at Band C, D or E depending on the size and location. The 1991 valuation tests apply (see bands guide). Individual rooms are not banded separately.

## Exceptions and overlap

If the HMO is occupied entirely by full-time students, the Class N exemption applies (see student exemption guide). The owner pays nothing.

If individual self-contained units in a converted block are sold or let on long leases, they are banded separately and the unit occupiers become liable. The whole-block treatment ends.

## Reporting changes

Tell us within 21 days when an HMO changes status (new tenants, vacant, change of use). Use https://www.aldershire.example.gov.uk/counciltax/hmo or call 01624 800200.

For HMO licensing (separate scheme), see https://www.aldershire.example.gov.uk/housing/licensing.
