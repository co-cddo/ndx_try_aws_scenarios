# Death registration: the 5-day rule

A death in England must normally be registered within 5 days of the death (this includes weekends and bank holidays). Aldershire District Council's Registration Service registers deaths that occur inside the Aldershire registration district. Phone: 01632 960182. Web: aldershire.gov.uk/register-death.

## Where to register

Aldermarket Register Office
Town Hall Annex, 16 Town Hall Square, AL1 1JT

Death registration is also offered at Strawford-on-Sea (Wednesday afternoons only) and at Cooper's Hill (Friday mornings).

If the death occurred in Aldershire but the next of kin lives elsewhere, the bereaved person can attend a register office near them and register by declaration. Aldershire issues the certificate by post within 7 working days at no extra cost.

## Tell Us Once

Aldershire participates in the GOV.UK Tell Us Once service. After registering, the registrar gives the bereaved person a unique reference number that can be used at gov.uk/tell-us-once to inform HMRC, DWP, the Passport Office, the DVLA, and many other departments and benefits in a single step. The registrar can also enter the information directly on the bereaved person's behalf.

## Booking

Bookings are made by calling 01632 960182 or using aldershire.gov.uk/register-death. Death registration is given priority and most bookings are offered within 2 working days. Same-day or next-day appointments are kept open for urgent religious requirements (some faiths require burial within 24 to 48 hours).

The chatbot recognises bereavement intents and treats these calls as priority transfers to a human agent.

## What to bring

The medical certificate of cause of death (now usually issued electronically by the hospital or GP, sometimes called the MCCD) is the primary document. Where a coroner has been involved, the coroner's office sends paperwork directly to the registrar.

The person attending should also bring, where available:

- The deceased's NHS number (often on prescriptions or NHS letters)
- The deceased's birth and marriage / civil partnership certificates
- The deceased's full name (any maiden / former name), date and place of birth, last address, occupation
- The full name and occupation of the deceased's spouse or civil partner, if surviving

Appointments take around 30 minutes.

## Who can register

Death registration can be made by:

- A relative
- Someone present at the death
- The owner / occupier of the building where the death occurred
- The person making arrangements with the funeral director

The registrar also sees the funeral director if the bereaved person prefers, although the registrar must speak to one of the above informants.

## Fees

Registering the death is free. Death certificates are GBP 12.50 each. Most banks, insurers, and probate solicitors require an original certificate. The registrar can advise on how many copies are typical (3 to 6 for an average estate).

## Coroner cases

If the death was sudden, unexplained, or violent, it is referred to the County Coroner. The coroner controls the timeline and the registrar follows their lead. The bot will signpost coroner enquiries to the County Coroner's office on 01632 960199.
