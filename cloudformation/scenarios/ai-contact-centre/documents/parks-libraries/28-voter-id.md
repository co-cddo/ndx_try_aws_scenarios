# Voter ID and the Voter Authority Certificate

Since the Elections Act 2022, voters at polling stations in Great Britain must show photographic ID before being issued a ballot paper. Aldershire District Council operates the policy in line with national guidance. Phone: Electoral Services on 01632 960192. Web: aldershire.gov.uk/voter-id.

## Accepted forms of ID

Any of the following, even if expired, as long as the photo still resembles you:

- UK, EEA, or Commonwealth passport
- UK, EEA, Channel Islands, Isle of Man, or Commonwealth driving licence (full or provisional, photocard or paper with photo)
- Blue Badge
- Older Person's bus pass (issued in any UK nation)
- Disabled Person's bus pass
- Oyster 60+ photocard
- Freedom Pass
- Voter Authority Certificate (VAC)
- Anonymous Elector's Document (for protected electors)
- Biometric residence permit
- Defence identity card (MoD form 90)
- National identity card from the EEA or Commonwealth
- Proof of Age Standards Scheme (PASS) accredited card with hologram

A full and authoritative list is at electoralcommission.org.uk/i-am-a/voter/voter-id.

## Voter Authority Certificate (VAC)

The Voter Authority Certificate is a free photographic ID, issued by your local council, specifically for voting. It is the right option for electors with no other accepted photo ID.

To apply:

1. Online at gov.uk/apply-for-photo-id-voter-authority-certificate (preferred)
2. Paper form from Electoral Services on 01632 960192

The application asks for your National Insurance number and a recent photograph (digital upload, or self-photograph via the gov.uk site). If you do not have a National Insurance number, an alternative attestation route is available.

## Free elector ID for younger people

The VAC is free for everyone, including young people. For under-18s, the VAC also doubles as a useful piece of photo ID outside the polling station, although it is not equivalent to a passport for international travel or right-to-work checks.

There is no separate "youth" elector ID scheme; the VAC is one product available to all eligible electors aged 14 and over (so that 14 and 15 year olds can have it ready for when they reach voting age in elections that allow younger voters).

## Application deadline

To use a VAC at an election, your application must reach Electoral Services by 5pm on the 6th working day before polling day. After that, the only option for voting in person is one of the existing accepted IDs, or the postal / proxy vote routes if applied for in time.

## What if I forget my ID at the polling station?

You will be turned away politely and asked to come back with ID. Polling stations close at 10pm; you can return to the same polling station any time before then with your ID and vote.

## Anonymous Elector's Document (AED)

For voters whose safety would be at risk if their address appeared on the public register (for example, survivors of domestic abuse), the Anonymous Elector's Document is the AED route. Apply via Electoral Services with supporting evidence. The AED is free.

## Help and language support

The chatbot can confirm which IDs are accepted and signpost the VAC application. It cannot upload a photo for you. Council reception at the Town Hall offers in-person help with VAC applications, including a free ID-quality photograph.
