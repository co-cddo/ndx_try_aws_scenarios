# EU citizen voting rights in local elections after Brexit

The Elections Act 2022 changed the rules on which EU citizens can vote and stand in UK local elections. Aldershire District Council follows the national framework and applies the rules to the electoral register. Phone: Electoral Services 01632 960194. Web: aldershire.gov.uk/eu-voting.

## Two categories of EU citizen

Since 7 May 2024 there have been two categories of EU citizen for UK local-election purposes:

1. Qualifying EU citizens: citizens of countries with a bilateral voting-rights treaty with the UK. These countries are Denmark, Luxembourg, Poland, Portugal, and Spain. Citizens of these countries can register, vote, and stand in local elections in England (and Northern Ireland, with separate rules).

2. EU citizens with retained voting rights: EU citizens of any nationality who were:
   - Legally resident in the UK on or before 31 December 2020 (the end of the Brexit transition period), AND
   - Have been continuously resident in the UK since that date

These individuals retain the local voting rights they held before Brexit.

## What can each category do?

Both categories can:

- Vote in local council elections in England
- Vote in parish council elections
- Vote in mayoral and Police and Crime Commissioner elections
- Stand as candidates in local elections (with normal candidate qualification rules)

Neither category can:

- Vote in UK Parliamentary general elections (this remains restricted to British, Irish, and qualifying Commonwealth citizens)
- Vote in UK referendums (subject to the franchise set in each individual referendum's enabling legislation)

## EU citizens NOT in the two categories

EU citizens who arrived in the UK on or after 1 January 2021 and who are not nationals of one of the 5 treaty countries (Denmark, Luxembourg, Poland, Portugal, Spain) cannot vote in any UK elections. They can apply for British citizenship after the standard residence period (usually 5 years; 3 if married to a British citizen).

## How the council confirms eligibility

When an EU citizen registers to vote, gov.uk asks for the country of nationality. Aldershire's electoral system applies a rule:

- If the country is one of the 5 treaty countries, voting rights are immediate.
- If the country is any other EU country, the elector is asked to confirm their pre-31-December-2020 residency, supported by the Home Office data check.

The chatbot can explain whether an EU country is in the treaty list, and whether a date of arrival appears to qualify, but the final eligibility is determined by Electoral Services after checking the Home Office record.

## Standing as a candidate

EU citizens in either category can stand as a candidate in:

- District council elections
- Parish council elections (subject to the standard parish residence / property / work tests; see the Parish Elections article)
- Town council elections

The same disqualifications apply as for British citizen candidates (bankruptcy, recent custodial sentence, etc.).

## What happens at the polling station

EU citizens with voting rights vote in the same way as any other elector: by turning up at their polling station with accepted photo ID, or by post / proxy. There is no different ballot paper.

## What if I think I have been incorrectly registered or removed?

Contact Electoral Services on 01632 960194 or electoral@aldershire.gov.uk. The team will check your record against the Home Office residency data and correct any error within 2 working days. If you remain unsatisfied, an appeal can be made to the County Court within 14 days of the decision letter.

## Future changes

Voting eligibility rules may change with future legislation. The current framework reflects the Elections Act 2022 and the bilateral treaties listed by the FCDO. Aldershire updates this knowledge article whenever Parliament makes a change.
