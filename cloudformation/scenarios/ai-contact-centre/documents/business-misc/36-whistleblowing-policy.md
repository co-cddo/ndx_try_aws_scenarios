# Whistleblowing — protected disclosure routes

Aldershire District Council operates a Whistleblowing Policy that meets the requirements of the Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998 (PIDA), as supplemented by the Employment Rights Act 1996 (sections 43A-43L). The policy applies to council employees, agency workers, contractors, councillors and the public.

## What is a "protected disclosure"

A protected disclosure under PIDA is a disclosure of information that, in the reasonable belief of the person making it, is in the public interest and tends to show one or more of:

- A criminal offence has been, is being, or is likely to be committed
- A failure to comply with a legal obligation
- A miscarriage of justice
- A danger to the health and safety of any individual
- Damage to the environment
- The deliberate concealment of any of the above

PIDA protects workers from detriment (dismissal, demotion, harassment) for making a qualifying disclosure through a recognised channel.

## Recognised channels at Aldershire

We accept protected disclosures through several routes. You should pick the one that gives you most confidence:

### Internally to the council

- Your line manager (least sensitive matters)
- Your service Director
- The Chief Executive (chiefexecutive@aldershire.gov.uk)
- The Monitoring Officer (monitoringofficer@aldershire.gov.uk) — particularly for matters of legality, councillor conduct, or constitutional concern
- The Section 151 Officer — particularly for financial irregularities
- The Audit and Performance Committee (chair via democraticservices@aldershire.gov.uk)

### Externally to a "prescribed person"

PIDA recognises a list of external bodies as "prescribed persons" to whom protected disclosure can be made. Relevant ones for council matters include:

- HM Revenue & Customs (tax matters)
- The Information Commissioner (data protection breaches)
- The Health and Safety Executive (workplace safety)
- The Environment Agency (environmental harm)
- The Charity Commission (concerns about a council-funded charity)
- The District Auditor and the Public Audit Forum (financial irregularity in public bodies)
- The Office of the Police and Crime Commissioner / Police professional standards (police-related)

A full prescribed-person list is at gov.uk/whistleblowing.

### To a councillor

You can disclose to any elected councillor; they are bound by their own code of conduct to take the matter seriously and refer it to the Monitoring Officer.

### Confidential helpline

Aldershire's whistleblowing helpline is operated by an independent third party (Safecall):

- 0800 915 1571 (24/7, free)
- safecall.co.uk/clients/aldershire

You can choose to remain anonymous. Anonymous reports are harder to investigate but we will still review them.

## Protection from detriment

If you suffer any detriment as a result of making a protected disclosure (dismissal, demotion, withdrawal of work, harassment), you have rights under the Employment Rights Act 1996 to bring an Employment Tribunal claim. Compensation can be uncapped for unfair dismissal where the reason was a protected disclosure.

## What we will do

We treat all whistleblowing reports seriously. The receiving officer will acknowledge within 5 working days, scope the investigation, agree communication arrangements (including anonymity where requested), and report findings back where appropriate.

## What this AI cannot do

The bot is not a whistleblowing channel. Disclosures should go through the named routes above, particularly the Safecall confidential helpline. The bot can email the policy and the helpline number.
