# Business rates explained

Business rates (formally non-domestic rates, NNDR) are a tax on most non-domestic properties used for business purposes. Aldershire District Council collects business rates on behalf of central government under the Local Government Finance Act 1988. Receipts are pooled nationally and partly retained locally under the Business Rates Retention Scheme.

## Rateable value

Every non-domestic property has a rateable value (RV), set independently by the Valuation Office Agency (VOA). The RV is broadly the open-market annual rent the property could be let for at the antecedent valuation date. The current rating list took effect on 1 April 2023, with the antecedent valuation date of 1 April 2021.

You can check or challenge your property's RV at gov.uk/correct-your-business-rates. Aldershire District Council does not set the RV and cannot change it.

## How your bill is calculated

Annual bill = rateable value x business rates multiplier, minus any reliefs.

For 2024-25:

- Standard multiplier: 54.6p in the pound (RV £51,000 and above)
- Small business multiplier: 49.9p in the pound (RV under £51,000)

Example: a small shop with RV £18,000 has a baseline bill of £18,000 x 0.499 = £8,982 before reliefs. Many such ratepayers qualify for Small Business Rate Relief (see separate article).

## Who pays

The occupier of the property pays. If the property is empty, the person entitled to possession (typically the leaseholder or freeholder) pays, subject to any empty property exemptions.

## When you pay

Bills are issued in March for the financial year starting 1 April. Default payment is 10 monthly instalments April to January. You can request 12 instalments April to March; phone the business rates team on 01632 960 220 to set this up.
