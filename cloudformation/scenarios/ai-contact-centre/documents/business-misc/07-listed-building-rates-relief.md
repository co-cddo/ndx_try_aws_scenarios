# Listed buildings and business rates

A property that is listed under the Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990 has specific business rates treatment when unoccupied, and may be eligible for rebate considerations under heritage protection rules.

## Listed buildings while empty

Under the Non-Domestic Rating (Unoccupied Property) (England) Regulations 2008, a listed building that is unoccupied is fully exempt from business rates indefinitely while it remains empty. There is no 3 or 6 month limit, unlike for unlisted property.

This recognises the difficulty of finding tenants for listed premises that may need substantial works subject to listed building consent.

## Listed buildings while occupied

There is no separate rate relief for occupied listed buildings. Standard reliefs apply (Small Business Rate Relief, Charity Relief, Retail/Hospitality/Leisure discount, etc.) on the same basis as for unlisted property.

In Aldershire, occupied listed buildings include the Town Hall (Grade II*), the Aldermarket Corn Exchange (Grade II), the Old Workhouse on Mill Road (now a community arts centre, Grade II), and approximately 240 listed shop and pub premises across the district, mostly in the Aldermarket Conservation Area.

## Telling us about a vacated listed building

When a listed building becomes empty, you should tell us:

- Date the previous tenant vacated
- Whether the building is being marketed for re-letting
- Any proposed change of use
- Any application for listed building consent

This helps us apply the indefinite exemption correctly, and helps the planning team if you intend to apply for listed building consent for adaptations.

## Heritage at risk

If your property is on the Historic England Heritage at Risk Register, the council's Heritage Officer (planning@aldershire.gov.uk) may be able to advise on grant funding routes. Aldershire District Council does not directly fund private heritage works, but we signpost to the Architectural Heritage Fund, the National Lottery Heritage Fund, and Historic England's repair grant programmes.
