# Trading standards — counterfeit goods

Aldershire's trading standards function is delivered jointly with Aldershire County Council under a shared-service arrangement. The team operates from the Town Hall annexe and covers the whole county. They enforce a wide range of consumer protection legislation including the Trade Marks Act 1994, the Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988, the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008, and the Tobacco and Related Products Regulations 2016.

## What counts as counterfeit

Counterfeit goods are unauthorised reproductions sold as if they were genuine branded products. Common in Aldershire markets and online listings:

- Designer clothing, handbags, watches
- Football, cricket and rugby shirts
- Fake fragrances and cosmetics (these can be physically dangerous — adulterants, allergens)
- Counterfeit electrical goods (chargers, batteries — fire risk)
- Counterfeit alcohol (adulterated with methanol — life-threatening)
- Counterfeit tobacco (no excise duty paid, often higher tar/nicotine, no health warnings)
- Pirated DVDs, software, games

## How to report

If you suspect counterfeit goods are being sold in Aldershire, report to:

- Citizens Advice consumer helpline: 0808 223 1133 (free). They triage and pass details to trading standards.
- Online: gov.uk/consumer-protection-rights — links to the same triage process
- Direct to Aldershire trading standards: 01632 960 280, tradingstandards@aldershire.gov.uk
- Anonymously via Crimestoppers: 0800 555 111

You will be asked: what was being sold, where, when, by whom (description if not name), and any evidence (receipts, photos, packaging — do not retain dangerous items).

## What happens next

The trading standards team reviews intelligence weekly. Cases proceeding to investigation may involve:

- Test purchases under the Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984
- Powers of entry under section 28 of the Consumer Rights Act 2015
- Seizure of goods
- Prosecution (typically Magistrates' Court; serious cases sent to Crown Court)
- Confiscation of proceeds under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002

Sentences for counterfeiting can include unlimited fines and up to 10 years' imprisonment.

## If you have bought counterfeit goods

You may be entitled to a refund from the seller under the Consumer Rights Act 2015. If the seller refuses, contact Citizens Advice. You should not knowingly resell counterfeit goods — this is itself an offence.
