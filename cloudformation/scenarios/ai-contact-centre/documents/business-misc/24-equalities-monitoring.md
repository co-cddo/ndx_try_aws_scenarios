# Equalities, monitoring, and the Public Sector Equality Duty

Aldershire District Council has duties under the Equality Act 2010, in particular the Public Sector Equality Duty (PSED) at section 149. The PSED requires us, in the exercise of our functions, to have due regard to the need to:

- Eliminate discrimination, harassment, victimisation and other prohibited conduct
- Advance equality of opportunity between people who share a relevant protected characteristic and people who do not
- Foster good relations between people who share a relevant protected characteristic and people who do not

The protected characteristics are: age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage and civil partnership, pregnancy and maternity, race, religion or belief, sex, and sexual orientation.

## What this means in practice

For every significant decision we make — adopting a policy, setting a budget, redesigning a service, restructuring staff — we complete an Equality Impact Assessment (EIA). The EIA asks: who is affected, by which protected characteristic, with what evidence, and how can we mitigate any disproportionate impact?

EIAs are publicly available alongside the Cabinet or committee report that proposed the decision. You can find them at aldershire.gov.uk/equalities/eias.

## Equality Objectives

Under the Equality Act 2010 (Specific Duties and Public Authorities) Regulations 2017, we publish equality objectives every four years. The current objectives (2023-2027) are:

1. Reduce the disability employment gap among our own workforce by 5 percentage points
2. Increase satisfaction with council services among our Black, Asian and minority ethnic residents to within 2 points of the all-resident average
3. Embed reasonable adjustment requests as a standard feature of our customer-facing digital services
4. Improve hate-incident reporting in partnership with Aldershire Constabulary

We report progress annually to Cabinet in March.

## Equalities monitoring data

When you contact us, you may be asked to share information on protected characteristics for monitoring purposes. This is voluntary — you can decline. The data is held separately from your service record and used only to:

- Report aggregate (anonymised) statistics on who uses our services
- Identify any disproportionate access or outcome differences
- Inform service redesign

We comply with UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018; sensitive data on race, religion, sexual orientation, health, etc. is processed under the substantial public interest condition (statistical and equality monitoring).

## Reporting discrimination

If you have experienced discrimination by a council service, raise it through our complaints procedure (Stage 1 / Stage 2 / LGSCO — see separate article). For hate incidents and hate crime, report to:

- Police 999 (emergency) or 101
- Hate Incident Reporting via Stop Hate UK: 0800 138 1625
- Aldershire Constabulary's hate-crime liaison officer through 101
