# Councillor surgeries — finding your ward member

Aldershire District Council has 51 councillors representing 23 wards. Each ward has 1, 2 or 3 councillors depending on its electorate. Councillors are independent of the council's officer structure and are accountable directly to their constituents.

## Finding your councillor

- Online lookup: aldershire.gov.uk/your-councillor — enter your postcode
- Phone Democratic Services: 01632 960 230
- All councillors with names, parties, ward, contact details, register of interests, allowance details: aldershire.gov.uk/councillors

You can contact your councillor at any time by email or, where they have published one, phone. Most councillors aim to respond within 5 working days.

## Surgeries

A surgery is an in-person, walk-in opportunity to speak to your councillor about any local matter. No appointment is needed (though some surgeries take bookings during peak demand). Surgeries are open to anyone living, working or studying in the ward; you do not need to be on the electoral register.

Aldershire surgery locations include:

- Aldermarket Town Centre: 1st Saturday of the month, 10:00-12:00, Council Reception lobby — multiple wards, on rotation
- Vale Road Library: 3rd Tuesday of the month, 17:30-19:00 — Vale, Vale East, Vale West wards
- Mill Lane Community Centre: 2nd Wednesday, 18:00-19:30 — Mill, Mill North wards
- Ridge Park Pavilion: 4th Saturday, 11:00-13:00 — Ridge, Ridge South, Aldershire Vale wards
- Outlying parishes: variable, typically rotating between parish halls — check your councillor's published schedule

The full surgery calendar is published at aldershire.gov.uk/your-councillor/surgeries.

## What you can ask about

Anything where the council has a role — bins, planning, housing, council tax, environmental health, parks, leisure, anti-social behaviour, licensing, fly-tipping, public footpaths in the council's care, parish matters within the council's powers.

Out-of-scope for the district council (but a councillor will signpost): potholes (county), schools (county), social care (county), policing (Constabulary), NHS, benefits other than housing benefit / council tax reduction.

## What councillors will not do

- Give legal advice
- Make decisions about specific planning applications outside committee (avoid prejudicing the application)
- Override officer decisions where the law gives officers a delegated power

## Casework data

Casework you raise with a councillor is held by them and shared with relevant council officers as needed to resolve it. Councillors are themselves data controllers under UK GDPR for their casework. Each councillor publishes their privacy notice on their council page.

## What this AI cannot do

The bot does not contact your councillor on your behalf in real time. It can identify your councillor by postcode and email you the surgery schedule and councillor's contact details.
