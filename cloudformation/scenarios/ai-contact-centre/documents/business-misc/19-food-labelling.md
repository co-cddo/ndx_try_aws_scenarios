# Food labelling and information

Food labelling is co-enforced in Aldershire by trading standards (composition, false claims, country of origin, weights) and environmental health (allergens, hygiene-related labelling, hot food). The two teams work closely; if you contact one, they will route you to the other where appropriate.

## What labels must show

Pre-packed food sold in Aldershire must carry, at minimum:

- The food name (a recognised legal name where one exists)
- A list of ingredients in descending order of weight
- Allergens emphasised in the ingredient list (Food Information Regulations 2014)
- Net quantity
- A "use by" date for highly perishable food, or a "best before" date for less perishable food
- Storage conditions and instructions for use
- Name and address of the food business operator
- Country of origin where omitting it would mislead
- Nutritional information (per 100g/ml, with energy in kJ and kcal)
- Alcoholic strength (where applicable)

## "Pre-packed for direct sale" (PPDS) — Natasha's Law

Since 1 October 2021, food prepared and packaged on the same premises from which it is sold (e.g. a sandwich made and packed by a deli, then sold from the same shop) must carry full ingredient and allergen labelling. This is known as PPDS labelling, introduced in response to the death of Natasha Ednan-Laperouse and brought into force by the Food Information (Amendment) (England) Regulations 2019.

Aldershire environmental health visited 312 food businesses in 2024 to check PPDS compliance; 78% were fully compliant, 19% had minor labelling issues now resolved, 3% required formal action.

## Allergen-free claims

If a business claims a food is "free from" a particular allergen (e.g. "gluten free", "dairy free", "nut free"), they must be able to demonstrate that the claim is accurate. Cross-contamination in a kitchen is a frequent cause of failed claims; "may contain" warnings should not be used as a substitute for proper allergen management.

## Country of origin

Origin labelling is mandatory for fresh meat, eggs, honey, fish, fruit, vegetables, olive oil, and a few other categories. For prepared foods, origin must be stated where omitting it would mislead — for instance, a "Cornish pasty" not made in Cornwall.

## Reporting concerns

- Mislabelled food (composition, origin, weight): trading standards 01632 960 280
- Hygiene-related labelling, "use by" date breaches, undeclared allergens: environmental health 01632 960 290
- Allergic reaction following an undeclared allergen: report to environmental health and seek medical advice immediately (call 999 if anaphylaxis)
