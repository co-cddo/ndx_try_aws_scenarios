# Volunteering opportunities with Aldershire District Council

Aldershire District Council offers a wide range of volunteering opportunities to residents, students and businesses (corporate volunteering days). Volunteers do not replace paid staff — they extend what we can do for our parks, libraries, museums, environmental health and community work.

## Current volunteering opportunities

### Parks and open spaces

- Aldermarket Park "Friends Group" — practical conservation, monthly Saturdays
- Mill Hill Park ranger volunteers — wildlife monitoring, footpath maintenance
- Tree wardens — surveying tree health, advocacy on planning, planting community trees
- Aldermarket Allotments stewards — supporting plot holders, site upkeep

### Libraries

- Reading volunteers (Aldermarket Central, Vale Road, Mill Lane libraries) — supporting reading groups for adults with literacy needs
- Home Library Service drivers — taking books to housebound residents
- IT-help volunteers — running drop-in sessions for older residents getting started online

### Aldermarket Museum

- Front-of-house welcome volunteers
- Object handling for school visits (training provided)
- Local history research — cataloguing the photographic archive

### Environmental

- Litter-pick coordinators (we provide equipment, you organise the local picks)
- River wardens for the River Alder — water quality, invasive species reporting
- Hedgehog highway volunteers — coordinating residents to cut hedgehog gaps in fence panels

### Civic and community

- Aldershire Sounding Board — paid resident panel of 50, 4 hours a month, £15/hour for usability testing of council services
- Census support volunteers (next census 2031, recruitment from late 2030)
- Coronavirus-style mutual aid contacts (held on standby for civil emergencies)

## How to apply

- Online: aldershire.gov.uk/volunteer
- Email: volunteer@aldershire.gov.uk
- Phone: 01632 960 200, ask for the volunteering coordinator
- In partnership with Aldershire Voluntary Action (the local Council for Voluntary Service): aldershirevolaction.org.uk

For most roles we ask for: contact details, availability, areas of interest, references, and (for volunteer roles working with children or vulnerable adults) a Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) check, which we pay for.

## What you get

- Training relevant to the role
- Out-of-pocket expenses (travel, parking)
- A regular point of contact within the council
- An annual Volunteer Recognition event in the Mayor's calendar
- For longer-term volunteers, a signed reference for employment or further volunteering

Volunteers are covered by the council's public liability and personal accident insurance while undertaking authorised volunteer activity.
