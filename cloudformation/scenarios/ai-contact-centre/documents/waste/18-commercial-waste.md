# Commercial waste

## Why businesses cannot use household bins

Section 34 of the Environmental Protection Act 1990 places a "duty of care" on businesses to ensure their waste is managed by a licensed carrier. Putting commercial waste in household bins is unlawful and may be prosecuted under the Controlled Waste (England and Wales) Regulations 2012. This includes "side waste" left next to a household bin by tradespeople.

## Aldershire commercial waste service

The council operates an in-house trade waste service, branded "Aldershire Trade", competing with private contractors. We collect:

- General refuse (1100L Eurobins or 240L wheelie bins)
- Mixed recycling (paper, card, plastic, cans, glass)
- Glass-only bins for hospitality
- Food waste (mandatory from March 2025 for all premises producing >5kg per week)

Contracts run for 12 months minimum. Pricing is per-lift plus a quarterly standing charge.

| Container | Standing charge | Per lift |
| --------- | --------------- | -------- |
| 240L wheelie | £25/quarter | £6 |
| 1100L Eurobin | £75/quarter | £18 |
| 660L glass | £40/quarter | £12 |
| 240L food | £25/quarter | £8 |

## How to sign up

Email trade@aldershire.example.gov.uk or call 01432 556 290 (Mon-Fri 09:00-17:00). A site survey is arranged within 10 working days. Bins are delivered after contract signing and a 30-day notice period for any change of service.

## Duty of care documentation

Customers receive a Waste Transfer Note quarterly, covering all collections. The note must be retained for two years and presented on request to enforcement officers.

## Small businesses

Cafes, salons, and one-person trades operating from home commercial premises typically choose the 240L wheelie option. The council also operates a "minimum lift" tier (£12 per ad-hoc collection) for very small generators.
