# Surplus food donation

## The hierarchy

Aldershire follows the food and drink waste hierarchy:

1. Prevent surplus
2. Redistribute to people
3. Feed animals (only via licensed routes)
4. Process by anaerobic digestion (food caddy stream)
5. Compost
6. Recover energy
7. Dispose to landfill (last resort)

The council promotes redistribution wherever it is safe and practical.

## What can be donated

Sealed packaged food in date, fresh fruit and vegetables, sealed dairy in date, sealed eggs in date, breads and bakery items (within use-by). Allergen labels must be visible.

What cannot: home-cooked food (food safety), food past use-by, leftovers from events, food without packaging or label.

## Food businesses

Cafes, supermarkets, and caterers can register as donor partners. The council holds an Approved Donor list and supports compliance with the Food Safety Act 1990 and Hygiene of Foodstuffs Regulation 852/2004 retained law.

The lead partner for retail surplus is FareShare Aldershire Region, which works with Tesco Aldermarket, Sainsbury's Aldermarket, Lidl Wendlewood, and the Vale Co-op. In 2025 the partnership redistributed 38 tonnes of surplus food, equivalent to 90,000 meals.

## Volunteering

Volunteers run the fridges, sort donations, and deliver to those unable to visit. Sign up at aldershire.example.gov.uk/community-fridge-volunteer. Hours are flexible; minimum commitment is 2 hours per week.

## Hygiene

All community fridge volunteers complete a Level 2 Food Hygiene course (council-funded). Fridges are inspected quarterly by Aldershire Environmental Health (see 05-environmental-health.md).
