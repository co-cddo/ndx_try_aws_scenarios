# Fly tipping: reporting and prosecution

## What is fly tipping

The illegal disposal of waste on land not licensed to receive it. Fly tipping is a criminal offence under section 33 of the Environmental Protection Act 1990. Maximum penalties on indictment are an unlimited fine and up to five years' imprisonment.

## How to report

Online at aldershire.example.gov.uk/fly-tipping or by phone on 01432 556 250 (Mon-Fri 08:00-17:30). Out-of-hours reports go to the duty officer via the main switchboard 01432 556 100.

When reporting, please provide:

1. Exact location (postcode, what3words, or grid reference if rural)
2. Description of the waste (size of pile, type of materials)
3. Whether it is blocking a road or footpath
4. Photographs (helpful for evidence)
5. Any witnessed details: vehicle make, colour, registration, time, occupants

Do not touch or move the waste; it may contain sharp, contaminated, or hazardous items.

## Response times

| Severity | Response |
| -------- | -------- |
| Hazardous (fuel, asbestos, clinical waste) | Same day make-safe |
| Highway-blocking | Within 24 hours |
| Public open space | Within 3 working days |
| Rural verge | Within 5 working days |

## Investigation and prosecution

Aldershire's Enforcement Team investigates around 1,200 fly-tipping cases a year. Officers search waste for identifying evidence (letters, parcel labels). Where occupiers can be identified, the team interviews under caution under the Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984. In 2025 the council secured 28 successful prosecutions and issued 412 fixed penalty notices (£400 each).

## Land ownership

The council clears fly tipping on public highway, council-owned land, and the public estate. Private landowners are responsible for clearing on their own land but should still report so the council can pursue offenders.
