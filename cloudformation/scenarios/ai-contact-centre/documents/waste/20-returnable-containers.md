# Returning bins to your property after collection

## Why bin presentation matters

Bins left on the public footway after collection are an obstruction. The Highways Act 1980 (sections 137 and 161) makes it an offence to obstruct the highway or deposit anything on it without lawful authority. Aldershire takes a pragmatic, education-first approach but can issue Community Protection Notices for repeated obstruction.

## When to bring bins back in

- Standard rule: by 21:00 on collection day.
- Assisted collections: crews return the bin to its agreed location.
- Communal blocks: managing agent must return bins to store within two hours of collection.

## Storage

When not at the kerb, bins should be kept on private property (driveway, side return, garden). They should not block public footways, ramps, dropped kerbs, or visibility splays at junctions. See also 34-bin-storage-rules.md.

## Common issues

| Issue | Action |
| ----- | ------ |
| Bin blown into road | Aldershire will collect and tag the bin; resident notified by letter |
| Bin left on highway >24 hours | First contact: education letter. Repeat: Community Protection Warning. Persistent: £100 fixed penalty |
| Bin on neighbour's property | Politely return; council can mediate if needed |
| Bin damaged by passing vehicle | Report on 01432 556 200 with photo and (if known) vehicle details |

## Lost wheels and lids

A bin left on the kerb for several days is more likely to be vandalised, blown over, or have its wheel snapped. Free wheel and hinge repairs are available; see 14-replacement-bins.md and 33-faulty-wheel.md.

## Wheelchair users and limited mobility

Residents who physically cannot return bins to private property qualify for assisted collection. Apply on 01432 556 200; see 15-extra-capacity-and-assisted.md.

## Dead-end roads and shared driveways

In private dead-end roads or shared drives, the council requires a single agreed presentation point at the public highway boundary. Crews will not enter private roads.
