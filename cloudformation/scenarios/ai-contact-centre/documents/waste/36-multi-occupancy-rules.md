# Multi-occupancy households: who pays, who provides

## Definitions

- **House in multiple occupation (HMO)**. A property let to three or more tenants forming two or more households who share facilities (kitchen, bathroom).
- **Large HMO**. Five or more tenants, two or more households. Mandatory licensing applies under Part 2 of the Housing Act 2004.
- **Bedsit-style block**. Self-contained units, each with their own facilities, in a single building.

## Bin entitlement

| Property type | Standard provision |
| ------------- | ------------------ |
| Single household HMO (3-4 sharers) | 1 brown 240L, 1 blue 240L, 1 food caddy. Optional green bin (paid). |
| Large HMO (5-9 sharers) | 1 brown 360L, 1 blue 360L, 1 food caddy. Reviewed annually. |
| Large HMO (10+ sharers) | 1100L Eurobins (refuse and recycling), 23L food caddies x 2 |
| Bedsit-style block | Per-unit caddies + shared communal bins |

The council carries out a Waste Storage Survey for every HMO licence application or renewal, ensuring storage matches expected occupancy.

## Who is liable

The landlord or managing agent is responsible for:

- Providing adequate bin storage on the property (under HMO licence conditions)
- Ensuring bins are presented and returned on collection days where the property layout requires it
- Maintaining cleanliness of communal stores
- Replacing damaged or stolen bins (treated as the landlord's chargeable account)

Tenants are responsible for:

- Correct segregation of waste into the right bins
- Not creating side waste or fly-tipping in communal stores
- Reporting damage or thefts to the landlord/agent

## Council Tax

For HMOs, council tax liability often sits with the landlord (regardless of who pays bills). Where multiple band-D liabilities apply (e.g. self-contained units with separate banding), tenants in those units are individually liable.

## Disputes between tenants and landlords

If a landlord is not providing adequate bins or is not maintaining storage, the council can serve enforcement notices under the Housing Act 2004 (HMO licence conditions) and section 46 EPA 1990. Tenants can report concerns confidentially to the Private Sector Housing team on 01432 556 270.

## Check the licence

Active HMO licences are listed at aldershire.example.gov.uk/hmo-register. Operating an unlicensed mandatory-licence HMO carries a fine of up to £30,000.
