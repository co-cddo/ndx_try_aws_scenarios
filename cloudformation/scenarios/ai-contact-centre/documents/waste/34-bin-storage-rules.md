# Bin storage: keeping bins out of sight when not collection day

## The principle

Wheelie bins permanently left on the public footway or at the front of properties degrade the streetscape and can obstruct pedestrians, mobility scooters, and prams. Aldershire's Local Environmental Quality Plan asks residents to store bins out of sight, on private property, between collections.

## Specific rules

- Bins should be returned to private property by 21:00 on collection day.
- Bins should not be visible from the highway during the period between collections, where physically possible.
- Bins must not block public footways, dropped kerbs, ramps, visibility splays, or cycle lanes.
- Bins on shared driveways must not encroach on the neighbour's frontage.

## Where storage is genuinely impossible

Some Aldershire properties (terraced cottages on Mill Lane, Vale Cobbles, and the conservation core of Aldermarket) have no rear access and no front garden. The council recognises these "limited curtilage" cases and accepts that bins remain at the front of the property. To register your property as "limited curtilage" call 01432 556 200; the case is logged and you are exempted from front-of-property bin enforcement.

## Bin stores in flats

Communal bin stores must be kept tidy, free from side waste, and accessible to crews. Managing agents are responsible for store cleaning, smoke alarms (in covered stores), and lighting where required.

## Listed buildings and conservation areas

Where building consent or conservation rules limit bin storage options, the Streetscene team works with the Conservation Officer to find pragmatic solutions. Options include shared bin stores, common-ground bin huts, and time-limited presentation rules.

## Bin huts and screens

Householders may install bin huts (timber screens, wheelie-bin housings) without planning permission, provided:

- The hut is under 1.2m high and within the front curtilage
- The hut does not obstruct the highway or visibility splays
- Materials are sympathetic to surroundings (especially in conservation areas)

For taller or more elaborate structures, check at aldershire.example.gov.uk/planning-bin-store.

## Enforcement

First contact for non-compliance is an education letter. Persistent non-compliance can lead to a Community Protection Notice and a £100 fixed penalty.
