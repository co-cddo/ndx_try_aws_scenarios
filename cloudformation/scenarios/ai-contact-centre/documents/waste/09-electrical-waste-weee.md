# Electrical waste (WEEE)

## What is WEEE

Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment includes anything that has a plug, runs on batteries, or has electronic components. Aldershire follows the Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment Regulations 2013.

## Small WEEE

Small WEEE means items that fit into a standard carrier bag: kettles, toasters, hairdryers, irons, phones, tablets, small lamps, remote controls, electric toothbrushes, vapes, and similar.

Drop-off:

- Pendle Road Recycling Centre, AL2 5GH (open Mon-Sun)
- Aldermarket Library small WEEE bin, AL1 2BB
- Vale Community Hub small WEEE bin, AL5 1QR

A kerbside small WEEE service runs on the second Wednesday of the month for residents who cannot travel. Place items in a clear plastic bag with a label saying "WEEE" and book on aldershire.example.gov.uk/weee.

## Large WEEE

Fridges, freezers, washing machines, dishwashers, tumble dryers, cookers, televisions, and microwaves over 30cm. Two routes:

1. Bulky waste collection (see 03-bulky-waste-collection.md): £35 plus £15 per refrigerant appliance.
2. Free drop-off at Pendle Road Recycling Centre (proof of Aldershire residence required).

Retailers selling new electrical goods must take back old equivalents under the take-back scheme; many also offer free home collection on delivery of replacements.

## Data wiping

The council does not wipe data on phones, tablets, or laptops. Reset devices to factory defaults and remove SIM and SD cards before drop-off. Aldershire is not liable for any data left on items handed to crews.

## Recovery rates

In 2025 Aldershire collected 412 tonnes of WEEE, of which 91% was reused, refurbished, or recycled in line with national WEEE protocol targets.
