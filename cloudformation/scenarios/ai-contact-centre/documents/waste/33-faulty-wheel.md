# Faulty wheel or hinge on your bin

## Common faults

Wheelie bins typically fail at three points:

1. **Wheels**. Plastic wheel bushings wear and the wheel can detach. The bin then drags rather than rolls.
2. **Lid hinges**. The lid pin can shear, leaving the lid loose or falling off entirely.
3. **Body cracks**. Plastic fatigue near the lift bar produces cracks that propagate.

## Free repairs

Most wheel and hinge faults are repairable on-site. Crews carry spare wheels and hinge pins in the cab and may fix the bin while emptying. To request a planned repair:

- Online: aldershire.example.gov.uk/bin-repair
- Phone: 01432 556 200

You will receive a visit within 10 working days. There is no charge for fair-wear-and-tear repairs.

## Replacement instead of repair

The bin will be replaced (rather than repaired) if:

- More than two faults are present at once
- The body has structural cracks longer than 30 cm
- The bin is over 15 years old (manufacture date stamped on the body)
- The bin has been declared end-of-life by the on-site repairer

Replacement is free for fair-wear cases. See 14-replacement-bins.md for stolen, lost, or wilfully damaged bins.

## Self-help

Lid pins can usually be reseated by gently pressing the pin back into the hinge cap. The council does not stock self-fit wheel kits because incorrectly fitted wheels lift off the wheel-on-axle retention spring and damage the lift bar.

## Old bins

Bins manufactured before 2010 are reaching end of life at scale. Aldershire is on a 12-year rolling renewal programme; expect a replacement letter the year after your bin's 12th birthday. The old bin is collected at the same visit and recycled into new bins via the Aldershire Closed Loop programme (90% of new bins issued in 2025 included recycled content from previous Aldershire bins).

## RFID chips

Each bin has an RFID chip linked to the property. When a bin is replaced, the chip is paired with the new bin so collection records remain consistent. Do not attempt to remove or move chips.
