# Trade waste duty of care

## The legal framework

Section 34 of the Environmental Protection Act 1990 places a "duty of care" on every business producing controlled waste. Failure to comply is a criminal offence with a maximum fine of £5,000 in the Magistrates' Court and unlimited on indictment.

## Five core duties

1. **Prevent escape**. Store waste securely so it cannot blow, leak, or be accessed by vermin or unauthorised persons.
2. **Use a registered carrier**. Anyone collecting your waste must hold a current Waste Carrier Licence. Check at environment-agency.gov.uk or ask for the registration number.
3. **Use authorised destinations**. Waste must be taken to a permitted facility.
4. **Keep records**. Waste Transfer Notes (WTNs) for every transfer for two years (three years for hazardous waste).
5. **Apply the waste hierarchy**. Reduce, reuse, recycle, recover, dispose, in that order.

## Who counts as a business

Any premises operating commercially, including:

- Cafes, restaurants, takeaways, and food trucks
- Salons, barbers, and tattoo studios
- Independent traders working from home (gardening, plumbing, joinery)
- Charities operating shops or community kitchens
- Schools, GP surgeries, and care homes
- Holiday lets and Airbnbs registered for business rates

## Common pitfalls

- Tradespeople taking customer waste home and putting it in their own household bin: this is unlawful disposal and a criminal offence. We have prosecuted three Aldershire-based tradespeople for this in the last 18 months.
- Cafes putting trade refuse in public street bins: pursued under the Litter (Animal Droppings) Order 1991 and Section 87 EPA 1990.
- "Free" man-with-van services on social media: many lack carrier licences. If your waste is later fly-tipped, the duty of care liability remains with you.

## Aldershire support

The council's Trade Waste Education Officer (currently Felix Tweedie) offers free site visits to small businesses. Book at trade@aldershire.example.gov.uk or 01432 556 290.

## Enforcement

In 2025 Aldershire issued 38 fixed penalty notices (£300 each) for duty of care failings, and prosecuted six businesses through Aldershire Magistrates' Court.
