# Abandoned shopping trolleys

## Why we treat them as fly tipping

Abandoned shopping trolleys on the public highway, in watercourses, or in parks are a hazard, an obstruction, and visually degrading. Aldershire works with retailers under section 99 of the Environmental Protection Act 1990, which allows the council to seize and recover costs from the retailer.

## How to report

- Online: aldershire.example.gov.uk/abandoned-trolley
- Phone: 01432 556 250
- Email: streetscene@aldershire.example.gov.uk

Provide:

1. Location (postcode, what3words, or grid reference)
2. Retailer name (Tesco, Sainsbury's, Aldi, B&Q, etc.) and any unique trolley number on the handle
3. Photograph if possible

## Retailer first

Wherever a retailer is identified, the council notifies them within 1 working day. Retailers have 5 working days to recover the trolley before the council acts.

## Council collection and charges

If the retailer fails to act, the council seizes the trolley. Recovery charge per trolley: £62 (covering officer time, vehicle, and storage). Trolleys recovered from watercourses incur an additional dive-team surcharge of £180 where applicable.

In 2025 the council recovered 412 trolleys; £19,800 was reclaimed from retailers.

## Trolley pound deposits

The council encourages retailers to use £1 coin-release pound systems. The Vale Aldi store implemented this in 2024 and recovered trolley losses fell by 78% over the next 12 months.

## Watercourse hazards

Trolleys in the River Alder cause snags and flood-defence concerns. Always report watercourse trolleys promptly; do not attempt recovery yourself.
