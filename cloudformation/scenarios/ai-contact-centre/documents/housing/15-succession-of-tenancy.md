# Succession of tenancy

When a council tenant dies, another household member may have the legal right to take over the tenancy. This is called succession.

## Statutory succession

For secure tenancies granted before 1 April 2012, succession is governed by ss.87-89 Housing Act 1985:

- Spouse or civil partner (in occupation as their only/principal home) succeeds
- A family member may succeed if no spouse/civil partner exists, providing they lived with the tenant for the 12 months ending with the tenant's death

For secure tenancies granted on or after 1 April 2012 (under the Localism Act 2011), only a spouse or civil partner has a statutory right; family members may succeed only if the tenancy expressly grants it.

A tenancy can be succeeded only once.

## Discretionary succession (Aldershire policy)

Where there is no statutory successor, Aldershire considers a discretionary tenancy for:

- A carer who lived with the deceased for 2+ years and gave up their own home
- An adult child who lived with the deceased as their only home for 5+ years
- A grandchild raised by the deceased

Discretionary cases consider whether the property is the right size, under-occupation may lead to an offer of alternative accommodation.

## How to apply

1. Notify Aldershire of the death within 7 days, call 01624 460 210 or use aldershire.example.gov.uk/housing/bereavement
2. Submit Form SUCC1 with the death certificate, proof of relationship, and proof of residence (utility bills, electoral roll)
3. A Succession Officer visits within 14 days
4. Decision in writing within 28 days

## Continuing rent during decision

Rent remains payable from the date of death. The successor (or estate) is liable. We can pause arrears recovery while the succession is decided.

## Right of review

A refusal can be reviewed within 21 days. The review is conducted by the Head of Tenancy Services.

## Property size

If the property has more bedrooms than the successor needs, Aldershire may offer a transfer to a smaller home. This is normally a Band A direct let; the succession is granted on the new property.
