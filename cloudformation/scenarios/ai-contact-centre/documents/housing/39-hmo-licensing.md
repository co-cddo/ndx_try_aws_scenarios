# HMO licensing

A House in Multiple Occupation (HMO) is a property let to 3+ unrelated people sharing facilities (kitchen, bathroom, or WC). Licensing rules are set by the Housing Act 2004.

## Mandatory HMO licensing

A licence is mandatory under s.55 HA 2004 if the HMO has:

- 5 or more occupants forming 2 or more households
- (regardless of property size or number of storeys, post-2018 reform)

This is a national scheme, applies everywhere in Aldershire.

## Aldershire's additional HMO licensing

Under s.56 HA 2004, Aldershire designates additional HMO licensing where evidence supports it. Current designation:

- District-wide additional HMO licensing for HMOs of 3 or 4 occupants (in 2 or more households) where the property has shared kitchen or bathroom facilities, designated 1 January 2025 to 31 December 2029

This means most HMOs of 3+ occupants need a licence in Aldershire.

## How to apply

- Online: aldershire.example.gov.uk/housing/hmo-licence
- Postal Form HMO1
- Phone: 01624 460 247. HMO Officer, Monday to Thursday 09:00 to 16:00

## Fee

April 2026 fees:

- 5-year licence (mandatory HMO, 5+ occupants): £1,485 + £45 per additional bed-space above 6
- 5-year licence (additional HMO, 3 or 4 occupants): £1,205
- Renewal discount: £200 off if applied 60+ days before expiry

## Mandatory conditions

- Gas safety certificate annual
- Hard-wired smoke detection (LD2 or better, BS5839-6)
- CO detection in any room with combustion appliance
- Electrical Installation Condition Report 5-yearly
- Adequate kitchen, bathroom, and WC provision (Aldershire Amenity Standards March 2026)
- Adequate fire doors (FD30 typical) and means of escape (HHSRS principles)
- Refuse storage and bin provision
- Maximum room sizes per occupant (6.51 m² minimum for single bedroom; 10.22 m² for double)

## Manager standards

The licensee or appointed manager must:

- Be a fit and proper person (no relevant convictions or unspent housing-related offences)
- Have arrangements for periodic inspection (at least quarterly)
- Have proper insurance

## Penalties

Operating an unlicensed HMO is a criminal offence. Penalties:

- Unlimited fine on conviction
- Civil penalty up to £30,000
- Rent Repayment Order, up to 12 months' rent
- Banning Order under HPA 2016 (severe cases)
