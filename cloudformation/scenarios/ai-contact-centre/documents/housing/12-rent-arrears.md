# Rent arrears

If you fall behind with rent, Aldershire follows a published arrears recovery process. The aim is to keep tenants in their homes; eviction is a last resort.

## Stage 1: early intervention (1 to 4 weeks arrears)

- SMS reminder on the day rent is missed
- Letter at week 2
- Income Officer phone call at week 3, payment plan offered
- Bot can take a partial payment and set up a plan

## Stage 2: formal warning (4 to 8 weeks arrears)

- Pre-action letter (compliant with the Rent Arrears Pre-Action Protocol)
- Tenant referred to Tenancy Sustainment for budgeting help
- Bidding on Aldershire Homefinder is suspended (see the Allocations Policy)
- Mutual exchange applications are paused

## Stage 3: Notice of Seeking Possession (8+ weeks arrears)

A Notice of Seeking Possession (NoSP) is served under s.83 Housing Act 1985 (secure tenants) or s.8 Housing Act 1988 (assured tenants). The NoSP gives at least 4 weeks' notice and 12 months' validity. Grounds 8, 10, and 11 are typically cited.

## Stage 4: Court action

If arrears continue, Aldershire applies to the County Court for a possession order. Outcomes:

- Outright possession order: tenant must leave by the date stated
- Suspended possession order: tenant stays as long as rent + agreed extra is paid
- Adjournment with directions

Court fees and legal costs are recharged to the tenant on top of the arrears.

## Stage 5: eviction (warrant)

Only after a possession order is breached. A County Court bailiff serves a Notice of Eviction (Form N54) with at least 14 days' notice.

## How to avoid action

- Always contact us before missing a payment
- Apply for Discretionary Housing Payment if you have a shortfall
- Get free, independent debt advice from Aldershire Citizens Advice on 0808 800 900
- The bot can refer you to tenancy sustainment in under 5 minutes
