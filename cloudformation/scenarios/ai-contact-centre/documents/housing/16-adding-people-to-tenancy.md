# Adding people to your tenancy

You may apply to add another person to an Aldershire tenancy as a joint tenant, or to assign the tenancy to another person in limited circumstances.

## Joint tenancy

A joint tenancy means two or more people share equal legal responsibility for the home. Both names appear on the agreement; either can give notice to end it for everyone (Monk principle).

Aldershire considers joint tenancy applications where:

- The applicant is a spouse, civil partner, or cohabitee of 12+ months' standing
- Both parties pass an Aldershire credit check
- The rent account is clear or in arrears < £100
- There are no unresolved tenancy breaches

Apply via aldershire.example.gov.uk/housing/joint-tenancy. Decision in 21 days.

## Assignment

Assignment transfers the tenancy from one person to another. Secure tenancies are generally not assignable (s.91 Housing Act 1985) except:

- Court order (e.g. a financial remedy order on divorce)
- Mutual exchange (see Mutual Exchange knowledge document)
- Assignment to a person who could have succeeded had the tenant died at the date of assignment

Apply via aldershire.example.gov.uk/housing/tenancy-assignment with a draft Deed of Assignment. Aldershire will not unreasonably withhold consent for permitted assignments.

## Family members and lodgers

You may take in a lodger without permission. You may sub-let part of your home with written permission from Aldershire, apply through the same route. Sub-letting the whole home is a breach of tenancy and grounds for possession (Ground 12).

## Adding a partner who has just moved in

Aldershire will not normally agree a joint tenancy until the new partner has lived in the home as their main home for at least 12 months and contributed to rent.
