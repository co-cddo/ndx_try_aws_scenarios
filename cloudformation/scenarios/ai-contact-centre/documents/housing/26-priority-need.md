# Priority need

Priority need determines whether Aldershire has a duty to provide interim and (if other tests are met) main-duty accommodation under Pt VII Housing Act 1996.

## Automatic priority need (s.189(1) HA 1996 + Priority Need Order 2002)

You are automatically in priority need if:

- A pregnant woman or you have a household member who is pregnant
- A dependent child lives with you (or might reasonably be expected to)
- You are homeless because of fire, flood, or other disaster
- You are 16 or 17 (referred to Children's Services first)
- You are a care leaver aged 18 to 21 (or up to 25 in some cases)
- You are an armed forces veteran (Homelessness (Priority Need) (England) Order 2024 amendment)
- You are fleeing domestic abuse (Domestic Abuse Act 2021)

## Vulnerability priority need

You may be in priority need if you are vulnerable as a result of:

- Old age
- Mental or physical illness
- Learning disability
- Time spent in prison, the armed forces, or care
- Other special reason

The vulnerability test (Hotak v Southwark LBC [2015] UKSC 30) compares you to an "ordinary person if made homeless", would you suffer significantly more harm? Aldershire takes evidence from your GP, social worker, mental-health team, probation officer, and any specialist services.

## Evidence we ask for

- Pregnancy: MAT B1 form
- Children: birth certificates and proof they live with you
- Vulnerability: medical or specialist evidence
- Care leaver: pathway plan or letter from leaving-care team
- Veteran: discharge papers (red book) or service number
- Domestic abuse: report from police, IDVA, refuge, or GP, or your account if no report exists

## Decision

The s.184 decision letter explicitly states whether you are in priority need, and why.

## What if I'm not in priority need

If you are eligible and homeless but not in priority need, Aldershire owes you the relief duty for 56 days (Personalised Housing Plan; see Homelessness Application knowledge document) but not interim accommodation. Aldershire House (see Hostels knowledge document) and the Severe Weather provision (SWEP knowledge document) may be available.

## Right of review

A negative priority-need decision can be reviewed within 21 days under s.202. The review is conducted by a senior officer.
