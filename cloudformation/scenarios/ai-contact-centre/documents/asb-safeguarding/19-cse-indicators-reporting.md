# Child sexual exploitation: indicators and reporting

Child sexual exploitation (CSE) is a form of child sexual abuse where an individual or group takes advantage of a power imbalance to coerce, manipulate, or deceive a child or young person under the age of 18 into sexual activity. CSE may involve money, gifts, alcohol, drugs, or affection in return for sexual acts; it can take place online or in person.

CSE is a form of child abuse regardless of whether the child appears to consent or believes they are in a relationship. The legal definition is set out in Working Together to Safeguard Children (HMG 2023) and Keeping Children Safe in Education (DfE annual update).

## Indicators

Frontline staff and the public are encouraged to be alert to:

- A child going missing for periods of time, especially overnight.
- A child returning home with unexplained gifts, money, clothing, or phones.
- An older "boyfriend" or "girlfriend" the child appears secretive about.
- A change in the child's appearance, language, or peer group.
- Use of drugs or alcohol where there has been no previous use.
- Unexplained injuries, sexually transmitted infections, or pregnancy in young teenagers.
- Reluctance to undress for medical examinations or PE.
- Self-harm and significant changes in mental health.
- Use of secret apps, multiple accounts, or chatting with adults online.

A single indicator on its own is rarely conclusive; we look for patterns and context.

## How to report

- **999** if a child is in immediate danger.
- **Aldershire MASH** (multi-agency safeguarding hub) on **01632 960 800**, 24/7.
- **Aldershire Constabulary Public Protection Unit** on **101**.
- **NSPCC**: **0808 800 5000** (adults concerned) or **Childline 0800 1111** (children themselves).
- **CEOP** (Child Exploitation and Online Protection Command), online at **ceop.police.uk/safety-centre** for online concerns including grooming and indecent images.
- **Stop It Now!** confidential helpline for those concerned about their own or another adult's behaviour: **0808 1000 900**.

## What happens after a referral

The MASH triages within 24 hours. For high-risk cases, a strategy discussion under section 47 of the Children Act 1989 is convened and a child protection investigation begins. The investigation is led by the police where there is likely criminal conduct, or jointly with social care for child protection.

Aldershire's response is coordinated through the **Aldershire Vulnerability Hub** (see county-lines page), which holds a weekly multi-agency CSE meeting reviewing every open case.

## Support for children and families

Specialist services available to children identified as victims of CSE:

- **Aldershire Specialist CSE Service**: a council-commissioned service delivered by Aldershire Children's Charity, providing one-to-one specialist support, peer groups, and family work. **01632 960 525**.
- **The Children's Society**: outreach workers in Aldershire as part of the regional Hidden Harms project.
- **NHS Sexual Assault Referral Centre (SARC) at Aldershire General Hospital**: forensic medical examination and ongoing health support, **01632 960 711**.
- **Aldershire Specialist CAMHS**: trauma-informed mental health support for children affected by sexual abuse, accessed through the standard CAMHS referral pathway.

## Prevention

The council works with all secondary schools to deliver age-appropriate Relationships, Sex and Health Education (statutory under the Children and Social Work Act 2017). The Aldershire programme covers consent, healthy relationships, online safety, and what to do if a friend tells you they are being exploited.

## Confidentiality and consent

Information is shared between agencies under the safeguarding gateway. Children are told what is being shared and why, in age-appropriate language, unless to do so would increase risk. The lawful basis is safeguarding (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(d) and (e); Schedule 1 paragraph 18 DPA 2018).

## Legal framework

Sexual Offences Act 2003; Children Act 1989 and 2004; Working Together to Safeguard Children 2023; Aldershire Multi-Agency CSE Procedure (2024).
