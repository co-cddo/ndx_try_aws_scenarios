# Civil injunctions for ASB

A civil injunction is a court order under Part 1 of the Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014. It can prohibit a person from specified conduct or require them to do specified things. The council uses civil injunctions where lower-level interventions have not stopped the behaviour and where a swift, flexible court order is the proportionate next step.

## Who can apply

The council, Aldershire Constabulary, registered housing providers operating in the district, Transport for Aldershire, and certain other relevant bodies can all apply. Where multiple agencies have an interest, we will normally make a joint application.

## What the court must be satisfied of

To grant an injunction the court must be satisfied:

1. On the **balance of probabilities** that the respondent has engaged or threatens to engage in conduct capable of causing nuisance or annoyance (in housing-related cases) or harassment, alarm or distress (in non-housing cases), and
2. That the injunction is **just and convenient** to prevent the respondent from continuing the conduct.

The civil standard of proof, lower than the criminal standard, is one reason civil injunctions are quicker than criminal prosecution.

## What the order can do

Injunctions can include:

- **Prohibitions**: not entering a defined area, not contacting named people, not consuming alcohol in public, not associating with named individuals.
- **Positive requirements**: attending a substance misuse programme, an anger management course, or mediation. The order must specify the supervising agency.
- **Power of arrest**: attached where the conduct involved violence or threat of violence, or where there is significant risk of harm. Police can arrest immediately on suspicion of breach.

## Duration

For adults the injunction can be of any length the court considers just; for under-18s the maximum is 12 months. Reviews must be held for under-18s at least every 12 months.

## Breach

Breach is contempt of court. For adults this can mean up to **2 years' imprisonment** or an unlimited fine. For under-18s breach is dealt with under the youth supervision and detention provisions of the 2014 Act.

## Working with the respondent

Wherever practicable we engage with the respondent before applying. Many people will accept undertakings (a formal promise to the court) which avoid the need for a contested hearing, retain the legal force of an order, and demonstrate engagement.
