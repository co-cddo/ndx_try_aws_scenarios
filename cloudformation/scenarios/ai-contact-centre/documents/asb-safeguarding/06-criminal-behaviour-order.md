# Criminal Behaviour Orders (CBO)

A Criminal Behaviour Order is a post-conviction order under Part 2 of the Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014. It is made by the criminal court at the same time as a sentence, where the offender's conduct has caused or is likely to cause harassment, alarm or distress.

## When a CBO can be made

A CBO can only be made:

- After the offender has been **convicted of a criminal offence**, and
- On the application of the prosecutor (usually the Crown Prosecution Service), and
- Where the court is satisfied beyond reasonable doubt that the offender has engaged in conduct that caused or was likely to cause harassment, alarm or distress, and that an order would help prevent further conduct of that kind.

The court can make a CBO of its own motion at sentencing or on application. The criminal standard of proof is required for the conduct, but the test for whether the order is necessary is "would help prevent" (a lower threshold than the conduct itself).

## What a CBO can include

CBOs can impose prohibitions and positive requirements similar to civil injunctions:

- Not entering specified areas of Aldershire.
- Not contacting named victims or witnesses.
- Not associating with named co-offenders.
- Attending offender-management programmes, drug or alcohol treatment, mental health support.

For each positive requirement the court must specify a supervising person or agency, and that body must consent to the supervision role.

## Duration

For adults, between 2 years and an unlimited maximum. For under-18s, between 1 and 3 years, with a mandatory review every 12 months. The court can vary or discharge a CBO on application.

## Breach

Breach of a CBO without reasonable excuse is a **criminal offence**. The maximum penalty is 5 years' imprisonment or an unlimited fine on indictment, or 6 months and the statutory maximum fine on summary conviction.

## Aldershire's approach

We work with the CPS, Aldershire Constabulary, and HM Probation Service to identify cases where a CBO would meaningfully reduce repeat ASB. The Community Safety team supplies the prosecutor with evidence about the wider community impact, including witness statements, incident logs from our case management system, and impact statements from victims.

## Reporting a breach

If you witness conduct you believe breaches a CBO, report it to Aldershire Constabulary on **101**, or **999** if there is immediate risk. You can also tell the council's Community Safety team on **01632 960 142** so we can update our case record and inform the supervising agency.

## Further information

The Home Office statutory guidance on CBOs (latest version 2023) is available at gov.uk and informs Aldershire's local protocol. The protocol is published at **aldershire.example.gov.uk/community-safety/cbo-protocol**.
