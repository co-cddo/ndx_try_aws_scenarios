# Financial abuse of older people

Financial abuse is one of the ten categories of abuse under the Care Act 2014 statutory guidance. It is defined as the actual or attempted theft, fraud, exploitation, pressure in connection with wills, property, inheritance, or financial transactions, or the misuse or misappropriation of an adult's property, possessions, or benefits.

It disproportionately affects older people, especially those with cognitive impairment, social isolation, or dependence on a carer.

## Common forms

- **Family member misappropriation**: an adult child or grandchild using the older person's bank account or pension for their own benefit.
- **Carer or paid worker theft**: a paid or informal carer taking cash, jewellery, or controlling spending.
- **Misuse of Power of Attorney**: an attorney making decisions or transactions outside their authority or in their own interest.
- **Doorstep fraud and rogue traders**: cold callers offering "essential" home repairs, then overcharging or doing inadequate work.
- **Romance fraud**: an online "partner" gradually extracting money over weeks or months.
- **Investment scams**: unsolicited offers promising high returns, often using high-pressure tactics.
- **Phone and email scams**: impersonating HMRC, the bank, or the police to extract account details or transfers.

## Warning signs for friends, neighbours, professionals

- Unexplained shortage of money in someone with sufficient income.
- Bills unpaid despite available funds.
- Unusual or unexplained transactions on bank statements.
- Disappearance of valued possessions.
- A change in the older person's mood: anxious, withdrawn, evasive about money.
- A new "friend", relative, or carer who controls access or finances.
- Pressure to change a will, sell a property, or sign powers over.

## How to report

- **Aldershire MASH** (adult safeguarding): **01632 960 850**, 24/7.
- **Action Fraud**: **0300 123 2040** for fraud and cybercrime.
- **Aldershire Trading Standards**: **01632 960 720** for rogue traders, doorstep crime, and consumer fraud.
- **Aldershire Constabulary**: **101** for non-emergency, **999** if a crime is in progress.
- **Hourglass** (the new name for Action on Elder Abuse): **0808 808 8141** confidential helpline for any form of abuse of older people.

## What we do

When a referral comes in, the council's Adult Safeguarding Team triages within 24 hours. If section 42 threshold is met, an enquiry is opened. The enquiry typically:

1. Speaks to the adult, in private, with an advocate if helpful.
2. Reviews bank statements, with the adult's consent or under a Power of Attorney/deputyship safeguard.
3. Coordinates with the police where there is a likely criminal offence.
4. Coordinates with the Office of the Public Guardian where misuse of LPA is suspected (referrals to **OPG safeguarding** on **0300 456 0300**).
5. Refers to **Trading Standards** for civil and consumer-protection action against rogue traders.
6. Supports the adult to take protective steps: changing bank cards, cancelling direct debits, fitting a "buddy" service for incoming calls.

## Prevention activities in Aldershire

- **Friends Against Scams**: a free online and in-person training programme delivered by Aldershire Trading Standards. Over 4,200 residents and 800 businesses have been trained since 2020.
- **No Cold Calling Zones**: 47 streets in the district are designated, with door-stickers and warnings to door-to-door traders.
- **Take Five**: national campaign cards available at every council reception and library urging "Stop, Challenge, Protect" before sharing financial information.
- **Banking Protocol**: all major banks operating in the district participate; staff trained to spot and intervene in scam transactions, with a direct line to the police.

## What you can do for a relative

If you are worried about a family member or friend:

1. Talk to them, calmly, in their own home. Do not begin by asking about money.
2. Help them set up notifications on their bank account so they (and you, with their consent) see large transactions.
3. Encourage Lasting Power of Attorney while they have capacity, with two attorneys for accountability.
4. If you have evidence of abuse, contact MASH or the police. Do not confront the suspected abuser.

## Statutory framework

Care Act 2014; Fraud Act 2006; Mental Capacity Act 2005; Aldershire Adult Safeguarding Multi-Agency Financial Abuse Pathway (2024).
