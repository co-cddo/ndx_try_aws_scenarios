# County lines and child criminal exploitation

County lines is a form of organised criminal exploitation where criminal groups from urban areas use children and vulnerable adults to transport and sell drugs in rural and coastal areas. It is one of the most prevalent forms of child criminal exploitation (CCE) in Aldershire.

## What it looks like

A typical pattern:

1. A criminal group identifies a vulnerable child (often pupil-referral-unit-attending, missing from home or care, in poverty, with mental health needs).
2. The child is groomed: gifts, attention, status, sometimes drugs or alcohol.
3. The child is asked to "hold something" or run a small errand.
4. The child is moved to a property in another local authority area to deal drugs ("going country" or "running a line").
5. The child is controlled through debt bondage, threats, and violence.

In some cases an older vulnerable adult's home is taken over for use as a base ("cuckooing"), with the resident threatened or coerced into compliance.

## Warning signs

Indicators we ask front-line staff and the public to watch for:

- A child unexplained absent from school or home, often returning with new clothes, phones, or cash.
- A child carrying a second phone (often a basic Nokia-style "burner").
- Travel to towns where the child has no family or social connection, particularly with one-way tickets or unusual cash.
- A vulnerable adult's home suddenly hosting unfamiliar visitors, especially young people, at all hours.
- A change in friendship group, behaviour, or appearance, particularly aggression or fearfulness.
- Unexplained injuries, especially knife or weapon-consistent injuries.

## How to report

- **999** if a child is in immediate danger.
- **101** for Aldershire Constabulary Public Protection Unit.
- **Aldershire MASH**: **01632 960 800**.
- **Crimestoppers Fearless** (a youth-focused anonymous service): **0800 555 111** or **fearless.org**.
- **Catch22 Missing People & County Lines** support line: **116 000** (free, 24/7).
- For cuckooing concerns about a vulnerable adult: **Adult Social Care** **01632 960 850**.

## Aldershire's response

The council is a member of the **Aldershire Vulnerability Hub**, a co-located team of police, social workers, education welfare officers, youth offending team, and ADAS workers handling complex exploitation cases. The Hub:

- Reviews every reported county-lines or CCE concern within 48 hours.
- Convenes a multi-agency strategy meeting under section 47 of the Children Act 1989 where threshold is met.
- Coordinates a return-home interview for every child reported missing, conducted by an independent worker.
- Maintains a daily intelligence picture of phone numbers, vehicles, and known adults of concern, shared with the Regional Organised Crime Unit.

## National Referral Mechanism

A child exploited through county lines is a victim of trafficking and modern slavery. The council's First Responder team makes an NRM referral in every confirmed case (see modern-slavery page). NRM status protects the child from being prosecuted for offences committed as a direct consequence of being trafficked, under section 45 of the Modern Slavery Act 2015.

## Prevention and disruption

The council, with Aldershire Constabulary and schools, runs:

- **#ExploitedNotInTrouble** awareness campaign in every secondary school and PRU each autumn term.
- Targeted youth diversion at the Aldershire Youth Hub (**01632 960 320**), open Tuesday-Saturday evenings and Saturdays.
- A "trusted adult" mentoring scheme matching young people at high risk with a vetted volunteer mentor.
- Joint home visits ("disruption visits") to addresses suspected of being used for cuckooing.

## Statutory framework

Children Act 1989 (section 17, section 47); Modern Slavery Act 2015 (sections 1, 2, 45); Working Together to Safeguard Children (HMG 2023); Aldershire Children's Safeguarding Partnership CCE Strategy 2024-2027.
