# Community trigger appeals: escalation to the Police and Crime Commissioner

Where a victim of persistent anti-social behaviour has used the ASB Case Review (community trigger) and remains dissatisfied with the outcome, the Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014 (section 105(2)) and Aldershire's local procedure provide an **appeal route to the Police and Crime Commissioner (PCC) for Aldershire**.

## When you can appeal

You can appeal to the PCC if:

1. You requested an ASB Case Review at Aldershire District Council, **and**
2. The threshold was met and a review was held (or the council declined a review you believe should have been held), **and**
3. You are dissatisfied with the outcome of the review or the council's threshold decision.

The appeal is not an appeal against the original behaviour or the agencies' day-to-day handling, except as those are reflected in the Case Review.

## How to appeal

- **Phone the PCC's office**: **01632 960 099** (Monday-Friday 9am-5pm).
- **Email**: **complaints@aldershire-pcc.example.uk**.
- **Online form**: **aldershire-pcc.example.uk/community-trigger-appeals**.
- **In writing**: PCC for Aldershire, Police HQ, Force Lane, Aldershire AL2 4PP.

You should appeal **within 28 days** of receiving the Case Review outcome letter, or 28 days from the threshold decision letter. The PCC may consider late appeals where there is good reason.

## What the PCC will do

The PCC's office acknowledges the appeal within 5 working days. The PCC then:

1. Requests the case file from the council and any partner agencies.
2. Reviews the original referral, the threshold decision, the Case Review meeting minutes, the action plan, and progress against it.
3. Assesses whether the agencies followed their statutory duties and the locally agreed procedure.
4. Considers whether the action plan was sufficient and whether it has been delivered.
5. Holds a meeting with the appellant where useful (in person, by phone, or by video) at the appellant's preference.

The PCC produces a written response within **8 weeks** (extendable to 12 weeks for complex cases, with the appellant told of the extension).

## What the PCC can do

The PCC does not have power to direct operational decisions on individual cases (those remain with the council, the police, and other agencies). The PCC can:

- Make findings about whether procedures were followed.
- Recommend that the council reconvene the Case Review, with new participants or additional information.
- Recommend that the action plan be revised.
- Refer issues of force performance to the Chief Constable.
- Refer issues of council performance to the council's Chief Executive.
- Make systemic recommendations to the Aldershire Community Safety Partnership.
- Publish the response (anonymised) in the PCC's annual report on community trigger performance.

The PCC cannot order the police or council to take specific operational action, prosecute someone, evict a tenant, or grant compensation.

## Other routes

If your concern is about the **conduct of an individual police officer**, that is a complaint to the police professional standards department, ultimately escalable to the Independent Office for Police Conduct (IOPC).

If your concern is about the **conduct of a council officer** or a council decision, that is a complaint through the council's complaints procedure (**01632 960 105**), ultimately escalable to the Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman (LGSCO) at **lgo.org.uk** or **0300 061 0614**.

If your concern is about a **breach of your data protection rights** in the handling of an ASB case, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at **ico.org.uk**.

## What you can expect

- A non-judgmental review by an independent function.
- Clear written outcomes.
- Anonymised publication of patterns in the annual report so that learning is shared.
- Transparency about what the PCC can and cannot do.

## Performance

In the year to March 2025, the Aldershire PCC received 14 community trigger appeals. The PCC upheld 3 in full, 6 in part, and dismissed 5. In 9 cases the council reconvened the Case Review or revised the action plan as a result.

## Statutory framework

Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014 (sections 104-106); Police Reform and Social Responsibility Act 2011 (PCC functions); Aldershire ASB Case Review and Appeals Procedure (2024).
