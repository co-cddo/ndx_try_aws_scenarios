# Acceptable Behaviour Contract (ABC)

An Acceptable Behaviour Contract is a voluntary written agreement between a person engaged in anti-social behaviour and the council (often jointly with Aldershire Constabulary or the person's landlord). It is usually our first formal step where informal warnings have not changed the conduct.

## When we use an ABC

ABCs are most effective for:

- Young people aged 10 to 17 whose conduct is escalating but has not yet reached criminal thresholds.
- Adults whose ASB is persistent but where engagement is realistic and a court order would be disproportionate.
- Households where multiple agencies want a coordinated, low-formality intervention.

ABCs are **not** statutory orders. They cannot be enforced directly through the courts. Their power lies in (a) the agreed expectations being recorded, (b) breach being a strong basis for stronger action such as a civil injunction, and (c) the perpetrator's recognition that the consequences of breach are real.

## What an ABC contains

Each ABC is bespoke but typically includes:

- The specific conduct the person agrees to stop (e.g. "I will not enter the area bounded by [streets]" or "I will not contact [named person]").
- Positive obligations (e.g. "I will attend the youth diversion programme at Aldershire Youth Hub on Wednesdays").
- Duration: usually 6 months, with a review at 3 months.
- Consequences of breach, set out in plain language.
- Signatures of the perpetrator (and a parent or guardian if under 18), the council officer, and any partner agency.

## How an ABC is agreed

The Community Safety officer arranges a meeting with the person concerned, often at home or at a neutral venue. For under-18s, a parent or guardian must attend. The officer presents the evidence of the conduct, explains the alternative (potential injunction), and negotiates the terms. The contract is signed in person.

## Review and breach

The case officer reviews compliance at 3 months. If there are no concerns, the contract continues to its end date and the case is closed with a record retained for 12 months. If there is a breach, the officer will normally issue a formal written warning and consider escalation to a Community Protection Notice or civil injunction.

## Statutory basis

ABCs are non-statutory but operate within the framework of the Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014. They form part of the structured escalation pathway alongside CPNs (section 43), civil injunctions (Part 1), and Criminal Behaviour Orders (Part 2).
