# Young carers: schools and council support

A young carer is anyone under 18 who provides regular and ongoing care to a family member who has a physical or mental illness, disability, or substance misuse problem. The Children and Families Act 2014 (section 96) and the Care Act 2014 (section 60) together create rights for young carers and parent carers respectively.

There are an estimated 1,200 young carers in Aldershire schools, although true numbers are higher because many young carers do not identify themselves as such.

## Young Carer's Needs Assessment

A young carer is entitled to an assessment under section 96 of the Children and Families Act 2014 (which inserted section 17ZA into the Children Act 1989). The assessment must consider:

- Whether the care is appropriate for the young carer to provide, given their age, education, and other commitments.
- The impact on the young carer's wellbeing, education, and life chances.
- The young carer's own wishes and feelings.
- Whether the cared-for person needs more support, in order to reduce the load on the young carer.

The assessment is free and is provided by the council's Children's Services. Where the young carer is over 16 and approaching adulthood, a "transition assessment" combines child and adult provision (Care Act 2014 section 63).

## How to request an assessment

- **Aldershire Young Carers Service** on **01632 960 580** (delivered by Aldershire Carers Hub).
- Through the school: every school in Aldershire has a designated Young Carers Lead.
- **Aldershire MASH (children)**: **01632 960 800**, for any concerns about a child's welfare including young caring.
- Self-referral by the young carer: yes, possible, no minimum age. We will explain to a younger child what an assessment is and how it might help.

## Support available

- **One-to-one support workers**: regular contact, often weekly, helping the young carer manage school, friendships, and caring.
- **Young Carers Action Days**: termly trips and activities (climbing, theatre, beach trips) giving young carers a break from caring with peers in the same situation.
- **Holiday programmes** in school holidays, with transport from home.
- **Help in school**: liaison with the school to flexibility on homework deadlines, lunchtime quiet space, awareness training for teachers.
- **Help at home**: where the cared-for person needs more support so the child can be a child, we coordinate with adult social care to arrange paid carers or respite.
- **Transition support** at age 16-18 into adult services.

## Young Carers ID Card

Aldershire issues a Young Carers ID Card to any young carer in the district. The card:

- Confirms the young person's status if they need to leave school early or be excused PE for caring reasons.
- Provides discounts at participating leisure centres and the cinema.
- Includes an emergency contact for the young carer's nominated lead worker.

Issued free at the Aldershire Carers Hub.

## Schools' role

Every school in Aldershire is a signatory to the **Aldershire Young Carers in Schools Charter**. Commitments include:

- Identifying young carers through PSHE lessons and inductions.
- Designating a Young Carers Lead among the staff, with annual training.
- Allowing flexibility on attendance, homework, and uniform where caring requires it.
- Working with the Aldershire Young Carers Service on referrals and joint plans.

## Safeguarding considerations

A young carer providing inappropriate levels of care is a child in need of support; in serious cases, child in need of protection. The threshold question is whether the caring is age-appropriate and impacting on the child's welfare. A child should not be the primary carer for a parent with severe mental illness, addiction, or terminal illness; that situation needs intervention not signposting.

## Practical things parents can do

- Tell the school. Schools cannot help if they do not know.
- Apply for **Personal Independence Payment** for the cared-for parent if eligible; PIP unlocks Carer's Allowance for an over-16 carer.
- Use the Aldershire Carers Hub.
- Talk with the young person about what they enjoy and how to protect time for it.

## Legal framework

Children Act 1989 (section 17ZA, 17ZB, 17ZC); Children and Families Act 2014; Care Act 2014; Aldershire Young Carers Strategy 2023-2028.
