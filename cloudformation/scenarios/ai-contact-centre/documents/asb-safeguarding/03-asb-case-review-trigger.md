# ASB Case Review (community trigger)

The ASB Case Review, sometimes called the community trigger, is a statutory right introduced by the Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014 (sections 104 and 105). It allows victims of persistent ASB to require relevant agencies to review their case where they feel the response so far has been inadequate.

## When you can use it

You can request an ASB Case Review if:

- You have reported **three or more incidents** of related ASB to a relevant agency (the council, Aldershire Constabulary, or a registered housing provider) within the previous **6 months**, and
- You believe no adequate action has been taken in response.

A single hate-motivated incident may also trigger a review, even where the three-report threshold has not been met, in line with the locally agreed lower threshold for hate crime.

## How to request a review

- Online at **aldershire.example.gov.uk/asb/case-review**.
- By phone on **01632 960 198**.
- In writing to ASB Case Review Coordinator, Aldershire District Council, Civic Centre, High Street, Aldershire AL1 1CC.

We will acknowledge your request within 5 working days and confirm whether the threshold is met. If it is, we convene a multi-agency review meeting within **20 working days**.

## What happens at the review

Representatives from the council, Aldershire Constabulary, your housing provider (if applicable), and any other relevant body meet to:

1. Review every incident you have reported.
2. Assess the response from each agency and identify gaps.
3. Agree a fresh action plan with timescales and a single accountable lead.
4. Communicate the plan to you in writing within 10 working days of the meeting.

## Who oversees the process

The ASB Case Review Coordinator chairs the meeting and is independent of the original case officer. The coordinator reports quarterly to the Aldershire Community Safety Partnership and annually to Cabinet.

## If you are still not satisfied

If you remain dissatisfied with the review outcome, you can appeal to the Police and Crime Commissioner for Aldershire, who has a statutory role in scrutinising the local ASB response. Details at **aldershire.example.gov.uk/asb/case-review/appeal** or by calling the PCC's office on **01632 960 099**.

## Numbers and transparency

The council publishes the number of ASB Case Reviews requested, threshold met, and outcomes annually on our open-data portal. In the year to March 2025 we received 31 requests, met the threshold on 22, and changed the action plan in 18.
