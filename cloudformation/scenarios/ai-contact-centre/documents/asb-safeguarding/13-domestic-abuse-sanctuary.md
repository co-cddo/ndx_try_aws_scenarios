# Domestic abuse: Aldershire Sanctuary scheme and refuge

Domestic abuse is any single incident or pattern of incidents of controlling, coercive, threatening, degrading, or violent behaviour, including sexual violence, by someone who is or has been an intimate partner or family member. The Domestic Abuse Act 2021 sets out the statutory definition; section 78 places a duty on tier-one local authorities to provide support to victims in safe accommodation.

## Aldershire Sanctuary scheme

The Sanctuary scheme allows victims to remain safely in their own home where they choose to do so, with home security upgrades funded by the council. We deliver the scheme in partnership with Aldershire Domestic Abuse Service (ADAS) and Aldershire Constabulary.

### What is included

- Specialist front and rear door locks and reinforcements.
- Window security upgrades (laminated glazing, multi-point locking).
- Personal alarm devices and panic buttons linked to a 24/7 monitoring service.
- A designated "sanctuary room" within the property: usually a bedroom upgraded to be temporarily defensible.
- Garden gate and shed security where appropriate.
- Escape lighting and CCTV doorbell.

### How to access

Sanctuary is available to victims of domestic abuse who own or rent their home in Aldershire and who choose, with safety planning support, to remain in it. The pathway is:

1. Referral by ADAS, Aldershire Constabulary, the council's Housing Options team, or self-referral on **01632 960 511**.
2. Risk assessment by a specialist DA practitioner using the DASH-RIC tool.
3. Property survey by the council's Sanctuary Coordinator.
4. Works delivered, typically within 5 working days of survey for high-risk cases.
5. Follow-up safety plan review at 6 weeks and 6 months.

The scheme is free to the victim. Costs are met from the council's section 78 grant and ring-fenced reserves.

## Refuge and emergency accommodation

Where remaining at home is not safe, the council and ADAS arrange refuge or emergency accommodation. Aldershire has:

- **Two specialist refuges** (one general, one for women from minoritised communities) operated by ADAS, with 18 family spaces in total.
- **Reciprocal arrangements** with refuges in 12 neighbouring local authorities for victims who need to relocate out of district.
- **Move-on accommodation**: dispersed self-contained flats for the next stage after refuge.

Phone the **24-hour ADAS helpline on 01632 960 999** or the **National Domestic Abuse Helpline on 0808 2000 247** at any time.

## What the council cannot do alone

Domestic abuse response is multi-agency. We coordinate with health, the police, the family courts, and specialist support services. The council cannot grant a non-molestation order or occupation order itself; those are made by the family court on application by the victim, supported by ADAS or a solicitor (legal aid is available regardless of means in many DA cases).

## Information for someone you are worried about

If you are worried about a friend, neighbour, or family member, you can call the helpline numbers above for advice. Bystander reporting is welcomed; we will treat the call confidentially. The bot recognises domestic abuse disclosures and routes immediately to a trained human agent.

## Statutory framework

Domestic Abuse Act 2021; Domestic Abuse Statutory Guidance (2022); Aldershire Domestic Abuse Local Strategy 2023-2026.
