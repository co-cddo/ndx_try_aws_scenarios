# Refugee and migrant integration support

Aldershire welcomes residents who have moved from elsewhere in the world. Some are British citizens by birth or naturalisation; some are EU/EEA citizens with settled status; some are refugees, asylum seekers, or holders of other forms of leave to remain.

The council's role in integration is to help all residents access the services they need, navigate UK systems, and feel part of community life, while being clear about who is eligible for which forms of support under the law.

## Aldershire Welcome Hub

The Aldershire Welcome Hub is the council's commissioned single point of contact for newly arrived residents. It is delivered by Aldershire Refugee Action in partnership with the council and several voluntary sector partners.

- Phone: **01632 960 740**.
- Email: **welcome@aldershire-refugee.example.org**.
- Drop-in: Tuesdays 10am-3pm and Saturdays 11am-2pm at the Aldershire Community Hub.

Services:

- One-to-one casework for the first 12 months after arrival.
- Help with school enrolment, GP registration, English classes, opening a bank account.
- Translation and interpretation in 22 languages with the council's commissioned provider.
- Connection to local community groups, faith groups, and friendship networks.
- Mental health support through Aldershire Refugee Action's specialist trauma counselling service (clinically led, free at the point of access).

## Schemes the council supports

- **UK Resettlement Scheme** and **Afghan Citizens Resettlement Scheme**: Aldershire has welcomed 47 families since 2021, housed in a mix of council and private rented properties with intensive support for the first 12-24 months.
- **Homes for Ukraine**: more than 280 individuals hosted by 142 households in the district as of April 2026. The council supervises sponsor checks, welfare visits, and rematch where needed.
- **Hong Kong British National (Overseas) visa**: arrivals are supported through the standard Aldershire Welcome Hub pathway.
- **Asylum dispersal**: Aldershire is part of the national dispersal scheme; the Home Office contracts accommodation through Mears, with statutory wraparound from health and education provided by local services.

## Eligibility for council services

Eligibility depends on immigration status. Most settled residents (British citizens, ILR, settled status, refugees, BNO visa holders) have full access to homelessness services, social care, and benefits subject to ordinary eligibility rules.

Residents with **No Recourse to Public Funds (NRPF)** restrictions on their visas have restricted access. Where there is a child in the family or an adult with care needs, support may still be available under section 17 of the Children Act 1989 or section 18 of the Care Act 2014. The council's NRPF Team assesses on a case-by-case basis: phone **01632 960 770**.

Asylum seekers are supported through the Home Office (accommodation and a weekly subsistence allowance) and have limited council entitlements. The Welcome Hub helps navigate.

## English language support

Free English for Speakers of Other Languages (ESOL) classes are available at:

- Aldershire College (full-time and part-time entry-level to GCSE-equivalent classes).
- Aldershire Adult Learning Service (community-based informal classes).
- Aldershire Library Service (conversation cafes).
- Aldershire Refugee Action (women-only English groups).

ESOL is free for those on means-tested benefits or with refugee/HFU/ARAP status.

## Children in school

Schools enrol any child of compulsory school age living in their catchment, regardless of immigration status. Free school meals are available where the family qualifies. The council's School Admissions Team (**01632 960 350**) assists with mid-year applications.

## Work, qualifications, and recognition

- **Aldershire Job Centre Plus**: standard employment support for those with the right to work.
- **NARIC / UK ENIC**: national recognition body for foreign qualifications.
- **Aldershire Adult Learning**: short courses and digital skills.
- **Aldershire Refugee Council Mentoring Programme**: matches refugees with professional mentors in their field.

## Faith, culture, and community

Aldershire has more than 60 active faith and cultural community organisations. The Aldershire Multi-Faith Forum (**01632 960 460**) maintains a directory and runs an annual joint celebration. The council's Cultural Programme funds events that bring residents together across communities.

## Hate crime

See the hate crime page. Aldershire Refugee Action is a designated third-party reporting centre. Refugees and migrants are sometimes targeted; we record and respond to hate-motivated conduct as we would for any resident.

## Statutory framework

Children Act 1989 (section 17); Care Act 2014; Asylum and Immigration Acts (various); Aldershire Welcoming Strategy 2024-2028.
