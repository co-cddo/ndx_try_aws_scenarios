# Community Protection Notice (CPN)

A Community Protection Notice is a statutory notice issued by the council under section 43 of the Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014. It is used to address conduct by individuals aged 16 or over, or by businesses or organisations, where the conduct is having a detrimental effect on the quality of life of those in the locality and is unreasonable.

## When we issue a CPN

Typical Aldershire uses include:

- **Persistent graffiti** on a building or wall where the responsible owner has not taken action despite advice.
- **Untidy gardens** that attract pests, present health hazards, or visibly blight a residential street.
- **Noise nuisance** from a business that does not warrant a statutory environmental health abatement notice but is detrimental to neighbours.
- **Persistent dog fouling** by an identifiable dog walker.
- **Litter accumulation** on private land visible from the public street.
- **Unmanaged vegetation** overhanging the public highway and obstructing pedestrians.

## The two-step process

### 1. Written warning

We must first issue a written warning before issuing the CPN itself. The warning sets out:

- The conduct.
- Why it is having a detrimental effect.
- What the recipient must do (or stop doing) to prevent escalation.
- A reasonable period to comply (typically 14-28 days, depending on the work involved).

The warning is served by hand, by post, or (for premises) by display.

### 2. The CPN

If the conduct continues after the warning period, the case officer issues the CPN. The notice can:

- **Prohibit** specified conduct.
- **Require** the recipient to do specified things to address the conduct.
- **Require reasonable steps** to prevent recurrence.

We work with the recipient where reasonable to agree achievable steps. Where time-limited financial support is needed (e.g. an older person unable to clear an overgrown garden), we can refer to community organisations such as Aldershire Care & Repair (**01632 960 234**).

## Authorised officers

In Aldershire, CPNs may be issued by Community Safety officers, environmental health officers, neighbourhood wardens, and (under our partnership protocol) Aldershire Constabulary PCSOs. All authorised officers carry an identity card and can produce written authorisation on request.

## Breach

Breach of a CPN is a **criminal offence**. The penalty is a fixed penalty notice of **£100** (reduced to £75 if paid within 14 days) or prosecution leading to a fine of up to **£2,500** for individuals or **£20,000** for businesses on summary conviction.

The council can also undertake the required works itself ("default works") and recover the cost from the recipient as a civil debt.

## Appeal

The recipient can appeal to the magistrates' court within **21 days** of being served the CPN, on specified grounds (the conduct did not take place, the conduct was reasonable, the requirements are not proportionate). An appeal suspends the notice.
