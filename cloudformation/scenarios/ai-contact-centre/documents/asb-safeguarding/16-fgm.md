# Female Genital Mutilation (FGM): mandatory reporting duty

Female Genital Mutilation is illegal in England and Wales under the Female Genital Mutilation Act 2003. It is a form of child abuse and a violation of the human rights of girls and women.

## The mandatory reporting duty

Section 5B of the FGM Act 2003 (inserted by the Serious Crime Act 2015) creates a **mandatory duty** on regulated health professionals, regulated social workers, and teachers to report known cases of FGM in under-18s to the police.

The duty applies where:

- The professional, in the course of their work, **observes physical signs** appearing to show that an act of FGM has been carried out on a girl under 18, **or**
- A girl under 18 **discloses** to the professional that an act of FGM has been carried out on her.

In Aldershire, council social workers, school-based teachers, and NHS staff at Aldershire General Hospital and the community health trust are all subject to the duty.

## How to make a mandatory report

The professional must report to the police by **calling 101** (or 999 if the child is in immediate danger) **as soon as possible** and **always within one month** of the disclosure or observation.

The report includes:

- Identifying details (name, date of birth, address).
- The reason for the report (disclosure or observation).
- Whether others (such as parents, carers, siblings) may also be at risk.

Aldershire Constabulary records each mandatory report on a unique CRIS reference and shares it (in line with the multi-agency protocol) with Aldershire Children's Services Multi-Agency Safeguarding Hub (MASH).

## Suspected FGM (where the duty does not apply)

The mandatory duty does not apply to **suspected** FGM (where signs are not seen and there is no disclosure but professional concern exists), nor to under-18s' siblings who may be at future risk, nor to over-18s. In those cases the professional follows ordinary safeguarding referral routes:

- **Aldershire MASH**: **01632 960 800** (children) or **01632 960 850** (adults).
- **NSPCC FGM helpline**: **0800 028 3550**.

## What happens after a report

The police safeguarding lead and MASH jointly assess. Outcomes can include:

- A child protection investigation under section 47 of the Children Act 1989.
- A criminal investigation against the person(s) responsible.
- Application for an FGM Protection Order under Part 4 of the Schedule 2 of the FGM Act 2003 (added by the Serious Crime Act 2015) to prevent travel, surrender passports, or impose other necessary conditions.

## FGM Protection Orders

These are civil orders, broadly similar to Forced Marriage Protection Orders. Applications can be made by the girl herself, by a relevant third party (the local authority is a relevant third party), or by another person with leave of the court. Breach is a criminal offence.

## Aldershire's approach

The council is a member of the Aldershire FGM Multi-Agency Group, which meets quarterly and includes representatives from Aldershire General Hospital, the maternity service (which records every pregnancy with a marker for FGM history), schools, and community organisations from communities affected. The group reviews data, training, and prevention activities.
