# Online safety for children: local resources

The internet is part of children's lives. Most online interaction is positive. A small but serious minority of online activity exposes children to grooming, sexual abuse, exploitation, bullying, or harmful content. The council's role, alongside schools, families, and the police, is to help families understand the risks and respond when something goes wrong.

## Key online harms

- **Grooming**: an adult or older young person befriends a child online with the intention of sexual abuse or exploitation.
- **Sextortion**: blackmail using sexual images, often shared after being elicited under false pretences.
- **Self-generated indecent images**: under-18s taking and sharing intimate images of themselves; the images become illegal as soon as they are made.
- **Cyberbullying**: persistent harassment via messaging, social media, or gaming.
- **Harmful content**: pornography, suicide and self-harm content, eating disorder content.
- **Online radicalisation**: targeting by extremist groups.

## How to report

For an immediate threat to a child: **999**.

For non-emergency online safety concerns:

- **CEOP** (Child Exploitation and Online Protection Command): the police's specialist unit. Report at **ceop.police.uk/safety-centre**. Trained child protection professionals review and act on every report.
- **Internet Watch Foundation (IWF)**: report indecent images of children at **iwf.org.uk**. Reports are anonymous; the IWF gets the images removed.
- **Report Remove (NSPCC + IWF)**: a free service for under-18s to get an intimate image of themselves taken offline. **childline.org.uk/report-remove**.
- **Aldershire MASH**: **01632 960 800** if the concern is about a specific identifiable child.

## Aldershire local resources

- **Aldershire Online Safety Hub**: a council webpage gathering trusted UK guidance, age-by-age advice, and a directory of local digital-safety workshops. **aldershire.example.gov.uk/online-safety**.
- **Family digital workshops**: monthly evening sessions at Aldershire Central Library and Riverside Community Centre, led by a council Digital Inclusion Officer. Free; book on **01632 960 410**.
- **School DSLs (Designated Safeguarding Leads)**: every school in the district has a DSL trained in online harms; contact your child's school first for school-related concerns.
- **Aldershire Youth Hub digital drop-in**: peer-led sessions for 11-19s on online safety, every Wednesday 4-6pm, **01632 960 320**.
- **Aldershire Children's Charity Online Wellbeing Service**: free counselling for under-18s affected by online harm, **01632 960 525**.

## What parents and carers can do

Practical steps:

1. **Talk early and often**. Make it normal to ask "what are you doing online today?" Don't wait for a crisis.
2. **Use parental controls**, especially on devices used by under-13s. Most ISPs and platforms offer them; instructions are at **internetmatters.org**.
3. **Set agreed family rules**: where devices are used (not behind closed doors for younger children), what time they go off, what apps and games are okay.
4. **Know who they're talking to**. For under-13s, the rule is no chat with people they have not met in person.
5. **Set up Report Remove** if a young person is worried about an image (see above).
6. **Don't promise secrecy**. If a young person tells you something serious online, you may need to share it with the police or the school.

## Statutory and policy context

Online Safety Act 2023; Keeping Children Safe in Education (annual DfE statutory guidance); Aldershire Children's Safeguarding Partnership Online Safety Strategy 2024-2027.

## When the AI cannot help

This contact centre's bot does not provide live online-safety counselling, child welfare advice, or crisis intervention. Online safety disclosures are routed to a human safeguarding-trained agent immediately.
