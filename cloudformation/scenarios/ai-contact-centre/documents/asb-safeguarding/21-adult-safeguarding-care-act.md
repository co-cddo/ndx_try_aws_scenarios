# Adult safeguarding referrals (Care Act 2014)

The council has a statutory duty under section 42 of the Care Act 2014 to make enquiries (or cause others to make enquiries) where it has reasonable cause to suspect that an adult in its area:

- Has needs for care and support (whether or not the council is meeting any of those needs), and
- Is experiencing, or at risk of, abuse or neglect, and
- As a result of those care and support needs, is unable to protect themselves from abuse or neglect or the risk of it.

This is the **section 42 enquiry duty**.

## Who is an "adult at risk"

An adult at risk is anyone aged 18 or over with care and support needs. Examples include:

- A person with learning disabilities, autism, or physical disabilities.
- An older person with dementia or frailty.
- A person with significant mental health needs.
- A person with substance dependence affecting their ability to protect themselves.
- A person with a serious health condition affecting their cognition or mobility.

A person without care and support needs is not within the scope of section 42, although other safeguarding routes (domestic abuse, hate crime, ASB) may apply.

## Categories of abuse

The Care and Support Statutory Guidance lists ten categories that the council recognises:

1. Physical abuse.
2. Sexual abuse.
3. Psychological / emotional abuse.
4. Financial / material abuse.
5. Neglect and acts of omission.
6. Self-neglect.
7. Discriminatory abuse.
8. Organisational abuse.
9. Domestic abuse.
10. Modern slavery.

## How to make a safeguarding referral

- **Aldershire MASH (adults)**: **01632 960 850**, 24/7.
- **Online**: **aldershire.example.gov.uk/adult-safeguarding/refer**.
- **By post**: Adult Safeguarding Team, Aldershire District Council, Civic Centre, High Street, Aldershire AL1 1CC.
- **In an emergency**: **999**.

Anyone can make a referral: the adult themselves, a family member, a neighbour, a professional. Professionals have a duty to refer if section 42 criteria are met.

## What we ask

We ask for:

- Identifying details of the adult (name, date of birth, address, GP if known).
- The nature of the concern.
- Any immediate risk.
- Whether the adult knows about the referral and consents to it (we ask, but we do not require consent for the referral if the adult lacks capacity to consent or there is overriding public interest).

## What happens after a referral

1. **Triage** within 24 hours by the MASH duty social worker.
2. **Section 42(2) decision**: does the case meet the duty? If yes, an enquiry is opened.
3. **Enquiry**: a social worker investigates, gathering information and speaking to the adult (with an advocate if needed).
4. **Outcome**: a safeguarding plan agreed with the adult, capturing what they want to happen and what we will do.
5. **Review**: at agreed intervals, with closure when risk is reduced and stable.

## Making Safeguarding Personal

We work to "Making Safeguarding Personal" principles set out in the statutory guidance. The adult sets the desired outcomes; we work towards those, not towards what we think is best for them. We presume capacity (Mental Capacity Act 2005) and respect the right to take risks where the adult has capacity and is informed.

## Independent advocacy

Where an adult has substantial difficulty engaging with the safeguarding process and there is no appropriate person to support them, we instruct an Independent Advocate under section 68 of the Care Act 2014. Aldershire's advocacy provider is Aldershire Advocacy CIC (**01632 960 470**).

## Confidentiality and information sharing

We share information with partner agencies under the Aldershire Adult Safeguarding Information Sharing Agreement, with the lawful bases of public task, vital interests, and (for special category data) the safeguarding condition in Schedule 1 paragraph 18 of the Data Protection Act 2018.

## Legal framework

Care Act 2014 (sections 42-46); Care and Support Statutory Guidance (DHSC 2023); Mental Capacity Act 2005; Aldershire Safeguarding Adults Board Multi-Agency Procedures (2024).
