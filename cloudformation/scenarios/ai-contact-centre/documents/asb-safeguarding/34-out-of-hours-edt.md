# Out-of-hours safeguarding: Emergency Duty Team

Safeguarding does not stop when the office closes. The **Aldershire Emergency Duty Team (EDT)** provides statutory social-care response outside normal working hours: evenings, weekends, and bank holidays. EDT is the council's statutory standby service under the Local Authority Social Services Act 1970 and the Children Act 1989.

## When EDT operates

EDT covers:

- **Weeknights**: 5pm to 9am the next morning.
- **Weekends**: 5pm Friday to 9am Monday.
- **Bank holidays**: 24 hours of cover for each bank holiday day.

During working hours, the appropriate daytime team handles cases (Aldershire MASH for new referrals, ongoing case workers for open cases).

## What EDT does

EDT social workers respond to safeguarding situations that cannot wait until the next working day. Typical scenarios:

- A child found alone or unsafe.
- An emergency placement of a child whose carers cannot keep them safe.
- A child or young person needing accommodation under section 20 of the Children Act 1989 because there is no safe family member.
- An adult at risk requiring emergency intervention (e.g. an older person discharged from hospital with no care in place).
- A Mental Health Act assessment where an Approved Mental Health Professional (AMHP) is needed.
- Welfare visits to high-risk safeguarding cases where the daytime team has flagged concerns.
- Court-ordered protection of a child outside court hours (Emergency Protection Order liaison).

EDT does not provide ongoing case management. It hands cases to the daytime team at 9am the next working day with a written summary of action taken.

## How to contact EDT

- **EDT phone**: **01632 960 999** (24/7).
- The phone number is the same as our 24-hour council emergency line; the call handler routes to EDT for safeguarding matters.
- **In a life-threatening emergency**: 999.

EDT does not take referrals by email or online form out of hours. The phone is the route. This is to ensure rapid triage and avoid mailbox-buried emergencies.

## What information to give

When you call EDT:

- Identifying details of the person at risk.
- Where they are now (precise location if possible).
- Immediate risks (medical, violence, environmental).
- Your relationship to the person and your contact details.
- Whether the police, ambulance, or fire services are already involved.

If the matter is genuinely an emergency to life, call 999 first, then EDT.

## Mental Health Act assessments

EDT includes Approved Mental Health Professionals on duty. They can coordinate Mental Health Act 1983 assessments in conjunction with two doctors (one section 12 approved). The pathway is:

1. NHS clinician or police identify need.
2. EDT AMHP attends or coordinates remotely.
3. Two doctors complete medical recommendations.
4. AMHP makes the application; the person is detained to a hospital named on the form.

Aldershire MHA assessments out of hours are completed at the patient's location, the hospital A&E, or a designated Section 136 suite at Aldershire General Hospital.

## Police and EDT joint working

EDT works closely with Aldershire Constabulary, especially the Force Control Room and the on-call Detective Inspector for Public Protection. Police-initiated section 46 (children) "police protection" decisions trigger EDT involvement to find suitable placement.

## Limits of EDT

EDT does not:

- Provide ongoing care planning, support packages, or non-urgent assessments. These wait until the daytime team.
- Provide housing accommodation outside of statutory homelessness duty (out-of-hours homelessness is handled by Aldershire's Out-of-Hours Housing Officer on the same number).
- Provide counselling, therapy, or substance misuse intervention. We can signpost to 24/7 services.

## Out-of-hours housing

The same 01632 960 999 number reaches the **Out-of-Hours Housing Officer**, who handles emergency homelessness applications under section 188 of the Housing Act 1996 (interim duty pending inquiries). The two roles are separate but co-located.

## Statutory framework

Local Authority Social Services Act 1970; Children Act 1989 (sections 20, 47, 46); Care Act 2014; Mental Health Act 1983 (sections 2, 3, 4, 136); Housing Act 1996 (Part 7).
