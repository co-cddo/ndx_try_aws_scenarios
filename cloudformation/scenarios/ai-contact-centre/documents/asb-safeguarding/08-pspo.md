# Public Spaces Protection Orders (PSPO)

A Public Spaces Protection Order is made by the council under Part 4, Chapter 2 of the Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014 (sections 59-75). It places restrictions on what people can do in a defined public space, addressing activities that are detrimental to the local community's quality of life.

## Aldershire's current PSPOs

The district has four active PSPOs (as of April 2026):

### Aldershire Town Centre alcohol PSPO

Covers the area bounded by High Street, Mill Road, Station Approach, and Park Lane. Restricts:

- Drinking alcohol or carrying open containers in a public place when asked to stop by an authorised officer.
- Failing to surrender alcohol on request.

Made 1 October 2022, in force until 30 September 2025, currently under review for renewal.

### District-wide dog control PSPO

Applies across the whole district. Restricts:

- Failing to remove dog faeces.
- Walking more than 4 dogs at once without a licensed exemption.
- Allowing dogs off lead in cemeteries, children's play areas, or fenced sports pitches.
- Failing to put a dog on a lead when directed by an authorised officer.

Made 1 April 2023, in force until 31 March 2026, renewal consultation runs to 30 June 2026.

### Riverside Park anti-vehicle PSPO

Restricts use of motor vehicles, off-road bikes, and quad bikes in Riverside Park and the adjoining nature reserve. Made 1 April 2024, in force until 31 March 2027.

### Aldershire Station car park night-time PSPO

Restricts gathering in groups of more than three between 11pm and 5am on the upper deck of the station car park. Made 1 September 2024, in force until 31 August 2027.

## Penalties

Breach of a PSPO is a criminal offence punishable by a **fixed penalty notice (FPN) of £100** (reduced to £75 if paid within 14 days) or, for non-payment or repeat breach, prosecution leading to a fine of up to **£1,000** on summary conviction.

## How a PSPO is made

The council must:

1. Be satisfied that activities have a detrimental effect on the quality of life of those in the locality, are persistent or continuing, and are likely to continue without intervention.
2. Consult the Chief Officer of Aldershire Constabulary, the Police and Crime Commissioner, the parish or town council (where relevant), and owners or occupiers of land within the area.
3. Publish the proposed order for community consultation (Aldershire's standard period is 6 weeks).
4. Consider responses and decide whether to make the order, with reasons.

## Reviewing and challenging a PSPO

PSPOs last up to 3 years and can be extended. Any individual who lives in, regularly works in, or visits the affected area can challenge a PSPO in the High Court within **6 weeks** of it being made or extended, on specific statutory grounds.
