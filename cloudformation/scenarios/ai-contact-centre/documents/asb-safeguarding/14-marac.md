# MARAC referrals: high-risk domestic abuse

A Multi-Agency Risk Assessment Conference (MARAC) is a meeting of statutory and voluntary sector agencies who share information about high-risk victims of domestic abuse to produce a coordinated safety plan. Aldershire's MARAC meets fortnightly and is chaired by the Detective Chief Inspector for Public Protection at Aldershire Constabulary.

## When a case is referred

A case is referred to MARAC where there is **high risk of serious harm or murder**, established through one or more of three routes:

1. **DASH-RIC scoring of 14 or more** on the Domestic Abuse, Stalking, Honour-based abuse, and Risk Identification Checklist (the standard tool used across England and Wales).
2. **Professional judgement** by a trained practitioner that the risk is high, even if the DASH-RIC score is below 14, with documented reasons.
3. **Repeat referrals** (3 or more incidents in the previous 12 months involving the same victim).

The threshold and process follow the SafeLives national guidance.

## Who attends MARAC

Standing members include:

- Aldershire Constabulary (chair, public protection unit, neighbourhood policing).
- ADAS (Aldershire Domestic Abuse Service) IDVAs (Independent Domestic Violence Advocates).
- Aldershire Council: Housing Options, Children's Services, Adult Social Care, Community Safety.
- Aldershire NHS Trust: A&E, mental health, midwifery, health visiting.
- HM Probation Service.
- Children & Family Court Advisory Service (Cafcass) for cases involving children.
- Aldershire Children's Safeguarding Partnership representative.
- Education representative (where children are of school age).

Other agencies attend by invitation depending on the case (substance misuse services, GP, refuge worker).

## How a referral is made

Any practitioner can refer. The referral form is on the **MARAC SharePoint** for partner agencies, or by secure email to **marac@aldershire-police.example.uk**. Self-referral by victims is not the route; victims work with an IDVA at ADAS who, where appropriate, makes the referral.

## What happens at MARAC

At the conference, partner agencies share what they know about the victim and (where relevant) the perpetrator. The conference produces an action plan with named lead agencies and timescales. Typical actions:

- Police: pursue criminal charges, increase patrols, install panic alarm.
- Housing: consider managed move, sanctuary works, emergency accommodation.
- Children's Services: child protection conference if needed.
- Health: alert flagging on patient records, discreet appointment scheduling.
- IDVA: continue safety planning with the victim, support court attendance.

## Information sharing

Information shared at MARAC is shared under the lawful basis of safeguarding life and limb (Article 6(1)(d) UK GDPR), public task (6(1)(e)), and the special category condition for safeguarding (Schedule 1 paragraph 18 of the Data Protection Act 2018). Each agency records the rationale for sharing on their own systems.

## Outcomes and review

Cases are reviewed at the next MARAC after the meeting and exit MARAC when risk is reduced and stable for at least 6 months. The MARAC coordinator (employed by ADAS) tracks outcomes and reports quarterly to the Aldershire Community Safety Partnership.

## Talking to the victim

The victim is told that their case is at MARAC unless to do so would increase risk. The IDVA is the victim's point of contact throughout. For information about MARAC support call ADAS on **01632 960 511** or the 24-hour helpline on **01632 960 999**.
