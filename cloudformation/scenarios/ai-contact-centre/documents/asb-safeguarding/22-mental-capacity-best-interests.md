# Mental Capacity Act 2005 and best interests decisions

The Mental Capacity Act 2005 (MCA) is the statutory framework for making decisions on behalf of adults who lack capacity to make a particular decision at a particular time. The council, NHS, and other bodies are bound by it, and so are family carers, attorneys, and friends supporting an adult who may lack capacity.

## The five statutory principles (section 1)

1. A person must be **assumed to have capacity** unless it is established that they lack capacity.
2. A person is **not to be treated as unable to make a decision** unless all practicable steps to help them have been taken without success.
3. A person is **not to be treated as unable to make a decision merely because they make an unwise decision**.
4. An act done, or decision made, on behalf of a person who lacks capacity must be done in their **best interests**.
5. The decision must be the **least restrictive** option that achieves the purpose.

## Capacity is decision- and time-specific

Capacity is assessed for a **specific decision** at a **specific moment**. A person may have capacity to choose what to wear but lack capacity to make a complex financial decision. A person with fluctuating capacity may be capable on a good day and incapable on a bad one; we time-shift decisions to good moments where possible.

## The two-stage test (section 2 and 3)

A person lacks capacity in relation to a matter if:

1. **Diagnostic test**: there is an impairment of, or disturbance in the functioning of, the mind or brain, **and**
2. **Functional test**: because of that, the person is unable to:
   - **Understand** the information relevant to the decision,
   - **Retain** that information (long enough to use it),
   - **Use or weigh** that information as part of the process of making the decision, **or**
   - **Communicate** the decision (by any means, including blinking).

Failing any one part of the functional test means the person lacks capacity for that decision at that time.

## Best interests (section 4)

The decision-maker must consider, so far as reasonably ascertainable:

- The person's **past and present wishes and feelings** (and any written statement made when they had capacity).
- The person's **beliefs and values**.
- Other factors the person would be likely to consider.
- The views of family, friends, carers, attorneys, deputies, and IMCAs.

The person must be involved as fully as possible. The decision must avoid discriminating on the grounds of age, appearance, condition, or behaviour. Where the decision concerns life-sustaining treatment, the decision-maker must not be motivated by a desire to bring about death.

## Documentation

Capacity assessments and best interests decisions must be **documented**. Aldershire uses the standard MCA1 capacity assessment and MCA2 best interests record, completed by the social worker, OT, or NHS clinician making the decision. A copy is kept on the case file and shared with the adult, family, and (where appropriate) attorneys.

## Independent Mental Capacity Advocate (IMCA)

An IMCA must be instructed where:

- A person lacks capacity to make a serious medical decision or change of accommodation, and
- They have no family or friends appropriate to consult.

Aldershire IMCA service is delivered by Aldershire Advocacy CIC. Phone **01632 960 470**.

## Lasting Powers of Attorney and Court of Protection deputies

A person with capacity can appoint a Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA) for property and finance, or for health and welfare. Once registered with the Office of the Public Guardian, the attorney can act for the person within the scope of the LPA. Where there is no LPA and the person lacks capacity, applications can be made to the Court of Protection for a deputyship.

## Council role

Council social workers, occupational therapists, and senior managers are trained to MCA standard. The Aldershire MCA Lead (**01632 960 855**) provides advice on complex cases. Compliance is monitored by the Aldershire Safeguarding Adults Board.

## Legal framework

Mental Capacity Act 2005; MCA Code of Practice (revised 2023); Aldershire MCA Multi-Agency Procedure (2024).
