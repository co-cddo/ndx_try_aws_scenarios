# Multi-Agency Safeguarding Hub (MASH)

The Aldershire Multi-Agency Safeguarding Hub (MASH) is the single point of referral for safeguarding concerns about children and adults at risk. It is a co-located, multi-agency team that ensures concerns are triaged consistently, with information from all relevant agencies pulled together at the front door of the safeguarding system.

## What MASH does

MASH receives every safeguarding referral in the district and:

1. Logs and acknowledges the referral.
2. Conducts a **research stage** drawing on each member agency's records.
3. Holds a **multi-agency triage meeting** where threshold or complexity warrants.
4. Decides on the **threshold** for response: information & advice, signposting, early help, statutory assessment, or section 47 (children) / section 42 (adults) intervention.
5. Routes the case to the right team for ongoing work.

The triage decision is recorded with rationale and shared with the referrer in writing, normally within 5 working days. For high-risk children's cases the decision is made within 24 hours.

## Member agencies

The Aldershire MASH co-locates representatives from:

- **Aldershire Council**: Children's Services duty social workers, Adult Social Care duty social workers, Education Welfare, Housing safeguarding lead.
- **Aldershire Constabulary**: Public Protection Unit officers, MASH analyst.
- **Aldershire NHS Integrated Care Board**: safeguarding nurses (children and adults), GP liaison.
- **Aldershire NHS Trust**: hospital-based safeguarding leads (on call).
- **National Probation Service**: domestic abuse and offender liaison.
- **Aldershire Domestic Abuse Service**: IDVA.
- **Aldershire Children's Charity**: early help triage.

The hub operates from a secure office at the council's Civic Centre, with information-sharing protocols approved by all agencies' Information Governance leads.

## What information to provide

Whether you are a member of the public or a professional, please give us:

- The person's identifying details (name, date of birth, address, GP).
- Family composition and other adults / children in the household.
- The nature of the concern, with examples and dates.
- What the person knows about the referral and whether they have consented (we will refer without consent if the threshold for doing so is met).
- Who else is involved (school, GP, social worker, probation officer).
- Your contact details and your relationship to the person.

## What happens after the triage

The MASH outcome is one of:

- **No further action**, with reasons.
- **Information, advice, and signposting**, where there are no safeguarding needs but help would be useful.
- **Early help**: a non-statutory family or individual support plan, often led by Aldershire Children's Charity for children or by community navigators for adults.
- **Statutory assessment**: a single assessment under section 17 of the Children Act 1989 (children in need), or a Care Act assessment (adults).
- **Section 47** (children) or **section 42** (adults) **safeguarding enquiry**: where the threshold for protective action is met. A strategy meeting is convened the same day for serious children's cases.

## Information sharing

MASH operates under a multi-agency information-sharing protocol approved by the Aldershire Children's Safeguarding Partnership and the Aldershire Safeguarding Adults Board. The lawful bases include public task (UK GDPR Art 6(1)(e)), vital interests (Art 6(1)(d)), and the safeguarding condition (Schedule 1 paragraph 18 DPA 2018). Each agency records its own audit trail.

## Performance and oversight

The MASH publishes performance data quarterly: referrals received, threshold decisions, average triage time. In Q4 2025/26: 4,321 children's referrals (61% met early help threshold or below), 2,108 adults' referrals (47% met section 42 or signposting threshold).

## Out of hours

The Children's and Adults' MASH operate 24/7 with rotating duty cover. Out of hours the **Emergency Duty Team** (covered separately) handles new urgent referrals.

## Statutory framework

Children Act 1989 and 2004; Care Act 2014; Working Together to Safeguard Children 2023; Care and Support Statutory Guidance 2023.
