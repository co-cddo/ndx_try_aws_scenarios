# Neighbour disputes and mediation

Many of the disagreements that come to the council as anti-social behaviour are, on closer look, neighbour disputes between two parties who could resolve the issue with support. We offer mediation as a first step in many of these cases. It is voluntary, confidential, and free.

## When mediation works

Mediation works well for:

- Disputes about boundary fences, hedges, and shared driveways.
- Recurring noise where neither party is acting maliciously and a small change in routine would resolve the issue.
- Children's play, ball games, and similar conduct where a parent and a neighbour have a different threshold for what is reasonable.
- Pets (dogs, cats, chickens) and the conduct of pet owners.
- Parking disputes where the parties live close to each other.
- Garden bonfire smoke, BBQ fumes, washing on the line.

Mediation does not work for cases involving threats, violence, intimidation, hate crime, controlling behaviour, or where one party has a substantial power imbalance over the other. In those cases we go straight to ASB or safeguarding routes.

## The mediation service

Aldershire Mediation Service is delivered by **Aldershire Mediation CIC**, a community interest company commissioned by the council. Mediators are trained to Civil Mediation Council standards and are independent of both the council and the parties.

The service:

1. Receives a referral (from a council officer, a housing provider, the police, or a self-referral).
2. Speaks to each party separately to understand the issues and check that mediation is suitable and consented to.
3. Arranges a joint session, usually face-to-face at a neutral venue (the Aldershire Community Hub) or by video.
4. Helps the parties draft a written agreement that captures what they have agreed.
5. Offers a follow-up at 6 weeks to check whether the agreement is working.

Most cases are resolved in 1-2 joint sessions. The agreement is not legally binding but most parties keep to it because they negotiated it.

## How to access mediation

- Self-referral: phone **01632 960 412** or email **info@aldershire-mediation.example.org**.
- Online form at **aldershire-mediation.example.org/refer**.
- Through your council case officer if you have already opened an ASB case.
- Through your housing provider's tenancy team.

The service is free to residents of the district. There is no waiting list as of April 2026; the service typically starts work within 5 working days.

## What if mediation does not work or one party refuses

If mediation does not produce an agreement, or if one party will not engage, we go back to the standard ASB or environmental health processes. Refusal to mediate is not held against either party in any subsequent enforcement, but a court considering an injunction can take into account whether reasonable alternatives were attempted.

## Confidentiality

Mediation is confidential. Nothing said in a mediation session can be used as evidence in subsequent proceedings, in line with the Civil Mediation Council's code of conduct. The mediator does not share notes with the council unless a safeguarding concern emerges.
