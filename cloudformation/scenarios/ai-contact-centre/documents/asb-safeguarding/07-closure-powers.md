# Closure powers for premises associated with ASB

Closure powers under Part 4, Chapter 3 of the Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014 allow the council, Aldershire Constabulary, or a partner agency to close premises that are being used (or are likely to be used) in a way that has resulted, or is likely to result, in nuisance to members of the public, or in disorder near the premises.

## The two-stage process

### Closure notice (sections 76-78)

A senior council officer (usually the Head of Community Safety) or an inspector or above in Aldershire Constabulary can issue a closure notice. The notice:

- Prohibits access to the premises by anyone except a habitual resident or owner.
- Lasts up to **48 hours**.
- Must be issued on reasonable grounds and only after consultation with people likely to be affected and with reasonable steps to identify those people.
- Triggers an application to the magistrates' court for a closure order before expiry.

### Closure order (sections 80-83)

The magistrates' court hears the application and can grant a closure order if satisfied that:

- A person has engaged or is likely to engage in disorderly, offensive or criminal behaviour on the premises, or use of the premises has resulted or is likely to result in serious nuisance, or there has been or is likely to be disorder near the premises associated with the use of the premises, **and**
- The order is necessary to prevent the conduct or nuisance.

A closure order can prohibit access by anyone, including residents, for up to **3 months**. The court can extend it to a maximum of **6 months**.

## When Aldershire uses closure

Typical scenarios in our district include:

- A flat being used as a base for drug supply, with constant visitor traffic at all hours.
- A property where serious noise and disorder has continued despite warnings, CPNs, and injunctions against named occupants.
- A licensed premises where disorder spills into the surrounding streets.
- A vacant property being used by groups for ASB or drug taking.

We use closure as a measure of last resort. Before applying we consider the impact on innocent occupants (including children), available alternatives (rehousing through Aldershire Housing Options, civil injunction against named perpetrators), and the proportionality of the closure period.

## Breach

Entering or remaining in premises subject to a closure order without reasonable excuse is a **criminal offence**. Penalties include up to **51 weeks' imprisonment** for residents' breach (3 months for non-residents) and unlimited fines.

## Affected occupants

We work with the housing provider and Aldershire Housing Options to find alternative accommodation for residents and their dependants who are not the cause of the disorder. Phone **01632 960 220** for urgent housing assistance during a closure.

## Reporting concerns

To report a property where you believe closure powers may be appropriate, phone **01632 960 142** or use the form at **aldershire.example.gov.uk/asb/closure-referral**.
