# Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DOLS)

The Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DOLS) are an additional safeguard within the Mental Capacity Act 2005 framework. They protect the rights of people aged 18 or over who lack capacity to consent to their care arrangements where those arrangements amount to a deprivation of liberty. DOLS apply in care homes and hospitals.

## When DOLS apply

A deprivation of liberty arises where the person is:

- **Under continuous supervision and control**, **and**
- **Not free to leave**, **and**
- **Lacks the mental capacity to consent** to those arrangements.

This is the so-called "acid test" from the 2014 Supreme Court ruling in *Cheshire West* (P v Cheshire West and Chester Council [2014] UKSC 19).

The criteria can be met even if the person does not object, even if the placement is in the person's best interests, and even if the placement is benign.

## Standard authorisations

When a care home or hospital ("the managing authority") believes a person in its care is being deprived of their liberty, it must apply to the council ("the supervisory body") for a **standard authorisation**.

The supervisory body must arrange six assessments:

1. **Age assessment**: is the person 18 or over.
2. **Mental health assessment**: do they have a mental disorder.
3. **Mental capacity assessment**: do they lack capacity to consent to the care arrangements.
4. **Best interests assessment**: is the deprivation in their best interests, necessary, and proportionate.
5. **Eligibility assessment**: do they meet the eligibility criteria (not detained under the Mental Health Act 1983 in conflict).
6. **No refusals assessment**: is there a valid advance decision or LPA refusing the proposed care.

If all six are positive, the council grants the authorisation. The person and their representative can challenge it in the Court of Protection.

## Urgent authorisations

A managing authority can self-authorise an urgent deprivation for up to **7 days** (extendable by 7) while the standard authorisation is being arranged. Aldershire requires notification of any urgent authorisation by the next working day.

## How to refer

- Care home or hospital staff: use the standard DOLS form available at **aldershire.example.gov.uk/adult-safeguarding/dols**.
- Family or friends concerned that a relative is being deprived of liberty without authorisation: contact the **Aldershire DOLS Team** on **01632 960 858**.
- Concerns about a deprivation of liberty in any other setting (own home, supported living): see the Liberty Protection Safeguards section below or contact the Adult Safeguarding Team.

## Relevant Person's Representative (RPR)

Every person subject to a DOLS authorisation has a **Relevant Person's Representative (RPR)**. The RPR is usually a family member or friend. Where there is no suitable person, the council appoints a paid RPR through Aldershire Advocacy CIC. The RPR has the right to challenge the authorisation in the Court of Protection (without legal aid means-test).

## Liberty Protection Safeguards (LPS)

The Mental Capacity (Amendment) Act 2019 introduced the Liberty Protection Safeguards as a planned replacement for DOLS, designed to extend protection to people deprived of liberty in their own homes, supported living, and other settings, and to streamline the process. Implementation has been repeatedly delayed; as of April 2026, DOLS remain in force. Aldershire continues to operate the DOLS framework and will give 6 months' notice of any move to LPS.

## Statistics and oversight

The council publishes DOLS data quarterly: applications received, authorisations granted, cases backlogged. As of Q4 2025/26: 478 active authorisations, 6.2-week median time from application to assessment completion. The Care Quality Commission inspects local DOLS performance.

## Statutory framework

Mental Capacity Act 2005, Schedule A1; Mental Capacity Act Code of Practice (2008, supplementary 2023); Cheshire West [2014] UKSC 19; Aldershire DOLS Multi-Agency Protocol (2024).
