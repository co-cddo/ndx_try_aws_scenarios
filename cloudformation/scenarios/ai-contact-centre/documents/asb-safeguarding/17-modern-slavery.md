# Modern slavery and the National Referral Mechanism

Modern slavery is the recruitment, harbouring, transportation, or receipt of people through threat, force, coercion, abduction, fraud, or deception, for the purpose of exploitation. It is criminalised in England and Wales by the Modern Slavery Act 2015 (sections 1 and 2 cover slavery, servitude, forced labour, and human trafficking).

Forms include forced labour (e.g. car washes, nail bars, agriculture), domestic servitude, sexual exploitation, criminal exploitation (including county lines), and organ harvesting. Victims include UK and non-UK nationals, adults and children.

## Spotting the signs

Front-line council staff (housing, council tax, environmental health, licensing) are trained to recognise indicators:

- Multiple unrelated people living at one address with no obvious family link.
- A worker who appears anxious, controlled by a third party, or unable to speak for themselves.
- A "client" whose ID, bank account, or correspondence is held by someone else.
- Living conditions that suggest the property is being used as a brothel or cannabis cultivation site (extreme heat, blacked-out windows, persistent strong smells).
- A person with no apparent freedom of movement or money.

## How to report

- **Modern Slavery & Exploitation Helpline**: **08000 121 700** (24/7, confidential).
- **999** if a person is in immediate danger.
- **101** for non-emergency reports to Aldershire Constabulary.
- **Aldershire MASH**: **01632 960 800** (children) or **01632 960 850** (adults at risk).
- Council Community Safety team (for licensing-linked or property-linked concerns): **01632 960 142**.

## The National Referral Mechanism (NRM)

The NRM is the UK's framework for identifying victims of modern slavery and ensuring they receive appropriate support. Two stages:

### Reasonable Grounds decision

A First Responder (police, local authority, NHS, GLAA, NCA, designated NGOs) refers the suspected victim to the **Single Competent Authority (SCA)** at the Home Office. The SCA aims to make a Reasonable Grounds decision within **5 working days**. A positive decision triggers immediate access to government-funded support.

### Conclusive Grounds decision

A more detailed assessment leading to a Conclusive Grounds decision. There is no statutory deadline; cases typically take 12-18 months.

## First Responders in Aldershire

Aldershire Council is a designated First Responder organisation. NRM referrals are made by:

- The Modern Slavery Lead (Adult Social Care) for adult victims with consent.
- Children's Services for under-18 victims (no consent required; consent does not apply to children in NRM).

NRM referrals are made on the Home Office referral portal. Training is delivered annually to all First Responder staff.

## Support during NRM

Adults with a positive Reasonable Grounds decision receive support from the **Modern Slavery Victim Care Contract** (currently delivered by The Salvation Army). This includes safe accommodation, subsistence, advocacy, and access to specialist legal advice. Children receive support through the local authority and the **Independent Child Trafficking Guardian** service operating in our region.

## Council role beyond First Responder

- Licensing Team: targeted inspections of high-risk premises (car washes, hand car washes, nail bars).
- Trading Standards: rogue trader and labour exploitation referrals to the **Gangmasters and Labour Abuse Authority (GLAA)** on **0800 432 0804**.
- Housing: identification of HMOs being used for exploitation.
- Council Tax: data triggers (multiple single-occupancy claims at one address) flagged to safeguarding.

## Aldershire Modern Slavery Statement

The council publishes an annual Modern Slavery Statement in line with section 54 of the Modern Slavery Act 2015. The statement covers our supply chains and our operational response. It is available at **aldershire.example.gov.uk/about/modern-slavery-statement**.
