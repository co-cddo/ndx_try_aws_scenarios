# Domestic Homicide Reviews

A Domestic Homicide Review (DHR), now also referred to as a Domestic Abuse Related Death Review, is a multi-agency review conducted following the death of a person aged 16 or over which has, or appears to have, resulted from violence, abuse, or neglect by:

- A person to whom they were related or with whom they were or had been in an intimate personal relationship, or
- A member of the same household as themselves,

with a view to identifying lessons to be learned. DHRs are required by section 9 of the Domestic Violence, Crime and Victims Act 2004.

## When a DHR is required

A DHR must be commissioned where the criteria above are met. Suspected suicides where there is evidence of domestic abuse are also subject to DHR following Home Office statutory guidance refreshed in 2023, which expanded the scope.

The review is **not** an investigation into the death itself (that is for the police, coroner, and CPS). It is a learning review focused on the agencies' contact with the victim and perpetrator before the death, to identify what could be improved.

## Process in Aldershire

DHRs are commissioned by the **Aldershire Community Safety Partnership** (CSP). The local DHR procedure follows Home Office statutory guidance (March 2023):

1. **Notification**: police notify the CSP of any death meeting the criteria within 5 working days of the death being identified.
2. **Decision to conduct a review**: the CSP DHR Subgroup decides within 28 days, applying the statutory criteria.
3. **Independent chair appointed**: a chair from outside the partnership area, with relevant expertise.
4. **Family engagement**: the chair contacts surviving family members, asks how they want to be involved, and offers an advocate (commissioned through AAFDA, Advocacy After Fatal Domestic Abuse).
5. **Agencies submit Individual Management Reviews (IMRs)** describing their contact with the victim and perpetrator.
6. **Panel meetings**: the panel (chaired independently, with statutory member agencies and family advocate as appropriate) reviews the IMRs and produces a synthesis.
7. **Overview Report**: the chair produces an overview report with findings and recommendations.
8. **Quality assurance** by the Home Office Quality Assurance Panel.
9. **Publication**: the executive summary is published; the full report is shared with statutory agencies, with the family's views taken into account on what is published.
10. **Action plan**: the CSP tracks implementation; the council reports annually to Cabinet on progress.

## Family involvement

Aldershire is committed to meaningful family involvement, in line with the AAFDA standards. Practical commitments:

- The independent chair makes initial contact within 4 weeks of appointment.
- The family is offered an AAFDA advocate (free, commissioned by the council).
- The family is invited to contribute, in whatever form suits them, including through a written statement, attending the final panel, or not engaging at all.
- The family sees and comments on the draft executive summary before publication.
- The family is given the published report at the same time as agencies, never later.

## Confidentiality and anonymisation

Published DHRs are anonymised. The victim is given a pseudonym, agreed with the family. Identifying details (employer, specific addresses, distinctive circumstances) are minimised. Agencies are named.

## Recent learning themes

DHRs published nationally and locally have produced consistent themes:

- Information sharing across agencies, especially between health and police.
- Recognition of coercive control patterns, beyond physical violence.
- Engagement with perpetrators, including through perpetrator programmes.
- Suicidal ideation in domestic abuse victims; the link between abuse and self-harm.
- Stalking and stalking risk assessment.

The Aldershire CSP publishes an annual learning summary at **aldershire.example.gov.uk/community-safety/dhr-learning**.

## Support for families

- **AAFDA**: Advocacy After Fatal Domestic Abuse. National charity providing advocacy and peer support to families bereaved by fatal domestic abuse. **0808 100 1850** (free).
- **Victim Support**: standard victim support service. **0808 168 9111**.
- **The Compassionate Friends**: bereavement support for parents who have lost a child of any age. **0345 123 2304**.

## Statutory framework

Domestic Violence, Crime and Victims Act 2004 (section 9); Multi-Agency Statutory Guidance for the Conduct of Domestic Homicide Reviews (Home Office, March 2023); Aldershire CSP DHR Procedure (2024).
