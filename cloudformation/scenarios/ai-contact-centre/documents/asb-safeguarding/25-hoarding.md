# Hoarding: graded approach and multi-agency response

Hoarding is the persistent difficulty discarding or parting with possessions, regardless of their actual value. It is recognised as a mental health condition (Hoarding Disorder, ICD-11 6B24) and often co-occurs with depression, OCD, ADHD, autism, and trauma history. It is also a recognised safeguarding concern when it presents risk of serious harm.

Hoarding is not the same as a cluttered home. The Clutter Image Rating (CIR) scale is the assessment tool used across Aldershire, with grades 1-9 from "no clutter" to "very severe".

## The Aldershire graded approach

Our response is calibrated to the CIR grade. The grading comes from a structured assessment by a trained worker (housing officer, environmental health officer, social worker, or fire safety officer) with the resident's consent.

### CIR 1-3 (low risk)

The home is cluttered but functional. Pathways are clear. No safeguarding concern. Offer signposting to declutter resources, peer support groups (e.g. **Aldershire Hoarders' Network** on **01632 960 540**), and Aldershire Mind's wellbeing service.

### CIR 4-6 (moderate risk)

Increasing clutter restricting use of rooms; cooking, washing, or sleeping in the planned space may be impaired. We offer:

- Engagement visits over weeks/months, at the resident's pace.
- A **Hoarding Action Plan** agreed with the resident.
- Specialist counselling (cognitive-behavioural therapy adapted for hoarding, available through Aldershire Talking Therapies).
- Practical support: a single skip, a few hours of decluttering with a trained volunteer.
- Fire risk assessment and a free Home Fire Safety Visit by Aldershire Fire and Rescue (**0800 169 1125**).

### CIR 7-9 (severe / very severe)

Significant risk to life: blocked exits, accumulated waste with public health implications, structural concerns, vermin. Response:

- **Multi-agency strategy meeting** within 10 working days.
- Joint Adult Safeguarding section 42 enquiry where threshold is met.
- Fire & Rescue **prevention notice** if appropriate.
- Environmental health consideration of statutory powers under the Public Health Act 1936 (filthy and verminous premises, section 83) or Housing Act 2004 (Category 1 hazards under HHSRS).
- Where the resident lacks capacity (MCA assessment by social worker), best interests decisions about a deep clean or temporary move.

## Engagement principles

The Aldershire approach across all grades is:

1. **Person-led**: the resident sets the pace and the goals, except where capacity is lacking and risk is severe.
2. **Multi-agency**: hoarding is rarely a single-agency problem. Housing, social care, mental health, fire, and environmental health work together under the multi-agency procedure.
3. **Sustained**: most cases need 12+ months of contact. Quick clearances without engagement frequently lead to reaccumulation.
4. **Compassionate**: people who hoard have often experienced trauma, loss, or chronic mental ill health. Shame is part of the condition; lecturing is counterproductive.

## What family members can do

Many calls come from worried family. Practical advice:

- Avoid threats, ultimatums, or cleaning the home behind the person's back. Both have been shown to entrench the hoarding and damage relationships.
- Encourage the person to contact their GP or Aldershire Talking Therapies (self-referral on **01632 960 600**).
- Use peer support: Hoarding UK has a national peer support line (**020 3239 1600**) and the Aldershire Hoarders' Network meets fortnightly.
- Where you believe there is serious risk of harm to the person or others, you can contact **Aldershire MASH** on **01632 960 850**.

## Pets and animal hoarding

Cases involving large numbers of animals trigger an additional pathway with the **RSPCA** (national line **0300 1234 999**) and Aldershire's animal welfare officer (**01632 960 175**). Animal hoarding is associated with both animal welfare concerns and human safeguarding issues.

## Statutory and policy framework

Mental Capacity Act 2005; Care Act 2014; Public Health Act 1936; Housing Act 2004; Aldershire Multi-Agency Hoarding Procedure (2024).
