# Serious case reviews: Children's and Adults'

Where a child or adult at risk dies or is seriously harmed and abuse or neglect is known or suspected to be a factor, statutory reviews are conducted to identify what can be learned about how agencies worked together to safeguard them. The two regimes (child and adult) are similar in purpose but distinct in legal framework.

## Children: Local Child Safeguarding Practice Reviews (LCSPR)

The Children and Social Work Act 2017 (section 16) replaced Serious Case Reviews with **Local Child Safeguarding Practice Reviews (LCSPR)**. These are the responsibility of the Aldershire Children's Safeguarding Partnership (the council, the Integrated Care Board, and Aldershire Constabulary) under Working Together to Safeguard Children 2023.

A Rapid Review is conducted within **15 working days** of any serious incident involving a child. The Partnership decides, on the basis of the Rapid Review, whether to commission a full LCSPR. Decisions on commissioning are sent to the **Child Safeguarding Practice Review Panel** (a national body) for oversight.

A full LCSPR:

- Is led by an independent reviewer.
- Is family-centred, with engagement of the surviving family or (in cases not involving death) the child themselves wherever possible.
- Examines the case using systems thinking, identifying systemic issues rather than focusing on individual blame.
- Produces a published report and an action plan tracked by the Partnership.

Aldershire publishes Rapid Review summaries and full LCSPR reports at **aldershire-safeguarding-children.example.org/lcspr**.

## Adults: Safeguarding Adult Reviews (SAR)

The Care Act 2014 (section 44) requires **Safeguarding Adult Reviews (SARs)** where an adult with care and support needs has died or experienced serious abuse or neglect, and there is concern about how agencies worked together. SARs are commissioned by the **Aldershire Safeguarding Adults Board (SAB)**.

The SAB must arrange a SAR if:

- The adult has died, and the SAB knows or suspects the death resulted from abuse or neglect (whether or not it knew about, or suspected, the abuse or neglect before the death), **or**
- The adult is alive but the SAB knows or suspects the adult has experienced serious abuse or neglect.

The SAB **may** commission a discretionary SAR in other cases where the SAB believes there is value in doing so.

Process in Aldershire:

1. Notification by any agency to the SAB Business Manager within 5 working days of identification.
2. SAR Subgroup decision on commissioning.
3. Independent author/lead reviewer commissioned.
4. Methodology agreed (often a "Significant Incident Learning Process" or "Learning Together" methodology rather than a traditional process review).
5. Family / adult engagement, with advocacy.
6. Agency reports and panel discussions.
7. Final report and action plan.
8. Publication: anonymised report on the SAB website, full version shared with agencies.

## Learning into practice

Both LCSPRs and SARs produce learning. Aldershire's approach to converting learning into practice:

- An action plan with named lead, deadline, and measurable outcome.
- Quarterly tracking by the relevant Board/Partnership Subgroup.
- Annual report to the Health & Wellbeing Board and Cabinet.
- Multi-agency learning events: at least two per year, drawing in front-line practitioners across the partnership.
- Inclusion of recurring themes in the multi-agency training programme.

## National and regional learning

The council also tracks national LCSPR Panel themed reviews, the SAR national network, and national thematic reviews. Findings are integrated into local procedures where applicable.

## Family and survivor engagement

Both review types prioritise meaningful engagement with families and (where the adult survives and has capacity) the adult themselves. Aldershire commissions a third-party advocate for families through Aldershire Advocacy CIC. Families see draft reports before publication and are consulted on anonymisation.

## Statutory framework

Children Act 2004 (section 16, as amended by Children and Social Work Act 2017); Care Act 2014 (section 44); Working Together to Safeguard Children 2023; Care and Support Statutory Guidance 2023.
